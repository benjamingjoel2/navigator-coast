Marvão does not sit on its hill so much as grow out of it. The castle walls rise straight from bare granite, the village streets follow the line of the rock, and the whole place hangs on a ridge with a drop on every side. From the battlements the view seems to go on for ever: the Serra de São Mamede in waves of oak and chestnut, the plains of the Alentejo fading south, and Spain spread out to the east.

A short drive away, Castelo de Vide is gentler: a town of fountains, flowered lanes and churches on a green hillside, with one of the best-preserved Jewish quarters in Portugal. Together they make the high Alentejo, a cooler, greener, more mountainous corner of a region better known for its plains.

## Marvão: the eagle's nest

The municipality's [guide to what to visit](https://www.cm-marvao.pt/turismo/descobrir/visitar/) puts the castle at more than 850 metres, on the hard rock of the serra, and the setting is the whole point. Walk the ramparts slowly, look down on the village roofs, and find the castle cistern: the same page calls it one of the largest recorded in Portugal, and believes it could have supplied the village with water for more than six months under siege.

<Pair a="mvc-marvao-keep" b="mvc-marvao-walls" captionA="The keep of Marvão castle above granite outcrops and a stone path." captionB="Marvão’s castle walls built straight onto the bare rock." />

The village's name has a story too. A sculpture by João Cutileiro, the municipality notes, honours Ibn Maruan, who took refuge here in the 9th century and gave Marvão its name. Elsewhere, the Praça do Pelourinho, the Câmara Velha (the old town hall, used from the reign of King Manuel until 1956) and a string of small churches fill the short streets. The oldest church, Santa Maria, now houses the [Museu Municipal](https://www.cm-marvao.pt/turismo/), built from objects lent by local people.

Marvão is small enough to see in a morning, but it rewards an overnight stay. When the day visitors leave, the streets are almost silent, and sunset from the walls is unforgettable.

## Staying in Marvão

The [Pousada Marvão](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-marvao) sits within the village walls. Pousadas de Portugal describes it as four small traditional houses, one of the first pousadas, built in the 1940s, at more than 860 metres, with views from its rooms and panoramic restaurant over Spain and the Alentejo plains. The municipality also keeps a [list of accommodation](https://www.cm-marvao.pt/turismo/descobrir/dormir/) in and around the village.

For more on pousadas and other historic places to stay across the country, see our [first-timer's guide to Portugal](/stories/first-timers-guide-to-portugal/).

## Ammaia: a Roman city in the valley

Below Marvão, near São Salvador da Aramenha, lie the ruins of a Roman town. The municipality's [heritage page](https://www.cm-marvao.pt/concelho/patrimonio/) explains that the site was long known to hold the ruins of a Roman city, but its name was confused for centuries until the archaeologist José Leite de Vasconcelos identified it in 1935.

<Figure id="mvc-ammaia-inscription" caption="A Roman inscribed stone bearing the name of Ammaia, the Roman city in the valley below Marvão." />

The [Fundação Cidade de Ammaia](http://www.ammaia.pt/), which manages the site and its museum, reports the identification of an amphitheatre about 60 metres long, which it describes as the fifth Roman amphitheatre known in the whole province of Lusitania. The restoration project won the Gulbenkian Foundation's Vasco Vilalva prize for heritage in 2009. Start in the museum to make sense of what you see in the field.

## The Serra de São Mamede

Both towns lie inside the Parque Natural da Serra de São Mamede, created in 1989 by [Decreto-Lei n.º 121/89](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/decreto-lei/121-1989-615244). This is a different Alentejo: granite outcrops, chestnut groves, streams and wooded slopes, right up to the border with Spain.

<Figure id="mvc-sao-mamede-landscape" size="full" caption="The view from Marvão’s walls over the valley and the wooded hills of the Serra de São Mamede." />

The municipality of Marvão lists [walking, cycling and riding activities](https://www.cm-marvao.pt/turismo/descobrir/) in the natural park on its tourism pages, and its [local products](https://www.cm-marvao.pt/turismo/) include chestnuts, olive oil, wine, cheese, sausages and honey. Autumn, when the chestnuts fall, is a lovely time to be here. Wear proper shoes; the paths are rocky and the gradients real.

## Castelo de Vide: fountains and the Jewish quarter

Castelo de Vide spills down the hillside below its castle, all whitewashed houses, steep lanes and pots of flowers on the steps. It has a gentler feel than Marvão and more of a working town about it, with cafés, shops and a sociable main square.

Its great treasure is the Judiaria. Visit Alentejo's [article on the town's Jewish heritage](https://www.visitalentejo.pt/en/blog/exploring-the-jewish-history-and-heritage-of-castelo-de-vide/) places the quarter on the northern slope between the castle and the town fountain, dates it to the reign of King Dinis in the 13th century, and calls it one of the best-preserved Jewish quarters in the country. Look closely at the doorframes and you will see the marks where mezuzahs once hung.

<Pair a="mvc-castelo-de-vide-synagogue" b="mvc-castelo-de-vide-fountain" captionA="The medieval synagogue in Castelo de Vide, with its pointed stone doorways." captionB="A canopied stone fountain in the streets of Castelo de Vide." />

The medieval synagogue, at the corner of Rua da Judiaria and Rua da Fonte, has been renovated as a museum of Jewish life, the same article explains. Nearby, the Casa da Inquisição uses augmented reality to tell the story of a local woman arrested by the Lisbon Inquisition in 1662. Visit Alentejo counts 24 churches in the town, among them the Capela do Salvador do Mundo, dating to the late 13th century.

## Two days in the high Alentejo

The two towns are close enough to combine in a single day, but they are better savoured over two. Sleep in Marvão, so you have the walls to yourself at dusk and again in the early morning, when the valleys below are still in shadow. Spend that first morning on the ramparts and in the lanes, then drive down to Ammaia in the afternoon and start in the museum.

On the second day, cross to Castelo de Vide. Wander up through the Judiaria, visit the synagogue museum, and let yourself get lost in the lanes between the castle and the fountains. Have a long lunch, then take the back roads through the serra on your way out, stopping wherever a viewpoint or a village tempts you.

If you have a third day, spend it walking. The natural park rewards slow exploration on foot far more than any view from a car window.

## When to go

The serra is noticeably cooler and greener than the plains. IPMA's [1991–2020 normals for Portalegre](https://www.ipma.pt/opencms/bin/file.data/climate-normal/cn_91-20_PORTALEGRE.pdf), the nearest major station, show average highs of about 31°C in July and August, against about 12°C in January, so summer is warm but more bearable than Évora, and winter can be properly chilly, especially up on Marvão's rock.

Spring brings green hills and flowers, and autumn brings chestnuts and soft light. Our [guide to the best time to visit Portugal](/stories/best-time-to-visit-portugal/) compares the regions month by month.

## Getting there

This is car country. The two towns are close to each other but far from the main railway lines, and the joy of the area lies in the back roads between villages. Our guide to [getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) covers hiring a car and driving in the interior.

The high Alentejo pairs naturally with the rest of the region. From here, head south to [Évora](/stories/evora-guide/), then on to the lake and dark skies of [Monsaraz and Alqueva](/stories/monsaraz-and-alqueva/) for a week in the Alentejo interior.

## Before you book

- Hire a car; you will need one to reach Ammaia, the walking routes and the villages around the serra.
- Book the [Pousada Marvão](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-marvao) or other rooms inside the walls well ahead for weekends and holidays.
- Pack a warm layer at any time of year: Marvão sits above 850 metres, and [IPMA's figures](https://www.ipma.pt/opencms/bin/file.data/climate-normal/cn_91-20_PORTALEGRE.pdf) show cool winters.
- Check opening hours for the Ammaia museum on the [foundation's site](http://www.ammaia.pt/) and for Castelo de Vide's synagogue museum locally.
- Bring sturdy shoes for the castle ramparts and the serra paths.
