Saturday morning, a little after nine, and Campo de Santa Clara is already spread with other people's lives. Blankets on the cobbles hold brass door knockers, azulejos prised from who knows where, old postcards, vinyl, a wedding dress, a box of keys. Above it all the white dome of the National Pantheon floats against the sky, and somewhere below, the Tagus glitters between the rooftops.

Lisbon shops at markets, and has done for centuries. Some are about food, some about junk and treasure, and the best mix both. They are also the most enjoyable way to see the city's neighbourhoods at work. Here is where to go, and when, because almost every one of them keeps its own particular days.

## Time Out Market and the Mercado da Ribeira

Start at the river. The Mercado da Ribeira, the big market hall on Avenida 24 de Julho at Cais do Sodré, is home to **[Time Out Market Lisboa](https://www.timeout.com/time-out-market-lisboa)**, which opened in 2014 and calls itself the original Time Out Market. Its own pages list 26 restaurants and 8 bars from Lisbon chefs and restaurateurs, arranged around long communal tables where anyone can sit.

<Figure id="lxm-time-out-hall" caption="Long wooden tables and food counters under the roof of the Mercado da Ribeira." />

How it works is simple: walk the counters, order from as many as you like, collect your plates and find a seat. The market says it takes credit and debit cards as well as cash, and has cash machines on site. Opening hours are long and run late at the weekend; check the current times on its site before you go.

It is busy, and at peak mealtimes very busy, so we aim for late morning or mid-afternoon. It is also the easiest place in the city to taste a wide spread of Portuguese food in one sitting, which makes it a good first stop if you have just arrived.

The same building has a second life on Saturdays. **[Sábados da Ribeira](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/places/sabados-da-ribeira-saturdays-at-the-ribeira)**, Visit Lisboa says, is a handicrafts, arts, books and antiques market held in the hall every Saturday from 10am to 7pm, with artisans showing new work alongside the stalls.

## Feira da Ladra, the flea market

Lisbon's best-known flea market fills Campo de Santa Clara, behind the church and monastery of São Vicente de Fora, on the edge of Alfama. According to [Visit Lisboa](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/places/feira-da-ladra-flea-market), the **Feira da Ladra** dates back to 1272 and moved around several parts of the city before settling here. It runs on **Tuesdays and Saturdays, from 9am to 6pm**.

<Figure id="lxm-flea-stalls" caption="Clothes, crockery and bric-a-brac spread out on the ground at a Lisbon flea market, with the river beyond." />

Expect vintage, kitsch and plenty of junk, with the occasional find: tiles, prints, old cameras and cutlery, records and books. Prices are rarely marked; a friendly offer is part of the game. Go early for the best choice, bring small change, and keep an eye on your bag in the crowds.

Afterwards, the Panteão Nacional and São Vicente de Fora are right there, and the lanes of Alfama tumble away below. Our walking guide to [Alfama, Mouraria and Graça](/stories/lisbon-alfama-mouraria-graca/) picks up from here.

## Príncipe Real: organic Saturdays and monthly fairs

Up the hill from Chiado, the garden at Príncipe Real hosts a whole calendar of markets. Visit Lisboa's [listing for the Príncipe Real fairs](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/events/fairs-and-markets-of-principe-real) gives this pattern:

| Market | When |
|---|---|
| Organic products market | Every Saturday, 7am to 2pm |
| Antiques, bric-a-brac and handicrafts | Last Saturday and last Monday of the month, 10am to 6pm |
| Portugal Real (food) | Second Friday and Saturday of the month, 10am to 6pm |
| Sabores de Perdição (gourmet products) | First Friday and Saturday of the month, 10am to 6pm |

The Saturday organic market, with producers from across Portugal, is the one to plan around if you like to shop for fruit and vegetables. Combine it with a slow wander through the neighbourhood's shops and a look over the city from the nearby viewpoint of São Pedro de Alcântara.

## Campo Pequeno: the Agrobio organic market

On the other side of town, the garden at Campo Pequeno hosts the **[Agrobio organic market](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/events/agrobio-organic-market)** on Saturdays. Visit Lisboa describes stalls of certified Portuguese organic farmers selling vegetables, aromatic plants, fruit and pulses, and notes that entry is free. It is a useful option if you are staying in the Avenidas Novas or near Saldanha, and a good reason for vegetarians to stock up. Our guide to [eating vegetarian and vegan in Portugal](/stories/vegetarian-in-portugal/) has more ideas.

## LX Factory on Sundays

Under the 25 de Abril bridge in Alcântara, the old industrial complex of LX Factory has become a cluster of shops, cafés and studios. On Sundays it hosts two markets, according to [Visit Lisboa](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/events/lx-market):

- **Lx Market**, a Sunday fair of handmade and vintage goods, with free entry.
- **LX Rural**, a farmers' market where producers sell directly to customers, every Sunday from 10am to 6pm.

It gets busy on sunny days. Go mid-morning, browse the market, then have lunch in one of the cafés on site.

## CCB Market in Belém

On the **first Sunday of each month**, the Centro Cultural de Belém holds the [CCB Market](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/events/ccb-market), which Visit Lisboa describes as focused on seasonal, certified Portuguese produce, alongside crafts, workshops and cooking demonstrations, with free entry. It pairs neatly with a day among Belém's monuments; our guide to [Belém in a day](/stories/belem-in-a-day/) has the rest.

## Mercado de Algés, just outside the city

Just west of Belém, across the city boundary in Algés, the **[Mercado de Algés](https://mercadodealges.pt/)** is a food hall with a cluster of counters under one roof, built around eating, drinking and meeting friends. Its site lists opening hours of 11am to midnight from Sunday to Thursday, and until 2am on Fridays, Saturdays and the eve of public holidays.

## Neighbourhood markets

Beyond the headline names, many Lisbon neighbourhoods still have a covered municipal market, where stallholders sell fish, fruit, vegetables and flowers. These are working markets rather than attractions, so go in the morning, when the stalls are full, and check the opening days on the door. If you are renting an apartment, the nearest one will quickly become part of your routine; our guide to [where to stay in Lisbon](/stories/where-to-stay-in-lisbon/) will help you choose a base.

## Tips for market days

- **Go early.** Fresh produce is best, and crowds smallest, in the first hours.
- **Bring cash and a bag.** Small change makes life easier at the stalls, and a tote saves on plastic.
- **Ask before photographing** stallholders and their goods up close.
- **Taste.** Cheese, olives, fruit and cured meats are often offered to try.
- **Check the day.** Lisbon's markets keep strict schedules, and a Saturday-only market will not be there on a Friday.

## Before you book

- Plan your week around the market days you care about: Feira da Ladra on Tuesdays and Saturdays, Príncipe Real's organic market and Campo Pequeno on Saturdays, LX Factory on Sundays, and the CCB Market on the first Sunday of the month.
- Check current opening hours on [Time Out Market's site](https://www.timeout.com/time-out-market-lisboa) and the relevant [Visit Lisboa listings](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/places/feira-da-ladra-flea-market) before you go; schedules can change around public holidays.
- If you plan to cook, look for accommodation with a kitchen near a neighbourhood market.
- Keep valuables secure in crowded markets, especially at Feira da Ladra and the Ribeira hall at peak times.
