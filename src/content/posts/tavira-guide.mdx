Tavira wakes slowly. The fishing boats rock on the River Gilão, a few early walkers cross the old bridge, and the cafés along the water put out their chairs without much hurry. By mid-morning the sun is on the white houses and their hipped tiled roofs, the church towers stand out sharp against the sky, and the smell of coffee and grilled sardines drifts along the quay.

This is the eastern Algarve, and it feels different from the cliffs and coves further west. The coast here is flat, sandy and sheltered behind the lagoons of the Ria Formosa. Tavira itself is a proper old town, with more churches than seems reasonable, a castle on the hill and a riverside that still belongs to the people who live there. It is the place to come when you want the Algarve at an easier pace.

## The river and the old bridge

Everything in Tavira starts at the Gilão. The town grew up on both banks, and the river is still its spine, lined with quays, gardens and the old market hall.

The **Ponte Antiga**, the old bridge, is the town's symbol. Locals often call it the Roman bridge, but the council's [heritage pages](https://cm-tavira.pt/site/descobrir/outro-patrimonio/) are more careful: the first written references to it appear in the medieval chronicle of the conquest of the Algarve from the Moors in the 13th century, even if many in town believe the Romans built it. Today it is classified as a monument of public interest, and it is at its loveliest in the late afternoon.

<Pair a="tavira-river-boat" b="tavira-santa-maria" captionA="A small boat on the still river in Tavira." captionB="The bell tower of Santa Maria do Castelo, above the old town." />

Just downstream, the **Mercado da Ribeira** sits on the riverfront. The council records that the market hall opened on 30 June 1887 and served as the town market until 1999, before restoration turned it to shops, cafés and cultural uses. It is a fine place to sit with a drink and watch the river.

## Up to the castle

From the river, climb the lanes to the top of Santa Maria hill. Tavira's castle, a national monument, has deep roots: the council's [heritage notes](https://cm-tavira.pt/site/descobrir/outro-patrimonio/) describe a Phoenician wall built here in the 8th or 7th century BC, and a castle built by the Muslims who refounded the town in the late 10th or early 11th century, partly to guard the ford across the Gilão.

<Figure id="tavira-castle-garden" caption="Bougainvillea over the walls of Tavira castle, where a small garden fills the old enclosure." />

Inside the walls is a small garden, and from the battlements you look out over a sea of tiled roofs to the river, the salt pans and the lagoon beyond.

Next door stands the **Igreja de Santa Maria do Castelo**, the town's main church. According to the council's [guide to Tavira's churches](https://cm-tavira.pt/site/descobrir/igrejas/), it was probably built in the 13th century over the mosque of the Muslim period. The 1755 earthquake damaged it so badly that it was rebuilt from 1790 to a neoclassical design. The same page lists many other churches and chapels across the municipality; you will stumble across many of them simply by wandering.

## Wandering the old town

Tavira rewards aimless walking. The streets on both banks are lined with houses with ironwork balconies, tiled doorways and, on many roofs, the distinctive four-sided hipped tiles that give the skyline its texture. Squares open up unexpectedly; church towers appear at the end of lanes.

<Figure id="tavira-bell-tower" caption="A church bell tower above the rooftops of Tavira as the light fades." />

Take it slowly, stop for coffee, and save some energy for the evening, when the riverside fills with people out for a stroll and the light turns warm on the white walls.

## The Ria Formosa

Tavira sits at the edge of the **Parque Natural da Ria Formosa**, and you should give the lagoon at least a day. The park authority ICNF's [visitor guide](https://www.icnf.pt/api/file/doc/f61c223d0d89e467) describes it stretching for 60 kilometres along the coast, from the Ancão peninsula to Manta Rota, and covering around 18,400 hectares. Most of that is lagoon: a chain of sandy islands and peninsulas shelters a maze of channels, salt marsh, mudflats and salt pans, much of which is uncovered at low tide. The Gilão is one of the rivers that feed it.

It is also one of Europe's great places for birds. The same guide notes that more than 20,000 waterbirds gather here in the wintering season. Bring binoculars, even if you have never used them before.

## Ilha de Tavira and Barril

Tavira's beach is not in the town but on the barrier island across the lagoon. The council's [beach page for Ilha de Tavira](https://cm-tavira.pt/site/descobrir/praias/praia-da-ilha-de-tavira/) lists access from Tavira itself, from the Quatro Águas pier or by water taxi. It notes that the beach has lifeguards, restaurants and bars, a first-aid post and a campsite, and that it holds a Blue Flag. Stay on the boardwalks: the council asks visitors not to walk on the dunes, which protect the island from erosion and storms.

Further west on the same island is **Praia do Barril**, and it is worth the extra effort. ICNF's guide describes a short trail starting at Pedras d'El Rei, across a footbridge, and running out to the beach. Here a tuna fishery, the armação do Barril, operated between 1841 and 1966; its fishermen lived at the camp from April to September, catching the tuna that passed offshore on their migration into and out of the Mediterranean. What survives is the old camp, now used for beach services, and the haunting **anchor cemetery**, rows of rusting anchors laid out in the sand.

A small train line to the beach was built about a century ago to serve the fishery, according to the same guide. Walking the path beside it is a pleasure in itself.

### Cacela Velha

East of Tavira, the little village of Cacela Velha sits on a low bluff above the lagoon. ICNF's guide describes its historic core, classified as a property of public interest, as one of the most important and best preserved heritage ensembles in the Algarve, once a pre-Roman trading post and Roman military base whose heyday came in the Islamic period. Go at sunset, when the lagoon below turns silver.

## Getting there

Tavira has a railway station; the council notes that the train first reached the town in 1905. Today it is on the Algarve line between Vila Real de Santo António and Lagos, which CP says has been [fully electrified since 19 July 2026](https://www.cp.pt/info/en/w/improved-service-algarve-line), with electric trains running the length of the line. The [current timetable](https://www.cp.pt/info/documents/d/cp/comboios-regionais-vila-real-s-antonio-lagos-19-julho-2026) lists Tavira along with Faro, Loulé and Lagos, so you can reach Faro or the far west of the Algarve without a car. See our guide to [getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) for the wider network, and our [Lagos guide](/stories/lagos-guide/) if you plan to combine east and west.

## Eating

Tavira lives off the lagoon and the sea. Look for clams and cockles from the Ria Formosa, octopus, and tuna in every form, a reminder of the fisheries that once shaped the coast. Our guide to [what to eat in Portugal](/stories/what-to-eat-in-portugal/) covers the Algarve's specialities, from cataplana to the region's almond and fig sweets.

## Where to stay

- **[Pousada Convento Tavira](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-tavira).** Part of the Pousadas de Portugal group, it was created from the reconstruction of the Convent of Nossa Senhora da Graça in the historic centre, a few minutes' walk from the old bridge. Its rooms are set around a courtyard and cloister. Its page notes a closure for structural works from 9 November to 22 December 2026.
- **[Vila Galé Albacora](https://www.vilagale.com/en/hotels-portugal/hotels-algarve/vila-gale-albacora/).** Out towards Quatro Águas, inside the Ria Formosa Natural Park, this hotel occupies the restored Arraial Ferreira Neto, a former tuna-fishing camp where, as the hotel explains, fishing families lived from March to September. The old houses, workshops and warehouses now hold its rooms, with views over the lagoon.

Tavira is also one of our favourite winter bases in the Algarve, because it is a real town that stays open all year. Our piece on [the Algarve in winter](/stories/algarve-in-winter/) explains why.

## When to go

Summer is hot and the island beach is busy, though rarely as packed as the resorts further west. Spring and autumn are ideal for walking the town and the lagoon trails. Winter is quiet, mild on good days, and the best time for birdwatching. Our month-by-month guide to [the best time to visit Portugal](/stories/best-time-to-visit-portugal/) has the detail.

## Before you book

- Check the Algarve line timetable on [CP's site](https://www.cp.pt/info/documents/d/cp/comboios-regionais-vila-real-s-antonio-lagos-19-julho-2026) for your dates.
- If you plan to stay at the Pousada in late 2026, note its [published closure](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-tavira) from 9 November to 22 December.
- Check the current crossing times to Ilha de Tavira locally or via the [council's beach page](https://cm-tavira.pt/site/descobrir/praias/praia-da-ilha-de-tavira/); services change with the season.
- Bring binoculars and plan a morning in the Ria Formosa, especially between autumn and spring.
- Stay on boardwalks on the island and do not walk on the dunes.
