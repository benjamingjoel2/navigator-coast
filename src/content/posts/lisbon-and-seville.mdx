Lisbon smells of the sea. Seville smells of orange blossom in spring and hot stone in summer. One faces the Atlantic from its hills; the other spreads out flat along the Guadalquivir, with the Giralda rising over everything. They are two of the most beautiful cities on the Iberian peninsula, they are a day's journey apart, and they make a natural pair for a first trip that takes in both Portugal and Spain.

The question is not whether to combine them, but how. The border between them runs through the far south of Portugal, which means the journey can be a long day on a coach, a road trip with a night or two in the Algarve, or a short flight. Here is how we would put it together.

## How long to give each city

For a trip of about ten days, we would split it like this:

| Nights | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Lisbon | The old hill neighbourhoods, Belém and a day in Sintra |
| 1–2 | Eastern Algarve (Tavira) | Breaks the journey and adds the coast |
| 3–4 | Seville | The Alcázar, the cathedral, Triana and slow evenings |

With a week, drop the Algarve stop and travel straight through: four nights in Lisbon, three in Seville. With two weeks, add Sintra as an overnight, give the Algarve three nights, and consider a side trip from Seville into the rest of Andalusia.

Plan the trip as an open jaw: fly into one city and out of the other, so the long overland leg only happens once.

## Getting between Lisbon and Seville

### By coach

The simplest door-to-door option is the coach. FlixBus's [Lisbon to Seville route page](https://global.flixbus.com/bus-routes/bus-lisbon-seville) lists several departures a day, from stops at Lisbon Oriente, Sete Rios and Jardim Zoológico, arriving at Seville's Plaza de Armas and Prado de San Sebastián bus stations and at Santa Justa train station. Journey times vary by departure, so check the timetable for your date, and book a daytime coach if you want to watch the Alentejo and the Algarve go by.

### By car

Driving gives you the freedom to stop on the way, which is the best reason to do it. The route runs south through the Alentejo to the Algarve, then east along the coast to the Guadiana river and the border with Spain.

Two things to sort out before you go. First, tolls: some Portuguese motorways have electronic-only tolls collected by overhead gantries, and [Portugal Tolls](https://www.pttolls.com/) explains how foreign-registered vehicles can pay, including Easytoll, which links a bank card to the number plate, and prepaid Tollcards. Second, if you are hiring a car, tell the rental company you plan to cross into Spain and ask about any cross-border and one-way fees. It can be simpler to hand a Portuguese car back in the Algarve and continue by coach.

### By air

There are flights between the two cities, and they make sense if time is very short. You lose the view of the country in between, though, which on this route is half the pleasure.

## Lisbon: four nights

Start where the city began, on the hill below São Jorge Castle. Alfama, Mouraria and Graça are a tangle of stairways, tiny squares and viewpoints over the river, best explored on foot; our [guide to Alfama, Mouraria and Graça](/stories/lisbon-alfama-mouraria-graca/) maps out a route. To get around, the Lisbon metro's [ticket page](https://www.metrolisboa.pt/en/buy/) explains the rechargeable navegante occasional card and the 24-hour tickets for metro, buses and trams.

<Pair a="lse-lisbon-tiles" b="lse-alcazar-tiles" captionA="Azulejos on an old wall in Lisbon." captionB="Glazed tile and carved plaster inside Seville's Real Alcázar." />

Give a day to Belém. Visit Lisboa notes that the Jerónimos Monastery was [proclaimed a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/p/Heritage), together with Belém Tower; our [Belém in a day](/stories/belem-in-a-day/) has the details. Give another day to Sintra, in the wooded hills west of the city, and plan it carefully with our [Sintra day trip](/stories/sintra-day-trip/) guide.

In the Baixa, [Confeitaria Nacional](https://www.confeitarianacional.com/) on Praça da Figueira has been on the same square since it opened in 1829, and it is a good place to start the habit of an afternoon pastry. You will find that Seville has its own version of the habit waiting for you.

The two cities share more than you might expect. Both were shaped by centuries of Moorish rule, both grew rich on Atlantic trade, and both cover their walls in glazed tiles. Seeing them a few days apart makes the family resemblance, and the differences, much clearer.

## The Algarve in between

If you are driving, or happy to take two shorter coach legs, break the journey in the eastern Algarve. Tavira, close to the Spanish border, is a low-rise town of churches, tiled facades and a river crossed by an old bridge; our [Tavira guide](/stories/tavira-guide/) covers where to walk and eat. From here, the islands of the Ria Formosa lagoon are an easy outing, and our [Ria Formosa islands guide](/stories/ria-formosa-islands/) explains how to reach them.

The Algarve is also on Portugal's rail network. CP's [Algarve page](https://www.cp.pt/info/en/w/algarve-places) suggests the Alfa Pendular from Lisbon to Faro as one of the best ways to get there, which opens up a train-plus-coach version of the trip.

## Seville: three or four nights

Seville rewards early starts and late nights, with a long pause in between. Plan the big sights for the morning, keep the middle of the day for a slow lunch in the shade, and go out again when the evening comes.

<Figure id="lse-giralda-cathedral" caption="The Giralda and Seville Cathedral above the rooftops of the old town." />

Begin at the [Real Alcázar](https://alcazarsevilla.org/), which describes itself as the oldest royal palace still in use in Europe, with art and architecture spanning the 11th century to the present. Its opening hours change with the season (it closes earlier from October to March than from April to September), and visits are booked through its own website. Leave time for the gardens, which are as much a reason to come as the halls.

Next door is the [cathedral](https://www.catedraldesevilla.es/), with the Giralda, its bell tower and the symbol of the city. Tickets can be bought on the cathedral's website, which also has the current opening times. Between the two, you will have walked through much of Seville's history in a single morning.

<Figure id="lse-alcazar-garden" caption="A pond and palms in the gardens of the Real Alcázar." />

On another day, walk south through the Parque de María Luisa to the Plaza de España. The city's tourism office explains that the architect Aníbal González designed it for the [Ibero-American Exposition of 1929](https://visitasevilla.es/en/anibal-gonzalez-the-architect-of-seville/), and that King Alfonso XIII inaugurated it; its great semicircle of brick, tile and arcades is the image on this page. Cross the river to Triana in the evening, the old neighbourhood of potters and flamenco, for tapas along the riverbank.

For a sweet stop, [Confitería La Campana](https://www.confiterialacampana.com/), at the top of Calle Sierpes, was founded in 1885 and makes its pastries by hand.

## When to go

We like this trip best in spring and autumn, when long days on foot in both cities are comfortable and the queues are shorter than in high summer. Winter is quieter, with shorter days.

## Before you book

- Portugal and Spain are both in the Schengen area. GOV.UK's [Portugal entry requirements](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/portugal/entry-requirements) explain the EU's Entry/Exit System and the 90-days-in-180 rule, which counts your days across all Schengen countries; the [Spain travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/spain) covers the Spanish side.
- Book an open-jaw flight: into Lisbon, out of Seville, or the reverse.
- Check coach times for your date on the [FlixBus route page](https://global.flixbus.com/bus-routes/bus-lisbon-seville) and book ahead in summer.
- If you are driving, arrange toll payment through [Portugal Tolls](https://www.pttolls.com/) and confirm cross-border rules and one-way fees with your rental company.
- Book the [Real Alcázar](https://alcazarsevilla.org/) and the [cathedral](https://www.catedraldesevilla.es/) online before you arrive, and check their seasonal opening hours.
