The approach is half the welcome. On a clear day the plane swings in over the Atlantic, the coast unrolls beneath the wing, and then suddenly there is the city: red roofs, the wide silver Tagus, the long bridge, all much closer than you expect. Lisbon's airport sits almost inside the city, and that is the best news for anyone arriving tired with a suitcase.

Visit Lisboa puts the airport [just 7 km from the centre](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/traveller-information). That makes the journey in short, whichever way you do it. The real decisions are about luggage, the time of day and where exactly you are staying, because Lisbon's hills and its tangle of narrow streets matter far more than the distance.

## Which terminal are you in?

Lisbon has two passenger terminals, and it is worth knowing which one you land at before you plan the next step.

- **Terminal 1** is the main terminal, and the one with the metro station.
- **Terminal 2** is used by low-cost carriers. The airport's [Terminal 2 page](https://www.aeroportolisboa.pt/pt/lis/acesso-e-estacionamento/chegar-e-sair-do-aeroporto/terminal-2) lists the airlines that use it and explains the free shuttle bus that links it with Terminal 1. Check the current shuttle hours there if you are arriving very late at night.

## Passport control and the EES

If you hold a non-EU passport, expect the Entry/Exit System at the border. As [GOV.UK's entry requirements page](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/portugal/entry-requirements) explains, on your first visit under the system you may be directed to a booth to register your fingerprints and photo before the immigration desk; registration is free, and checks can take longer than you are used to. Build some slack into any onward plan on the day you land. Our guide to [Portugal's entry requirements, EES and ETIAS](/stories/portugal-entry-requirements/) goes through the rules in full.

## Before you leave arrivals

There is a tourist information desk in the arrivals hall. Visit Lisboa lists the [Ask Me Lisboa airport desk](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/places/ask-me-lisboa-aeroporto-de-lisboa) as open every day from 07:00 to 22:00, offering transport and tourism information, maps and accommodation bookings. If you are thinking about a Lisboa Card, Visit Lisboa says the [card includes free public transport](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/p/lisboa-card) in the city; check exactly what it covers, and where to collect it, before you buy.

If you have a contactless bank card, you may not need any ticket at all for the metro or the buses. More on that below.

## The metro: cheap, simple, and best with light bags

The airport has its own metro station, Aeroporto, at the end of the red line. According to Metropolitano de Lisboa, the [metro runs every day](https://www.metrolisboa.pt/en/travel/timetables-and-frequency/) from 06:30 until 01:00, and you can reach any station on your line if you board before 1am.

From Aeroporto, the red line runs through Oriente, where you can change for mainline and suburban trains, then on to Alameda, Saldanha and São Sebastião, as the [network diagram](https://www.metrolisboa.pt/en/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2026/11/Diagrama-da-Rede_site_2026_download.pdf) shows. Each of those three central stations is an interchange: Alameda with the green line towards the Baixa, Saldanha with the yellow line, and São Sebastião with the blue line. The airport's [public transport page](https://www.aeroportolisboa.pt/pt/lis/acesso-e-estacionamento/chegar-e-sair-do-aeroporto/transportes-publicos) puts the ride from the airport to Saldanha at around 20 minutes.

### Paying

The [metro's ticket page](https://www.metrolisboa.pt/en/buy/) sets out the options:

- **Contactless bank card.** Tap your card at the gates. Take it out of your wallet first, and remember each card covers one person, so groups need one card each.
- **navegante occasional card.** A reusable card you load with single tickets or with zapping credit, which works across the member operators.
- **24-hour tickets.** Valid for 24 hours from first use, with versions that add Carris buses and trams, and combinations with ferries or CP suburban trains.

Check current fares on the metro's own page; the ticket machines in the station take cards.

### When the metro is not the answer

The metro is the cheapest way in and often the fastest. But think about the last stretch. If your hotel or apartment is in Alfama, Mouraria, Graça or the Bairro Alto, the nearest station may still leave you with a steep climb over cobbles. Two large suitcases, a hill and a staircase are a miserable combination after a flight. Our walk through [Alfama, Mouraria and Graça](/stories/lisbon-alfama-mouraria-graca/) will give you an idea of the gradients.

## Buses

City buses also serve the airport. Visit Lisboa lists [Carris buses](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/traveller-information) as running daily from 05:30 to 00:30, with a night service between 00:30 and 05:30, which makes them the fallback for flights that land after the metro closes.

You can pay on board. According to Carris's [occasional journeys page](https://www.carris.pt/en/buy/occasional-journeys-2/), on-board tickets are valid only on the vehicle where you buy them, and you can also pay with a bank card or contactless payment at the validator. Use Carris's [journey planner](https://www.carris.pt/en/travel/planear-viagem/) to find the route to your address, because lines and stops change. City buses have limited room for luggage at busy times; with big cases, the metro or a taxi is usually easier.

## Taxis and ride-hailing

A taxi is the straightforward choice if you have heavy bags, are arriving late, are travelling as a family, or are staying somewhere the metro does not reach comfortably. The journey is short in normal traffic. Use the official taxi rank outside arrivals rather than accepting offers from anyone approaching you inside the terminal.

Ride-hailing apps also operate at the airport, from a designated pick-up area. The airport's [pick-up and drop-off page](https://www.aeroportolisboa.pt/en/lis/pick-up-drop-off) explains where it is; follow the signs rather than walking out to the kerb and guessing.

A few tips that apply to both:

- Have your address written down, including the street number, and if possible a landmark nearby. Many old-town streets are pedestrian or one-way, so drivers may stop at the closest point they can reach.
- If you are staying in a pedestrian lane, ask your host in advance where a car can drop you.
- Ask for a receipt in a taxi if you want one.

## Which option for which traveller

| You are... | We would take... |
| --- | --- |
| Travelling light, staying near a metro station | The metro, paying by contactless card |
| Arriving after 1am | A taxi, ride-hailing, or a night bus |
| Carrying big cases to a hilltop neighbourhood | A taxi or ride-hailing car to the door, or as close as it can get |
| Heading straight out of town by train | The metro to Oriente, then CP |
| A group or family | A taxi or a larger ride-hailing car, booked through the app |

## Leaving again

Everything works in reverse, with one addition: allow more time than you think. Border checks under the EES apply on the way out too, and GOV.UK notes you may need to give a fingerprint or photo when leaving. If you are flying from Terminal 2, take the free shuttle from Terminal 1 or go there directly by taxi. Our wider guide to [getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) explains metro cards and contactless payment in Lisbon and Porto in more detail.

## Before you book

- Check which terminal your airline uses, and the current shuttle hours if it is Terminal 2.
- Look up your accommodation on a map: how far is it from the nearest metro station, and is it uphill?
- If you land after 1am, plan on a taxi, ride-hailing or the night bus.
- Make sure each traveller has their own contactless card if you plan to tap in on the metro.
- Allow extra time at passport control for the EES, on arrival and on departure.
