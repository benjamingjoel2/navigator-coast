At midnight on New Year's Eve, the whole bowl of Funchal seems to catch fire. Fireworks go up along the waterfront and across the hillsides at once, and the city, strung with Christmas lights from the harbour to the upper slopes, cheers itself into January.

It is one of the great nights on Portugal's calendar, but far from the only one. The country's year is stitched together with feasts: carnival parades in winter, candlelit processions in spring, saints' nights in June that fill whole cities with the smoke of grilled sardines, and harvest festivals as the grapes come in. Plan a trip around one and you will see Portugal at its most joyful, and most crowded.

This is our month-by-month guide, with dates for 2027 where the organisers have published them. Dates do shift, so always confirm on the official sites linked below before you book.

## February: Carnival

Carnival follows the church calendar, landing in February or early March. In Madeira, the regional [tourist entertainment calendar](https://visitmadeira.com/media/1knpekh3/calend%C3%A1rio-de-anima%C3%A7%C3%A3o-turistica_2026-2029_en.pdf) lists 2027's festivities in downtown Funchal from 3 to 14 February, with the children's carnival on 4 February, the Allegoric Carnival Parade on 5 and 6 February and the irreverent Slapstick Parade on 9 February. Our [Funchal guide](/stories/funchal-guide/) has more on the city itself.

On the mainland, towns stage their own parades. Loulé's has a long history; check the municipality's programme for dates. In the Azores, Terceira marks the season with satirical street performances, the danças and bailinhos, as our [Terceira guide](/stories/terceira-guide/) explains.

## March and April: Holy Week in Braga

Easter Sunday falls on 28 March in 2027, so Holy Week runs through the second half of March. Nowhere marks it with more solemnity than Braga, the old archiepiscopal city of the north.

The [official Semana Santa de Braga site](https://semanasantabraga.com/) notes that the celebrations are listed on Portugal's National Inventory of Intangible Cultural Heritage. Its programme includes the night-time Ecce Homo procession, which in recent years has left the Misericórdia church at 9.30pm on Maundy Thursday, as well as processions through the old centre over the following days. Check the programme for 2027 once it appears.

## May: pilgrims, flowers and the Holy Christ

May is a month of faith and flowers.

**Fátima.** The sanctuary's [events calendar](https://www.fatima.pt/en/events) lists the international anniversary pilgrimage on 12 and 13 May 2027, the biggest dates in its year. Expect enormous crowds and accommodation booked out well ahead. If you want to see the sanctuary quietly, go another week.

**Ponta Delgada.** On São Miguel, Visit Azores describes the [Festa do Senhor Santo Cristo dos Milagres](https://www.visitazores.com/en/the-azores/sao-miguel) as an age-old festival venerating the statue of Christ kept at the Esperança chapel in Ponta Delgada. It says the festivities last three days around the fifth Sunday after Easter, which in 2027 points to the start of May.

<Figure id="fest-ponta-delgada" caption="Ponta Delgada's town hall square. In May the city fills for the Senhor Santo Cristo festivities." />

**Funchal's Flower Festival.** Madeira's spring showpiece runs from 29 April to 30 May 2027, according to the [regional calendar](https://visitmadeira.com/media/1knpekh3/calend%C3%A1rio-de-anima%C3%A7%C3%A3o-turistica_2026-2029_en.pdf). Highlights include the Wall of Hope ceremony on 1 May and the Great Allegoric Flower Parade on 2 and 16 May, with flower installations around the city throughout.

## June: the month of the saints

If you only plan around one month, make it June. The popular saints, Santo António, São João and São Pedro, are celebrated with street parties across the country.

**Lisbon: Santo António.** The [Festas de Lisboa](https://www.visitlisboa.com/pt-pt/eventos/festas-de-lisboa-1) take over the city through June. Visit Lisboa describes street parties with music, dancing and grilled sardines in Alfama, Bica, Bairro Alto, Castelo and Mouraria; the Marchas Populares, when neighbourhoods compete in a parade down Avenida da Liberdade; and the Santo António weddings at the Sé, a nod to the saint's reputation as a matchmaker. Pots of manjerico, a small-leaved basil, are sold everywhere and given as tokens of love. Visit Lisboa lists the 2027 edition for June, with details to be confirmed. Our guide to [Alfama, Mouraria and Graça](/stories/lisbon-alfama-mouraria-graca/) will help you find your way through the arraiais.

**Porto: São João.** On the night of 23 June, Porto stays up until dawn. For 2026, the city's [Agenda Porto listing](https://www.agenda-porto.pt/en/page/festas-sao-joao-2026-1779981091893/) showed stages open from 8pm to 4am and a 12-minute fireworks display at midnight over the Porto and Gaia riversides, between the Luiz I and Arrábida bridges. It also lists traditions that run through the month, including a community model of the city's houses and the parish rusgas, now a competition within the official programme. Book a room months ahead and expect to walk everywhere.

<Figure id="fest-porto-ribeira-night" caption="Porto's Ribeira after dark. On São João the riverfront fills for midnight fireworks." />

**Terceira: Sanjoaninas.** Angra do Heroísmo celebrates São João too. The [official Sanjoaninas site](https://www.sanjoaninas.pt/) gave the 2026 dates as 19 to 28 June, calls it the largest festival in the Azores, and says 23 June is always the longest and liveliest night of the year in Angra. The Terceira guide covers the island's other feasts, including the Holy Ghost festivals that Visit Azores says are [celebrated on all the islands](https://www.visitazores.com/en/the-azores/sao-miguel).

**Madeira: Atlantic Festival.** Funchal's June festival runs from 4 to 27 June 2027, according to the [regional calendar](https://visitmadeira.com/media/1knpekh3/calend%C3%A1rio-de-anima%C3%A7%C3%A3o-turistica_2026-2029_en.pdf), with an international fireworks contest on each Saturday: 5, 12, 19 and 26 June.

## July: Tomar's trays

Every four years, Tomar hosts the Festa dos Tabuleiros, and 2027 is one of them. Turismo Centro de Portugal gives the dates as [3 to 12 July 2027](https://www.centerofportugal.com/article/trays-feast-in-tomar). Residents make thousands of paper flowers to decorate their streets, and the festival ends with the Sunday parade of tall, decorated trays, as the parishes converge on the Praça da República. It is one of the most remarkable sights in Portugal. Read more in our guide to [Tomar and the Templars](/stories/tomar-and-the-templars/), and book early.

In Madeira, the same month brings the SAL gastronomic festival, listed for 9 to 18 July 2027 in the [regional calendar](https://visitmadeira.com/media/1knpekh3/calend%C3%A1rio-de-anima%C3%A7%C3%A3o-turistica_2026-2029_en.pdf).

## August and September: harvest time

As summer turns, the vineyards take centre stage. The Madeira Wine Festival runs from 22 August to 12 September 2027 according to the [regional calendar](https://visitmadeira.com/media/1knpekh3/calend%C3%A1rio-de-anima%C3%A7%C3%A3o-turistica_2026-2029_en.pdf), with a live harvest in Estreito de Câmara de Lobos on 11 September and concerts in the vineyards on the first two weekends of September. Over on Porto Santo, the Columbus Festival follows from 23 to 26 September.

On Terceira, Visit Azores lists the [Festas da Vinha e do Vinho](https://www.visitazores.com/en/the-azores/terceira) in Biscoitos at the start of September. And in the Douro, the grape harvest itself is the spectacle: see our [three days in the Douro Valley](/stories/three-days-in-the-douro-valley/) for timing and how to visit during the vindima.

## October and November: quieter months

Autumn is gentler. Madeira's calendar lists a Nature Festival from 5 to 10 October 2027, and Visit Azores mentions [Angra Jazz](https://www.visitazores.com/en/the-azores/terceira) in October. Across the country, this is a good time to travel without festival prices.

## December: lights and fireworks

Christmas brings lights to every town square, but Funchal goes further than most. The [regional calendar](https://visitmadeira.com/media/1knpekh3/calend%C3%A1rio-de-anima%C3%A7%C3%A3o-turistica_2026-2029_en.pdf) lists the illumination of the city's amphitheatre and Christmas displays downtown from 1 December 2026 to 10 January 2027, the New Year's Eve fireworks on 31 December, and the Chants of Epiphany in the Municipal Gardens on 5 January. The pattern repeats the following winter.

## Before you book

- Confirm this year's dates on the official sites before booking: [Visit Madeira's calendar](https://visitmadeira.com/media/1knpekh3/calend%C3%A1rio-de-anima%C3%A7%C3%A3o-turistica_2026-2029_en.pdf) says it is subject to change, and Lisbon's dates are still to be confirmed.
- Book accommodation early for São João in Porto, Santo António in Lisbon, Fátima in May, the Tabuleiros in Tomar and New Year in Funchal.
- Expect closed roads, crowded public transport and late nights on the big festival dates; plan to walk.
- Religious processions are acts of worship. Dress respectfully and keep a little distance when photographing.
- If you would rather avoid the crowds, use this calendar the other way round, and visit just before or after.
