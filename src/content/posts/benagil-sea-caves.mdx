You hear it before you see it. The slap of water against rock echoes under the dome, and then the boat noses through the entrance and the light changes. Above you, layers of ochre and cream limestone curve up into a vast vault, open to the sky through a round hole in the roof. Below, a crescent of sand catches the sun. For a minute or two, everyone stops talking.

The Algar de Benagil, on the coast of Lagoa municipality in the central Algarve, has become one of the most photographed places in Portugal. That popularity brought crowded water, accidents and falling rock, and since the summer of 2024 the cave has had its own set of rules. If you saw a photo of someone lying on that beach, forget it: you can no longer land there. Here is what is allowed now, how to visit well, and where else to go.

## Why there are rules

The rules come from the Capitania do Porto de Portimão, the local maritime authority. Its [Edital 019/2024](https://www.amn.pt/DGAM/Capitanias/Portimao/Lists/Documentos_AMN/20240730%20Edital%20019-2024%20-%20Instru%C3%A7%C3%A3o%20Navega%C3%A7%C3%A3o%20Grutas%20de%20Benagil_signed.pdf) sets out the reasons plainly. The cliffs here erode through sudden, unpredictable rockfalls, some with serious consequences already recorded. The beach inside the cave lies entirely within the cliff risk zone, as the boulders on its sand show. And the water had become crowded with tour boats, private craft, rented kayaks and swimmers, with frequent collisions, especially at the cave entrances in summer.

The area also lies inside the Parque Natural Marinho do Recife do Algarve – Pedra do Valado, a marine park created in 2024. The rules aim to keep visits safe and the reef healthy at the same time.

## The rules, in plain English

The edital covers the sea between Praia do Vale do Lapa and Praia de Albandeira, for anyone coming within 500 metres of the shore. It was amended in July 2025 by [Edital 009/2025](https://www.amn.pt/DGAM/Capitanias/Portimao/Lists/Documentos_AMN/20250702%20Eidtal%20009-2025,%20alt1%20ao%20Edital%20019-2024_signed.pdf). Both documents are listed on the [Capitania do Porto de Portimão page](https://www.amn.pt/DGAM/Capitanias/portimao/Paginas/Capitania-do-porto-de-Portimao.aspx), which is the place to check for any later change. As of our last check, these are the points that matter for visitors:

**Banned outright**
- Landing on, or using, the sand inside the Algar de Benagil or any similar cave in the area.
- Reaching the inside of the cave by swimming, or with any flotation aid.
- Renting or touring in kayaks, paddleboards and similar craft without a guide.
- Entering the caves when visibility is under 1,000 metres, and any navigation between sunset and sunrise.
- Diving inside the caves or around their entrances, except authorised scientific work, and fishing in or near the cave.

**How visits work**

| | Motorised boats | Kayaks and paddleboards |
|---|---|---|
| Entrance | West entrance only | East entrance only, single file, in and out in a U-turn |
| Group | Up to 5 boats inside at once | Groups of 6 with a guide trained in first aid; up to 14 inside at once |
| Time inside | 2 minutes | No more than 8 minutes per group |

Everyone on a visit, guests included, must wear a lifejacket for the whole trip, and helmets should be worn near the cliff zones at risk of falling rock. Visits can also be suspended when weather and sea conditions are poor, under a separate notice from the Capitania.

In short: you can still see the inside of the cave, but only from the water, briefly, and with a licensed operator.

## Option 1: a boat trip

A short boat trip is the simplest way in. Licensed maritime-tourism operators run trips along this coast from nearby beaches and harbours, usually combining Benagil with other caves and arches on the same stretch. Two minutes inside does not sound like much, but it is enough to see the dome, the skylight and the light on the water.

<Figure id="benagil-roof-opening" caption="Looking down through the skylight in the cave roof, the view walkers get from the clifftop." />

Choose an operator that clearly states it is licensed for maritime-tourism activity, and expect cancellations when the swell is up. The sea here can change quickly. Check [IPMA's beaches and ports forecasts](https://www.ipma.pt/en/maritima/costeira/) the day before.

## Option 2: a guided kayak or paddleboard trip

Kayaking in is slower, quieter and closer to the rock, and many people prefer it. Under the 2025 amendment, all kayak and paddleboard trips here must be guided, in groups of six with a guide, using the eastern entrance only. You get up to eight minutes inside as a group, which feels generous after the boats' two.

You will be paddling on open sea, sometimes with chop and boat wash, so this is for reasonably confident paddlers. Wear the lifejacket and helmet you are given, keep your place in line, and do not try to land. Good guides will tell you the same.

## Option 3: from the clifftop

You do not have to go on the water at all. The **Percurso dos Sete Vales Suspensos**, or Seven Hanging Valleys Trail, runs along the clifftops right above the cave. Lagoa municipality's [trail page](https://www.cm-lagoa.pt/conhecer/percursos/sete-vales-suspensos) describes it as a 5.7-kilometre walking route linking Praia de Vale Centeanes with Praia da Marinha, following an almost unbroken line of limestone cliffs. The name comes from the streams that cut through those cliffs and mostly reach the sea above sea level, leaving their valleys hanging.

<Pair a="benagil-clifftop" b="benagil-marinha-arch" captionA="Cliffs and coves seen from the trail above Benagil." captionB="A double arch below the cliffs near Praia da Marinha." />

From the path you look down into the cave through the hole in its roof, and the views along the coast to the arches near Praia da Marinha are every bit as striking as the cave itself. Stay on the marked path and well back from the edges and the skylight. The same rockfall risk that closed the beach applies on top: these cliffs crumble without warning.

Walk early in summer, when it is cooler and quieter, carry water, and wear proper shoes. There is little shade. For more coastal walks in the region, see our guide to [the Algarve in winter](/stories/algarve-in-winter/), when the clifftops are at their most pleasant.

Nearby, the shorter [Caminho do Algar Seco](https://www.cm-lagoa.pt/conhecer/percursos/caminho-do-algar-seco) near Carvoeiro leads through what the council describes as a labyrinth of ochre rock, sculpted by rain and sea. It makes a good half-day pairing with the Seven Hanging Valleys.

## Calmer alternatives

Benagil is not the only sea cave on the Algarve. If you want grottoes without the queue, head west to Ponta da Piedade near Lagos. Lagos council's [coast guide](https://www.cm-lagos.pt/images/site/cliente/AF_LAGOS_PRAIAS_E_COSTA.pdf) describes golden rocks sculpted by wind and tide, with arches, stacks and grottoes best explored on foot or by small boat. Our [Lagos guide](/stories/lagos-guide/) covers the headland, the beaches and how to get there by train.

<Pair a="benagil-piedade-grotto" b="benagil-piedade-boats" captionA="Inside a grotto at Ponta da Piedade, near Lagos." captionB="Small boats under the arches off the Lagos coast." />

Further west again, the cliffs of [Sagres and Cape St Vincent](/stories/sagres-and-cape-st-vincent/) are higher, wilder and far less crowded, though the sea there is rarely calm enough for cave trips.

## When to go

Summer usually brings the calmest seas, and with them the most boats. Operators set their own seasons, so check what is running for your dates, and aim for an early departure if you can. Our guide to [the best time to visit Portugal](/stories/best-time-to-visit-portugal/) sets out what to expect from the Algarve's weather month by month.

Whatever the season, the sea decides. If the Capitania suspends visits, or your operator calls off a trip because of swell, take it in good grace. It is for exactly the reasons that led to the rules in the first place.

## Before you book

- Read the rules on the [Capitania do Porto de Portimão page](https://www.amn.pt/DGAM/Capitanias/portimao/Paginas/Capitania-do-porto-de-Portimao.aspx) and check for any new notice before your trip.
- Book boat, kayak or paddleboard trips only with licensed operators; unguided kayak and paddleboard rentals to the caves are not permitted.
- Do not plan to swim in or land on the cave beach. Both are banned.
- Check [IPMA's coastal forecasts](https://www.ipma.pt/en/maritima/costeira/) and keep a spare day in case your trip is cancelled.
- For the clifftop walk, set out early with water, sun protection and solid shoes, and stay on the marked path.
