Drive the length of the Algarve and the coast changes character every hour. In the far west, the land ends in high, wind-scoured cliffs and the Atlantic arrives in long, cold rollers. Through the middle, the rock turns honey-coloured and breaks into stacks, arches and hidden coves. Then, past Faro, the cliffs give way to lagoons, salt pans and low barrier islands, and the light goes soft and silvery over the water.

That is the first thing to understand about where to stay. "The Algarve" is not one place, and a base in Sagres and a base in Tavira make for two quite different holidays. This guide works from west to east, with notes on which towns work without a car and a few established places to stay along the way.

## The shape of the coast

The western edge, from Sagres up towards Aljezur, sits within the Southwest Alentejo and Vicentine Coast Natural Park. Rota Vicentina's [guide to the region](https://rotavicentina.com/en/the-region/) describes around 110 km of wild coastline, with lighter limestone cliffs on the Algarve stretch, and names Lagos as the main gateway from the Algarve side. If you are coming to walk, our guide to [walking the Rota Vicentina](/stories/walking-the-rota-vicentina/) covers the trails.

The central coast, roughly from Lagos to Albufeira, is the classic cliff-and-cove Algarve. East of Faro, the Ria Formosa Natural Park takes over. According to the park authority ICNF's [guide to the Ria Formosa](https://www.icnf.pt/api/file/doc/f61c223d0d89e467), it stretches along 60 km of the Algarve coast, from the Ancão peninsula to Manta Rota, behind a chain of barrier islands and peninsulas.

## Getting around without a car

The Algarve line runs along the whole region. CP says it [connects Vila Real de Santo António and Lagos](https://www.cp.pt/info/en/w/improved-service-algarve-line), is about 141 km long, and that from 19 July the entire line has been electrified, with direct trains between the two ends so that you no longer need to change at Faro. The same page describes roughly hourly trains between Vila Real de Santo António and Faro and a train every two hours between Faro and Lagos, with extra services at peak times.

The [current timetable](https://www.cp.pt/info/documents/d/cp/comboios-regionais-vila-real-s-antonio-lagos-19-julho-2026) lists stops including Lagos, Portimão, Tunes, Albufeira–Ferreiras, Faro, Olhão, Fuseta, Tavira, Cacela and Monte Gordo. Several stations are some way from the town centre or the beach, Albufeira's among them, so check the distance before you count on walking. Our [guide to getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) covers buses, tolls and car hire.

In short: Lagos, Faro, Olhão and Tavira all work well without a car. Sagres, the west coast, the cliffs around Carvoeiro and the inland villages are much easier with one.

## Lagos: the all-rounder

Lagos is the west's liveliest town all year. It has a walled old town, a working harbour and marina, a long sandy beach on one side and, on the other, the sculpted headland of Ponta da Piedade, with its stacks and grottoes, a short walk or ride from the centre. It is also the end of the railway line, which makes car-free trips east easy.

<Figure id="algs-lagos-headland" caption="The cliffs and sea stacks of Ponta da Piedade, on the edge of Lagos." />

It suits first-timers, families who want a real town rather than a resort, and anyone planning to explore the west coast by day. In July and August the old town is busy, so look a little out of the centre if you want quiet nights.

**Where to stay.** The [Lagosmar Hotel](https://www.lagosmar.com/) describes itself as a family hotel on Rua Dr. Faria e Silva, a few steps from the historic centre, and says the marina and the train and bus stations are within walking distance.

## Sagres and the west coast

At the south-western tip of the country, Sagres is small, windswept and focused on the sea: surfing, fishing and long walks along the clifftops towards Cape St Vincent. North of Vila do Bispo, the west coast is wilder still, with surf beaches below the cliffs and very little development. It is a superb base for surfers, walkers and anyone who likes their coast raw. It is less suited to a classic beach holiday, as the water is colder and the wind more persistent than further east.

<Figure id="algs-west-coast-cliffs" caption="A clifftop track above the Atlantic on the far western Algarve." />

**Where to stay.** [memmo Baleeira](https://www.memmohotels.com/baleeira/) in Sagres describes itself as set within the Costa Vicentina Natural Park, with sea-view rooms and a spa with an indoor pool. Inland near Vila do Bispo, [Aldeia da Pedralva](https://www.aldeiadapedralva.com/en/) is a restored rural village: its website says that by 2006 only nine of its original inhabitants remained and many houses were in ruins before the current owners began to rebuild it.

## The central cliffs: Portimão to Carvoeiro

Between Lagos and Albufeira lies the coast on most Algarve postcards. Alvor and Ferragudo are low-key fishing towns at the mouths of their rivers; Portimão is a working city with a big resort beach next door; Carvoeiro is a former fishing village tucked into a cove, with clifftop paths running east. One of the best of these is the Seven Hanging Valleys trail which, according to [Lagoa council](https://www.cm-lagoa.pt/conhecer/percursos/sete-vales-suspensos), is a 5.7 km route linking Praia de Vale Centeanes and Praia da Marinha.

<Figure id="algs-praia-da-marinha" caption="Praia da Marinha, at one end of the Seven Hanging Valleys trail." />

This is the area for coves and coastal walks, and for villa rentals with pools. You will want a car, as the best beaches are not on the railway.

## Albufeira and Vilamoura: the resort belt

Albufeira is the Algarve's busiest resort town, with a whitewashed old centre above the beach and a long strip of bars and hotels to the east. Vilamoura, a little further on, is a purpose-built resort around a large marina, surrounded by golf courses. Both are lively, well supplied with hotels at every level and convenient for families who want everything in one place. Neither is the place for a quiet, local experience, especially in high summer.

## Faro and Olhão

Faro is usually seen only from the airport, which is a pity. Its walled old town, cathedral and marina are compact and pleasant, and boats leave for the Ria Formosa's islands; ICNF's park guide notes the [boat to Ilha Deserta from Faro](https://www.icnf.pt/api/file/doc/f61c223d0d89e467). A night or two here is a sensible start or end to a trip, and it is excellent without a car. Next door, Olhão is a fishing town with a busy waterfront, looking out across the lagoon to the islands of Armona and Culatra.

**Where to stay.** A short drive inland, the [Pousada Palácio Estoi](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-estoi) is a restored 18th-century palace in the village of Estoi, close to the Roman villa of Milreu.

## Tavira and the eastern Algarve

Tavira is the most handsome town in the east: a river lined with old houses, a castle and many churches, and the Ria Formosa on its doorstep, with the beaches of Ilha de Tavira reached by boat. Further east, Cacela Velha sits on a bluff above the lagoon, and Manta Rota and Monte Gordo have long, gently shelving sandy beaches, where the sea is often calmer than in the west.

<Pair a="algs-tavira-bridge" b="algs-ria-formosa-lighthouse" captionA="The old bridge over the River Gilão in Tavira, in late-afternoon light." captionB="A lighthouse on one of the Ria Formosa's barrier islands." />

The east suits families with young children, couples and slow travellers, and anyone who likes a real town to come back to after the beach. It is also good for birdwatching; our piece on [the Algarve in winter](/stories/algarve-in-winter/) explains why the lagoon is at its best in the cooler months.

**Where to stay.** The [Pousada Convento Tavira](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-tavira) occupies the former Convent of Nossa Senhora da Graça, a few minutes' walk from the Roman bridge over the River Gilão. Its page notes that it will be closed for structural works from 9 November to 22 December 2026.

## Inland: Silves, Loulé and Monchique

Behind the coast, the Algarve has a quieter, greener side. Silves sits below its red sandstone castle; Loulé is a market town with a strong local life; Monchique is a hill town in cork and eucalyptus country. They make sense for a second visit, a walking holiday or a hot summer when you would rather sleep somewhere cooler, but you will need a car for the beaches.

## Matching the base to the trip

| If you want... | Look at |
| --- | --- |
| A real town, car-free, near cliffs and beaches | Lagos |
| Surf, wind and wild coast | Sagres, the west coast |
| Coves, clifftop walks and villas | Carvoeiro, Ferragudo, Alvor |
| Lively resort with everything on hand | Albufeira, Vilamoura |
| Short break or easy arrival | Faro |
| Calm sea, lagoons and a handsome town | Tavira, Cacela, Manta Rota |
| Hills and village life | Silves, Loulé, Monchique |

Timing matters as much as place. Our guide to [the best time to visit Portugal](/stories/best-time-to-visit-portugal/) explains how the Algarve changes through the year.

## Booking a villa or apartment

Much of the Algarve's accommodation is in apartments and villas. These are registered as *alojamento local*, and you can check a property's registration in Turismo de Portugal's [national register of local accommodation](https://rnt.turismodeportugal.pt/RNT/Pesquisa_AL.aspx). A legitimate listing should show its number; if it does not, ask for it.

## Before you book

- Decide whether you will hire a car before you choose a base; it changes the answer.
- If you plan to use the train, check the [current timetable](https://www.cp.pt/info/documents/d/cp/comboios-regionais-vila-real-s-antonio-lagos-19-julho-2026) and how far the station is from your accommodation.
- Book well ahead for July and August, when prices and crowds peak across the coast.
- In the west, pack layers and a windproof even in summer.
- For villas and apartments, check the local accommodation registration number and how far the property is from the nearest shop and beach on foot.
- If you are relying on a specific hotel outside summer, confirm it is open for your dates.
