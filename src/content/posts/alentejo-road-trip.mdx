Leave Lisbon early, cross the Tagus, and within the hour the country starts to open up. The hills flatten, the towns thin out, and the road runs on between cork oaks and fields that are green in spring and gold by June. Every so often a white village appears on a hilltop, ringed by walls, with a castle at the top. That is the Alentejo, and a car is the best way to see it.

This five-day loop starts and ends near Lisbon and takes in the region's greatest hits: Évora, the marble towns, the fortress of Elvas on the Spanish border, and Monsaraz above the great lake of Alqueva. Driving times are modest, which leaves the afternoons for long lunches and the evenings for the light.

## Before you set off

You will want a car for this trip; it is by far the easiest way to link the smaller towns. Our guide to [getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) covers car hire and the motorway tolls, including the electronic-only ones. Roads in the Alentejo are generally quiet, but they are also long and straight, and summer heat on the plains is serious: drive in the morning, keep water in the car, and plan indoor sights for the middle of the day.

The other rule is wine. You will pass some very good estates, and Portugal's blood alcohol limit is 0.5 g/l for most drivers, according to the EU's [Your Europe road rules page](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/driving-abroad/road-rules-and-safety/portugal/index_en.htm). Decide in advance who is tasting.

## Day 1: Évora

Évora's historic centre has been a [UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1986](https://www.cp.pt/info/en/w/alentejo-evora), and it is the natural first stop. If you would rather not drive on day one, CP's page points out that the station is close enough to walk to Praça do Giraldo, the main square; you could take the train out and pick up a hire car in the city.

Spend the afternoon on foot: the square with its arcades and marble fountain, the cathedral and its views from the roof, the Roman temple, the Chapel of Bones in the church of São Francisco. The city's museum network sells an [Évora Ticket](https://www.evoraticket.pt/) valid for one, two or three days, covering sites such as the Palácio de Dom Manuel, the Casas Pintadas and the Igreja do Salvador. Évora and the Alentejo are also preparing to be [European Capital of Culture in 2027](https://www.evora2027.com/en/), so look out for events if you travel then. Our full [Évora guide](/stories/evora-guide/) has more.

**Stay:** **Pousada Convento Évora** sits between the cathedral and the Roman temple, in a building whose guest rooms were once monks' cells, per [Pousadas de Portugal](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-evora).

## Day 2: Estremoz, Borba and Vila Viçosa

Head north-east into marble country, where the three towns of Estremoz, Borba and Vila Viçosa sit close together.

Start in **Estremoz**, where the old town climbs to a castle on the hill. The castle was built by King Dinis for Queen Saint Isabel in the 13th century, and its keep, the Torre de Menagem, gives wide views over the plain. Today the castle is the **Pousada Castelo Estremoz**, and [Pousadas de Portugal](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-estremoz) notes that it is in the historic centre, easy to explore on foot. It makes a memorable base for nights two and three.

**Borba** is a short hop away and a wine town as much as a marble one; its cooperative winery, [Adega de Borba](https://www.vinhosdoalentejo.pt/en/producers/adega-de-borba/), is on the Alentejo Wine Route. Then continue to **Vila Viçosa** and its Ducal Palace, the former seat of the House of Bragança. According to the [Fundação da Casa de Bragança](https://www.fcbraganca.pt/en/museum/ducal-palace/), building began in 1501 under Jaime, the fourth Duke, and its 110-metre façade is unique in Portuguese civil architecture. The visit runs through the state rooms, with their frescoes, tiles and painted ceilings, and ends in an enormous kitchen hung with copper.

Check the [opening hours](https://www.fcbraganca.pt/en/visit-us/) before you go: the palace opens only in the afternoon on Tuesdays and is not open on Mondays, and it does not allow filming, selfies or flash photography inside.

## Day 3: Elvas

From Estremoz it is an easy drive east to **Elvas**, the star-shaped garrison town on the Spanish border and a UNESCO World Heritage Site in its own right. Walk the bastions and the ditch, then visit the **Forte da Graça** on its hill to the north, a late-18th-century fortress that later served as a military prison. The [municipality's page](https://cm-elvas.pt/descobrir/forte-da-graca/) lists its seasonal hours; it is closed on Mondays. Finish the day with the Amoreira aqueduct in the evening light, and a plate of the town's golden salt cod.

Elvas deserves its own story, and it has one: see our guide to [Elvas, a fortress town on the Spanish border](/stories/elvas-border-fortress/). Without a car, CP's [Raiano train](https://www.cp.pt/info/en/raiano) also stops in Elvas, on its daily run between Entroncamento and Badajoz.

Return to Estremoz for the night, or stay in Elvas and set off south from there in the morning.

## Day 4: Monsaraz and Alqueva

Drive south through Redondo and Reguengos de Monsaraz to **Monsaraz**, a walled village on a ridge above the Guadiana. Below it spreads the Alqueva reservoir, a maze of inlets and islands. The village is tiny and best explored slowly on foot. The [castle](https://www.visitalentejo.pt/en/catalogue/what-to-do/museums-and-places-to-visit/castelo-de-monsaraz/) is listed as always open, and its walls are the place to be for sunset. Our [Monsaraz and Alqueva](/stories/monsaraz-and-alqueva/) story covers the village and the lake in depth.

<Pair a="art-monsaraz-arch-lane" b="art-monsaraz-street" captionA="A cobbled lane leading to a stone arch in Monsaraz." captionB="Whitewashed houses along one of Monsaraz's main streets." />

Reguengos is also serious wine country, home to large estates and to one of the region's traditional amphora cellars. Our guide to [the Alentejo wine route](/stories/alentejo-wine-route/) suggests where to taste, and the regional tasting room in Évora can [help arrange visits](https://www.vinhosdoalentejo.pt/en/wine-route/visit-us/).

Stay late if you can. The area around Alqueva is promoted as **Dark Sky Alqueva**, which describes itself as [the world's first starlight tourism destination](https://darkskyalqueva.com/en/), with its observatory at Cumeada in the municipality of Reguengos de Monsaraz. Guided [stargazing sessions](https://darkskyalqueva.com/en/atividade/stargazing-session/) need booking, and the experience depends on the weather and the moon, so check before you commit an evening.

<Figure id="art-alqueva-view" size="full" caption="The Alqueva reservoir from the heights around Reguengos de Monsaraz, a scatter of islands and inlets." />

## Day 5: back to Lisbon, or on to the coast

From Monsaraz, the most direct way home runs back through Évora, which gives you a last chance for lunch in the city or a stop you missed on day one. Alternatively, turn this into a longer trip: the Alentejo coast and the Algarve are both within a day's drive, and they make a natural second week.

## Before you book

- Book a car with enough time to collect it calmly, and read up on [tolls and driving](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) before you leave Lisbon.
- Plan around closures: the [Ducal Palace](https://www.fcbraganca.pt/en/visit-us/) in Vila Viçosa and the [Forte da Graça](https://cm-elvas.pt/descobrir/forte-da-graca/) in Elvas both close on Mondays, so do not let Mondays fall on days two and three.
- In summer, start early and save the castles and walls for the evening.
- Book pousadas and dark-sky sessions ahead, especially at weekends and in high season.
- Agree a designated driver before any winery visit.
