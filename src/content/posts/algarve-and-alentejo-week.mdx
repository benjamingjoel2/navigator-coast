Somewhere south of Évora, the road empties. The wheat fields roll away in long, pale swells, cork oaks stand alone with their trunks stripped rust-red, and a white village appears on a hilltop like a scatter of sugar cubes. Then, a day or two later, the land runs out altogether at a line of black cliffs above the Atlantic, and the light changes from gold to silver.

That is the pleasure of putting the Alentejo and the Algarve together. Most visitors fly into Faro and never leave the coast. Spend half the week inland first, and the Algarve makes much more sense: it is the sunny southern edge of a much bigger, quieter country.

## How this week works

This is a road trip. You can reach Évora by train from Lisbon and travel along the Algarve coast by train, but the best of the Alentejo, and the wild west coast between them, are far easier with a car. Pick it up when you leave Évora, not before. Our guide to [getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) covers car hire, and how Portugal's electronic tolls work; the official [PT Tolls](https://www.pttolls.com/en) site explains the options for paying them. The EU's [Your Europe road rules page](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/driving-abroad/road-rules-and-safety/portugal/index_en.htm) sets out the drink-driving limits, which matter in wine country.

**The shape:** two nights in Évora, one on the Alentejo coast, one in Sagres, one in Lagos, two in Tavira, then out from Faro.

**When to go:** spring and autumn. The inland Alentejo is very hot in high summer: IPMA's [climate normal for Beja](https://www.ipma.pt/opencms/bin/file.data/climate-normal/cn_91-20_BEJA.pdf) gives an average July high of 33.5°C. The coast trails are also designed to be walked [between September and June](https://rotavicentina.com/en/help-guide/), according to the Rota Vicentina.

## Days 1–2: Évora

Start in Évora, the Alentejo's capital and the easiest place to arrive. CP's [Évora page](https://www.cp.pt/info/en/w/alentejo-evora) notes that the city has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1986, and that the station is a walk from the centre, past the public garden, the church of São Francisco and its Chapel of Bones, and the markets, to Praça do Giraldo.

Visit Alentejo's [UNESCO page](https://www.visitalentejo.pt/en/alentejo/culture/unesco-world-heritage/world-heritage-list/) lists what to look for: the Roman temple, the cathedral, the painted houses, the Cadaval Palace, the 16th-century university and Praça do Giraldo. Give it a full day, on foot. Climb to the cathedral roof for the view across the terracotta rooftops, then lose the afternoon in the whitewashed lanes inside the walls.

<Pair a="alg-evora-cathedral" b="alg-evora-cloister" captionA="The roof and lantern tower of Évora's cathedral." captionB="The cathedral cloister, seen from the roof above." />

Stay inside the walls. The [Pousada Convento Évora](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-evora), also called the Pousada dos Loios, is in a 15th-century convent between the cathedral and the Roman temple; its rooms are the former monks' cells, and there is a pool in the inner courtyard. For wine, [Cartuxa](https://www.cartuxa.pt/en/), the winery of the Fundação Eugénio de Almeida, is on the edge of town at Quinta de Valbom and books wine-tourism visits through its website.

## Day 2: Monsaraz and the Alqueva

On the second day, drive east to Monsaraz. Visit Alentejo describes the [walled village](https://www.visitalentejo.pt/en/alentejo/nature/transalentejo-alqueva/reguengos-de-monsaraz/) high on its rock at more than 300 metres, with the best views of the Alqueva reservoir, whose water almost reaches the foot of the hill. Walk the castle walls, then wander back through the village's slate and whitewash. It is tiny, so arrive early or late in the day to have it to yourself.

On the way back, stop at a wine estate. [Herdade do Esporão](https://esporao.com/en/origins/alentejo), near Reguengos de Monsaraz, says its boundaries have been unchanged since 1267 and that it has been open to visitors since 1997. Tours take in the vineyards, olive groves and cellars, with olive oil and wine tastings, and there is a restaurant and wine bar. Book ahead, and settle who is driving before the first glass.

## Day 3: To the Alentejo coast

Head south-west across the plains to the coast, where the Alentejo turns wild. Porto Covo and Vila Nova de Milfontes are the classic bases, small white towns above coves and river mouths inside the natural park of the south-west.

This is the start of the [Fishermen's Trail](https://rotavicentina.com/en/fishermens-trail/), the Rota Vicentina's coastal path: 226.5 km in 13 day-stages along the cliff tops. You do not have to walk it all. An afternoon on the stretch from Porto Covo, with the Atlantic booming below, is enough to understand why people come back for the whole thing. Our guide to [walking the Rota Vicentina](/stories/walking-the-rota-vicentina/) has the details on stages, safety and transport.

<Pair a="alg-porto-covo-lighthouse" b="alg-porto-covo-coves" captionA="Rocks, clear water and a small lighthouse on the coast at Porto Covo." captionB="Coves and rock stacks on the Porto Covo coast, seen from the air." />

In Porto Covo, [Hotel Porto Covo](https://hotelportocovo.com/), on Rua Vitalina da Silva, is an aparthotel with studios, apartments and rooms, inside the natural park and close to the sea.

## Day 4: Down the Costa Vicentina to Sagres

Drive south through the park, crossing from the Alentejo into the Algarve at Odeceixe, and on past Aljezur, stopping at whichever beaches tempt you. The landscape is all cliffs, dunes and wind-bent scrub, and the towns stay small.

End at Sagres, at the south-western corner of Europe, and drive out to Cabo de São Vicente for sunset; take a jacket, as the wind out there is relentless. [Memmo Baleeira](https://www.memmohotels.com/baleeira/), a design hotel at Sítio da Baleeira within the Costa Vicentina Natural Park, has sea-view rooms, a spa and an indoor pool.

## Day 5: Lagos

A short drive east brings you to Lagos, the liveliest town in the western Algarve, with a working old town, a harbour and the sculpted cliffs and sea stacks of Ponta da Piedade on the edge of town. Take a boat trip into the grottoes if the sea is calm, or walk the cliff-top paths, keeping well back from the edge.

[Lagosmar Hotel](https://www.lagosmar.com/), a family hotel on Rua Dr. Faria e Silva in the town centre, says the old town, the marina and the railway and bus stations are all a short walk away. That is useful if you want to drop the car here: CP says the [whole Algarve line is now electrified](https://www.cp.pt/info/en/w/improved-service-algarve-line), with direct trains from Lagos to Vila Real de Santo António, calling at Faro and Tavira.

## Days 6–7: Loulé and Tavira

Head east, and stop in Loulé on the way if it is not a Sunday. The [Mercado Municipal de Loulé](https://geoparquealgarvensis.pt/patrimonio-cultura/56-patrimonio-arquitetonico/846-mercado-municipal-de-loule-en), opened in 1908 in a Moorish-revival building, is open Monday to Saturday from 7am to 3pm, according to the Geoparque Algarvensis. Come for the fish, the dried figs and almonds, and the local cheese.

End the week in Tavira, the prettiest town in the eastern Algarve, on the river Gilão with its old bridge, churches and castle. From here, the lagoons and barrier islands of the Ria Formosa are on your doorstep: the park authority ICNF's [guide](https://www.icnf.pt/api/file/doc/f61c223d0d89e467) lists short, flat trails, including the Barril trail on Ilha de Tavira. Spend the last full day on the island beach or out with binoculars, then drive to Faro for your flight.

If you are doing this route between November and February, read our guide to [the Algarve in winter](/stories/algarve-in-winter/), which covers what stays open and the best towns to base yourself in. And for the dishes you will meet along the way, from Alentejo pork to Algarve cataplana, see our guide to [what to eat in Portugal](/stories/what-to-eat-in-portugal/).

## Variations

- **No car?** Swap the coast for the train. Do Évora as a return trip from Lisbon, then take the train south to the Algarve and use the coastal line between Lagos and Tavira. You will miss Monsaraz and the Costa Vicentina, but the rest works.
- **More time?** Add a second night on the Alentejo coast and walk a full stage of the Fishermen's Trail, or a night in Monsaraz to see the village after the day visitors leave.
- **Beach week?** In July and August, flip the balance: one night in Évora, then the rest on the coast, and avoid the inland heat in the middle of the day.

## Before you book

- Book the hire car early for spring and autumn, and ask the company how tolls are charged.
- Reserve winery visits ahead and agree a designated driver; check the limits on [Your Europe](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/driving-abroad/road-rules-and-safety/portugal/index_en.htm).
- Avoid July and August for walking; the Rota Vicentina's [help guide](https://rotavicentina.com/en/help-guide/) explains why.
- Check the [CP timetable](https://www.cp.pt/info/en/w/improved-service-algarve-line) if you plan to drop the car and use the Algarve train.
- Check market days: Loulé's market is closed on Sundays.
