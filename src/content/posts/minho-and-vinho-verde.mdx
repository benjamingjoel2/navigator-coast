North of Porto the country turns green, and it stays green. Maize stands tall in small walled fields, granite farmhouses sit among orchards, and vines climb wherever there is room: up posts, along pergolas over the lanes, sometimes into the trees themselves. Rain comes in from the Atlantic and the whole landscape drinks it. This is the Minho, Portugal's green north-west, and the home of vinho verde.

Many visitors meet the wine first, as a cold, light white on a Lisbon terrace. Meeting the region is better. It is one of the most rewarding corners of Portugal to explore slowly, with river towns, hilltop sanctuaries, Romanesque churches and a wine culture that is relaxed and local.

## What vinho verde actually is

Start with the name, because it confuses everyone. Vinho verde means "green wine", but it is not green. [Wines of Portugal](https://winesofportugal.com/discover/wine-regions/vinho-verde/), the national wine promotion body, explains that the word may refer to the wine's freshness and acidity, which recall unripe fruit, or to the region's lush landscape, which stays green even in winter.

It is a place before it is a style. The regional commission, the CVRVV, notes that the region was [first demarcated on 18 September 1908](https://www.vinhoverde.pt/en/region/history-of-the-region/), making it one of the oldest demarcated regions in Portugal, and that the first Portuguese wines to be exported left from here in the 14th century. Its [designation of origin](https://www.vinhoverde.pt/en/region/designation-of-origin-vinho-verde/) covers still and sparkling wines, white, red and rosé, and nine sub-regions can appear on the label: Monção e Melgaço, Amarante, Ave, Baião, Basto, Cávado, Lima, Paiva and Sousa.

So yes, there is red vinho verde. The CVRVV calls Vinhão [the most widely planted red grape in the region](https://www.vinhoverde.pt/en/grape-varieties/red-wines/), known for its deep, sometimes almost violet colour. Our guide to [what to eat in Portugal](/stories/what-to-eat-in-portugal/) covers the northern dishes it meets at the table.

## Land, rain and granite

According to [Wines of Portugal](https://winesofportugal.com/discover/wine-regions/vinho-verde/), the region runs from the Minho river in the north, down the Atlantic coast to Porto, and south as far as the Vouga. The influence of the ocean is strong: the climate is humid and cool with heavy rainfall, and the soils are mostly granite. The CVRVV adds that the valleys of the [Minho, Lima and Douro](https://www.vinhoverde.pt/en/the-wines/soils-and-climates/) carry sea winds far inland.

That climate explains the look of the vineyards. Wines of Portugal describes traditional training systems such as the *enforcado*, where vines climb trees, and the pergola-style *latada*, alongside the simpler cordon systems that are now more common. Drive the back roads and you will still see vines trained high above vegetable plots and along the edges of fields, a habit of a region where farmland has always been precious.

The main white grapes, again per Wines of Portugal, are Alvarinho, Arinto (locally Pedernã), Avesso, Azal, Loureiro and Trajadura. Two names you will see on labels again and again are **Loureiro** and **Alvarinho**. Alvarinho belongs above all to Monção e Melgaço, which Wines of Portugal calls the most distinctive sub-region: the only one shielded from direct Atlantic influence, producing fuller-bodied wines with more alcohol.

## The towns: where to go

The CVRVV's own [cities and towns route](https://www.vinhoverde.pt/en/routes/cities-and-towns/) is a good starting framework, and it points to a handful of places that together make a fine week.

**Ponte de Lima** is the postcard. The CVRVV highlights its "half Roman-half Medieval" bridge, a Romanesque church and the small roadside shrines called *alminhas*. The river is wide and slow here, and the town gathers itself around the water. It sits in the Lima sub-region, one of the nine named on vinho verde labels.

**Viana do Castelo** combines the Lima river with Atlantic beaches and green hills, per the same route. Praia do Cabedelo, near the river mouth, is one of its busiest beaches and holds a Blue Flag. Above the town, the Santa Luzia sanctuary looks out over the whole estuary. For a stay with that view, the [Pousada Viana do Castelo](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-viana) sits on top of Monte de Santa Luzia; Pousadas de Portugal says it was created in 1979 by restoring the former Santa Luzia Hotel, and looks over the city, the Lima and the Atlantic.

**Monção and Melgaço** are, in the CVRVV's words, small towns on the Spanish border with more than a thousand years of history, sharing the Minho river and a microclimate of their own. This is Alvarinho country. In Monção, the cooperative winery [Adega de Monção](https://adegademoncao.pt/en/enoturism/) offers visits and tastings with a short museum stop, by prior appointment only; its long-running Muralhas de Monção blend combines Alvarinho and Trajadura.

**Guimarães** and **Braga** sit in the south of the region. The CVRVV calls Guimarães a symbol of Portuguese identity, associated with the birth of the nation, with a historic centre on the UNESCO World Heritage list, and describes Braga as one of the oldest cities in Portugal and in the Christian world. In Guimarães, the [Pousada Mosteiro Guimarães](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-guimaraes) occupies a 12th-century Augustinian monastery a short distance from the historic centre.

## Visiting wineries

Vinho verde wine tourism tends to be relaxed and personal. A few principles help:

- **Book ahead.** Many producers are family estates or cooperatives that open by appointment, as Adega de Monção does.
- **Use the route directory.** The CVRVV keeps a list of [wine route adherents](https://www.vinhoverde.pt/en/adherents/), including wineries and wine tourism stays, searchable by place.
- **Mix in a garden or a monastery.** Some of the best visits are as much about place as about wine.

One estate that combines the two is [Quinta da Aveleda](https://aveledavinhoverde.com/enoturismo), in Penafiel, which the [Rota do Românico](https://rotadoromanico.com/en/Experiences/where-to-buy/quinta-da-aveleda) lists for its romantic gardens. Aveleda describes gardens with hundreds of species from around the world and a family history on the land since 1870, with garden and vineyard visits, tastings and lunches on offer.

If you only have a day in Porto, you do not even need to leave the city. The [Casa dos Vinhos Verdes](https://www.vinhoverde.pt/en/experiencias/index.php), in the Palacete Silva Monteiro, has been the CVRVV's headquarters since 1944; the commission runs one-hour guided visits on Saturday afternoons that end with a tasting.

## Getting around

The Minho line makes a lot of this possible without a car. CP's [Minho line timetable](https://www.cp.pt/info/documents/d/cp/comboios-regionais-porto-valenca-minho) runs from Porto São Bento and Campanhã north through Barcelos, Viana do Castelo and Caminha to Valença on the Spanish border, with some services continuing to Vigo. Trains call at both Porto São Bento and Campanhã, and timetables change during the year; find the current PDF on [CP's timetable page](https://cp.pt/info/en/w/trains-timetables-pdf). Our guide to [getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) explains tickets and train types.

For the wineries and smaller villages, though, a car is far easier. And if you are tasting, take that seriously: the EU's [Your Europe guide to Portugal's road rules](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/driving-abroad/road-rules-and-safety/portugal/index_en.htm) sets the blood alcohol limit at 0.5 g/l for standard drivers and 0.2 g/l for novice and professional drivers. Agree a driver, use the spittoon, or book a driver for the day.

## Putting it together

The Minho pairs naturally with Porto and the Douro. A week might run: two days in Porto, perhaps with a morning on our [walk among Porto's azulejos](/stories/porto-azulejos/); two nights in Ponte de Lima or Viana for the coast and the Lima valley; a night in Monção for Alvarinho; and a final night in Guimarães or Braga. Add the mountains of [Peneda-Gerês](/stories/peneda-geres-national-park/) if you like to walk, or head upriver for [three days in the Douro Valley](/stories/three-days-in-the-douro-valley/) to see how differently the same river makes wine further east.

## Before you book

- Check the current Minho line timetable on [CP's timetable page](https://cp.pt/info/en/w/trains-timetables-pdf), including which Porto station your train starts from.
- Book winery visits in advance, and confirm opening days: [Adega de Monção](https://adegademoncao.pt/en/enoturism/), for example, closes on Sundays and public holidays.
- Plan the Casa dos Vinhos Verdes for a Saturday afternoon, and confirm the time on the [CVRVV site](https://www.vinhoverde.pt/en/experiencias/index.php).
- Decide who is driving before the first tasting.
- Pack a waterproof in any season; [Wines of Portugal](https://winesofportugal.com/discover/wine-regions/vinho-verde/) describes the region's climate as humid, with heavy rainfall.
