Stand anywhere in Alcântara and you can hear the bridge before you see it. A low, constant hum drifts down from the deck of the Ponte 25 de Abril, the sound of traffic and trains far overhead, and every so often the rumble of a train crossing below the road. Down at street level, the old warehouses and workshops of Lisbon's industrial west have been turned into shops, studios, restaurants and museums, and the river is never more than a few minutes' walk away.

This is a different Lisbon from the tiled hills of Alfama. It is flatter, newer in spirit, more about brick and steel than azulejos, and it rewards a slow afternoon on foot between the city centre and Belém. Here is how we would spend it.

## The bridge that shapes the district

You cannot write about Alcântara without starting with the bridge. According to [Infraestruturas de Portugal](https://www.infraestruturasdeportugal.pt/index.php/pt-pt/node/8367), which manages it, the Ponte 25 de Abril opened on 6 August 1966, and in 1999 it began carrying trains as well as cars. That makes 2026 its sixtieth year, and IP has marked the anniversary with a programme of events that opened with a drone show over the Tagus.

<Figure id="lxa-bridge-cristo-rei" size="full" caption="The Ponte 25 de Abril from the Alcântara side, with the Cristo Rei statue on the far bank of the Tagus." />

The bridge's red towers and long span frame almost every view in this part of the city. Its southern end lands at Almada, below the Cristo Rei statue, and from the Alcântara waterfront you see both at once: the deck curving away across the river, the statue on its hill beyond.

### Pilar 7: inside the bridge

If you want to understand the structure rather than just look at it, the [Pilar 7 Bridge Experience](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/places/pilar-7-bridge-experience) is built into one of the bridge's pillars on Avenida da Índia. Visit Lisboa describes it as an interactive attraction covering the construction of the bridge, the outside and inside of the pillar, a lift up to a panoramic viewpoint, and a virtual reality section showing parts of the bridge that are normally off limits.

Visit Lisboa lists it as open daily from 10:00 to 18:00, with last entry at 17:00, closed on 25 December, and free with the Lisboa Card. At the time of writing the same page carries a notice that the lift is under repair, so check before you go if the viewpoint is the main reason for your visit.

## LX Factory

A few minutes inland from Pilar 7, tucked right underneath the bridge's approach, is [LX Factory](https://lxfactory.com/). It occupies a former industrial complex on Rua Rodrigues de Faria, and its old factory buildings, with their high ceilings, exposed brick and iron, now house independent shops, design studios, cafés, restaurants and event spaces. Visit Lisboa's listing for [Fábrica XL and Fábrica L](https://www.visitlisboa.com/pt-pt/locais/lx-fabricas), two of the venues inside, notes the industrial features of the halls and a terrace looking out at the bridge.

<Pair a="lxa-factory-street" b="lxa-bridge-lane" captionA="The main street through LX Factory, with café terraces under the bridge's concrete approach." captionB="A cobbled street in Alcântara, with the bridge deck crossing high above the rooftops." />

What makes it work is the setting. You wander between brick walls and loading bays, past street art and painted shutters, and every time you look up, there is the underside of the bridge carrying traffic overhead. It is at its liveliest from late morning into the evening, and it is the kind of place where an hour easily turns into three.

<Figure id="lxa-red-sign" caption="Inside LX Factory: brick sheds, a painted water tower and sticker-covered signs." />

A practical note: LX Factory is a collection of separate businesses rather than a single attraction, so opening hours vary from unit to unit. Most shops keep daytime hours; the restaurants and bars carry on later.

## Getting there and getting around

The riverside west is easy to reach without a car, and the journey is part of the pleasure.

- **By tram.** Carris [route 15E](https://www.carris.pt/viaje/carreiras/15e/) runs from Cais do Sodré out to Algés, with stops in Alcântara on Avenida 24 de Julho, at Santo Amaro station, at Calvário, at Altinho by MAAT and in Belém by the Coach Museum. Calvário is the handiest stop for LX Factory.
- **By train.** The CP urban line from Cais do Sodré to Cascais calls at Alcântara-Mar and Belém. CP's own [account of the Cascais Line](https://www.cp.pt/info/en/w/cascais-line) describes the Tagus filling the view from the window as the train runs west. Check departure times on the current [Lisbon to Cascais timetable](https://www.cp.pt/info/documents/d/cp/comboios-urbanos-lisboa-cascais-1).
- **On foot.** From [Cais do Sodré](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/places/cais-do-sodre), the riverside walk west past Santos and the Alcântara docks is flat and mostly traffic-free, and it continues all the way to Belém.

The [Lisboa Card](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/p/lisboa-card) includes free travel on Lisbon's public transport along with free or discounted entry to many museums and monuments, so it can be worth doing the maths if you plan to pack several of the sights below into one day. For the bigger picture, see our [guide to getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/).

## Alcântara's docks and the Museu do Oriente

Between the railway and the river, Alcântara still feels like a working port, with container cranes and cruise ships on one side and converted dockside warehouses on the other. The Docas de Santo Amaro, a marina lined with restaurants in old riverside sheds, sit almost directly beneath the bridge, and CP's Cascais Line story describes passing them on the way back into the city.

On the Doca de Alcântara, the [Museu do Oriente](https://www.foriente.pt/o-museu) is run by the Fundação Oriente in a large dockside building on Avenida Brasília. According to [Visit Lisboa](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/places/museu-do-oriente), its permanent exhibition has two main parts: a collection on the Portuguese presence in Asia, with more than a thousand artistic and documentary pieces, and the Kwok On Collection, with more than 13,000 objects related to the performing arts from Turkey to Japan. Visit Lisboa gives its hours as 10:00 to 18:00 from Tuesday to Sunday, with late opening until 22:00 on Thursdays and closure on Mondays.

## The Carris Museum at Santo Amaro

If you have ridden a Lisbon tram, the [Museu da Carris](https://museu.carris.pt/en/homepage-museu/) fills in the backstory. Run by Carris, the city's public transport operator, it tells the history of the company with a focus on the electric tram network, and its address is Rua 1.º de Maio, close to the Santo Amaro tram depot. The museum's own site notes that this year marks 125 years of electric traction in Lisbon, with an urban art exhibition that put murals by five artists on the walls of the museum and the depot.

It is a good stop for families and for anyone who has spent the week squeezing onto tram 28. The museum lists its hours as Monday to Saturday, 10:00 to 13:00 and 14:00 to 18:00, closed on Sundays and public holidays.

## MAAT and the walk to Belém

From Alcântara, keep walking west along the river and the architecture changes again. The [MAAT](https://www.maat.pt/en/plan-a-visit), the Museum of Art, Architecture and Technology, sits on the waterfront on Avenida Brasília, and its low, curving building is hard to miss from the riverside path. Its roof is walkable, and from it you get a broad view up and down the Tagus, with the bridge on one side and Belém on the other.

<Figure id="lxa-maat" size="column" caption="The curving roof of MAAT on the Belém waterfront, with walkers on the top and the riverside promenade below." />

MAAT lists its hours as 10:00 to 19:00, Wednesday to Monday, closed on Tuesdays and on 1 January, 1 May and 25 December. It has a café and restaurant in the MAAT Gallery building, and its plan-a-visit page warns that the ticket office may close before the museum does on busy days.

Belém itself is only a short walk further on, with the Jerónimos Monastery, the Tower of Belém and the custard tarts that made the district famous. We cover it properly in our [day in Belém](/stories/belem-in-a-day/) guide. Doing Alcântara in the morning and Belém in the afternoon makes a neat, flat, riverside day, and the 15E tram or the train will bring you back to the centre.

## When to go

Lisbon's riverfront is at its best in the late afternoon, when the light turns warm and the bridge glows against the sky. In summer, the shade of LX Factory's lanes and the breeze off the river are welcome; in winter, the museums give you somewhere dry to go if a shower comes in from the Atlantic. Weekends bring more locals to LX Factory and the docks, which is part of the appeal, but it does mean busier restaurants.

If you are deciding where to base yourself, Alcântara is quieter in the evening than the historic centre. Our guide to [where to stay in Lisbon](/stories/where-to-stay-in-lisbon/) compares the main neighbourhoods, and if you want the older, hillier side of the city for contrast, start with [Alfama, Mouraria and Graça](/stories/lisbon-alfama-mouraria-graca/).

## Before you book

- Check the Pilar 7 page on [Visit Lisboa](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/places/pilar-7-bridge-experience) for the current status of the lift before you go.
- Note the closing days: MAAT closes on Tuesdays, the Museu do Oriente on Mondays, and the Carris Museum on Sundays and public holidays.
- If you are taking the train, check the current [Lisbon to Cascais timetable](https://www.cp.pt/info/documents/d/cp/comboios-urbanos-lisboa-cascais-1).
- Work out whether a [Lisboa Card](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/p/lisboa-card) covers enough of your plans to be worth it.
- Wear comfortable shoes. The walk from Cais do Sodré to Belém is flat, but it is long, and Lisbon's stone pavements are hard on the feet.
