The path is barely a boot wide, the sand is soft enough to swallow your ankles, and to your right the land simply stops. Below, the Atlantic heaves against dark schist, and on a rock stack out in the surf a white stork stands on a nest of sticks as if it were a church tower in the plains. This is the south-west coast of Portugal on foot, and once you have walked it, other coastal paths start to feel a little tame.

The Rota Vicentina is the network of walking trails that makes it possible. According to its [Help Guide](https://rotavicentina.com/en/help-guide/), it adds up to around 750 km of waymarked walking routes across the south-west of Alentejo and the western Algarve: two long-distance "Great Routes" and a set of shorter circular walks. Here is how to choose, when to go and what to know before you lace up.

## Two trails, two characters

The big decision is which of the two Great Routes to walk. They run roughly parallel, one hugging the cliffs and one threading the farmland inland, and they feel like two different countries.

| | Fishermen's Trail | Historical Way |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 226.5 km | 263 km |
| Stages | 13 one-day stages | 13 one-day stages |
| Longest stage | 22.5 km | 25 km (33 km on the Odemira–Sabóia link) |
| Terrain | Narrow single track on cliff tops, 60–70% sand | Rural tracks, cork-oak woods, hills, valleys and rivers |
| Rated | Somewhat difficult | Easy |
| Not for | Anyone with vertigo or fear of heights | — |

Figures are from the official [Fishermen's Trail](https://rotavicentina.com/en/fishermens-trail/) and [Historical Way](https://rotavicentina.com/en/historical-way/) pages.

### The Fishermen's Trail

This is the one on the postcards. It follows the paths that local people have long used to reach beaches and fishing spots, staying close to the cliff edge almost the whole way. You pass through coastal villages such as Porto Covo, [Vila Nova de Milfontes](/stories/vila-nova-de-milfontes/), Almograve, Zambujeira do Mar and Odeceixe, dropping into river mouths and climbing back onto the plateau. It is spectacular, exposed and, above all, sandy.

<Figure id="rv-porto-covo-trail-start" caption="The Fishermen's Trail leaves Porto Covo on a fenced sandy path across the cliff-top scrub." />

### The Historical Way

The Historical Way follows rural roads and tracks through the towns and villages of the interior: Santiago do Cacém, Cercal do Alentejo, São Luís, Odemira, São Teotónio, Aljezur and on towards the Algarve. Expect cork oaks, whitewashed hamlets, storks on chimney pots, river valleys and long, quiet views. It is gentler underfoot, and the official guide suggests it as the alternative for anyone uneasy about heights.

Many walkers mix the two, using the Historical Way for inland stages and dropping to the coast on others. The routes are designed to be combined.

## Which direction?

Both routes are signed in both directions, and the [Help Guide](https://rotavicentina.com/en/help-guide/) is clear that north-to-south is only indicative. Walking south gives you the Alentejo first and the Algarve's cliffs and the [Cabo de São Vicente](/stories/sagres-and-cape-st-vincent/) as a finale. Reversing it is just as valid if you arrive through Faro.

## Walking in sand

Do not underestimate it. The Help Guide describes around 70% of the Fishermen's Trail as deep sand on a narrow path, and that changes the arithmetic of a day. A 20 km stage in loose sand feels much longer than 20 km on a track, so build in time and keep your expectations of pace modest.

<Figure id="rv-creleizao-panorama" size="full" caption="Sand underfoot and the Atlantic alongside: the trail near Creleizão beach, Zambujeira do Mar." />

A few things help:

- Boots or trail shoes that are already broken in, and gaiters if sand in your socks drives you mad.
- Walking poles, which the guide recommends for steep climbs and descents.
- At least 1.5 litres of water, a hat and sunscreen; not every stage passes a café or shop.
- Starting early, so the hottest part of the day falls near the end of the stage.

## When to go

The trails are designed to be walked [between September and June](https://rotavicentina.com/en/help-guide/). The official advice is to avoid July and August: temperatures can rise above 30°C, the beaches are full, accommodation often asks for minimum stays and prices rise.

- **Spring** (from March) is, in the guide's words, one of the best times of the year: sunny days mixed with showers and a landscape full of colour and scent.
- **Autumn** is usually mild, with warm sea temperatures and much less wind, and the region calms down after the summer crowds.
- **Winter** is the wettest season, though daytime temperatures rarely drop below 11°C. Bring waterproofs and proper boots.

Whatever the month, check the forecast close to your start date on [IPMA](https://www.ipma.pt/en/), Portugal's weather and sea service, and look at the Rota Vicentina [warning map](https://blog.rotavicentina.com/en/warning-map-of-the-rota-vicentina-trails/) for alerts. After heavy rain it flags flooded sections, fallen trees and unstable slopes, and it also carries [wildfire risk](/stories/staying-safe-in-portugal/) notices in summer.

## A walk through a natural park

Most of the coastal route lies inside the Southwest Alentejo and Vicentine Coast Natural Park. [ICNF](https://www.icnf.pt/conservacao/rnapareasprotegidas/parquesnaturais/pnsudoestealentejanoecostavicentina), Portugal's nature conservation institute, describes it as running from São Torpes, south of Sines, to Burgau on the Algarve's south coast, including a 2 km-wide marine strip, across the municipalities of Sines, Odemira, Aljezur and Vila do Bispo. The sea cliffs are the park's symbol.

### The cliff-nesting storks

Look out to sea as you walk the Alentejo cliffs. White storks usually nest on trees, old buildings or electricity pylons, but here, ICNF explains, they build on sea cliffs and rock stacks, a situation it calls unique in the world. Give the nests a wide berth, keep noise down and never leave the path for a closer photo.

### The rules on the trail

The Fishermen's Trail page and the Help Guide set out what the park's fragile ecosystem needs:

- **On foot only.** Bikes are not allowed on the coastal pedestrian trails, and the trail is not suited to trail running.
- **Stay on the marked path.** Wandering off tramples dunes and protected vegetation.
- **No wild camping and no fires.** Use campsites. ICNF and the Maritime Police [carry out joint inspections](https://www.icnf.pt/imprensa/campismoilegalnoparquenaturaldosudoestealentejanoecostavicentina) for illegal camping and motorhome stays in the park.
- **Groups of no more than 15 people.**
- **No vehicles in the dunes.**
- **Dogs on a lead**, which is tricky on the narrow coastal path, and always clean up after them.
- **Respect private land.** Many sections cross farms with the owners' permission, for walkers only. Close gates behind you.

## Cliff safety

The cliffs are constantly eroding, and the official guidance is plain: the Fishermen's Trail is not recommended for anyone with vertigo or fear of heights, and children need close supervision. Keep well back from edges, especially in wind or after rain, when the ground can give way. The guide also recommends not walking alone and telling your accommodation which stage you are doing. In an emergency, call 112.

Ticks are common in grazing country from spring to late autumn, so check yourself at the end of each day.

## Following the waymarks

The marking is clear and the [signposting page](https://rotavicentina.com/en/signposting/) shows every sign. Each trail has its own colour code, painted as two short horizontal bars:

- **Historical Way:** white over red.
- **Fishermen's Trail:** green over blue.

Two parallel bars mean you are on the right path, crossed bars mean wrong way, and an angled bar or arrow shows a turn to the left or right. On the Fishermen's Trail the four signs are labelled follow, do not follow, on your left and on your right. Where the routes meet, check which colour you are following.

## Getting to the start

The region's own [travel guide](https://rotavicentina.com/en/the-region/) points out that the Historical Way begins in Santiago do Cacém and the Fishermen's Trail in Porto Covo.

- **By bus from Lisbon.** [National express coaches](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) run by Rede Expressos leave from Sete Rios in Lisbon for the main towns of the region, including Santiago do Cacém and Porto Covo. This is the most direct option.
- **By train.** Trains from Lisbon stop at Ermidas-Sado, which is about 30 km from Santiago do Cacém, so you will still need a taxi or a transfer to reach the trail.
- **By air.** [Lisbon airport](/stories/arriving-at-lisbon-airport/) suits the northern end; Faro suits the southern end.

The [Help Guide](https://rotavicentina.com/en/help-guide/) warns that local public transport does not meet most walkers' needs. Bus timetables are often published only 14 days ahead, tickets are sometimes sold in cafés or shops rather than on board, and some must be bought the day before.

## Getting home from the south-west corner

Both routes run down to the far south-west, and walks continue past Cabo de São Vicente along the south coast through Sagres and Salema to [Lagos](/stories/lagos-guide/). The Help Guide describes the stretches between Lagos and Cabo de São Vicente as the most demanding in the network.

From Lagos, the [region guide](https://rotavicentina.com/en/the-region/) lists Rede Expressos national buses and regional VAMUS Algarve services to Sagres, Vila do Bispo, Cabo de São Vicente, Aljezur and Odeceixe, plus trains from Lagos to Faro. Some routes run on weekdays only, so check before your final stage.

## Sleeping and transfers

Accommodation along the way is mostly small and local: guesthouses, rural houses, village rooms and campsites near the coast. The Rota Vicentina [accommodation listing](https://rotavicentina.com/en/accommodations/) includes over 100 partner properties, and the organisers recommend booking well ahead.

You do not have to carry everything. Many places to stay offer luggage and passenger transfers between stages, which must be requested when you book, and the [transport partners](https://rotavicentina.com/en/transports/) list includes taxis based in Porto Covo, Santiago do Cacém and Sagres. Contact them directly for current rates.

### A few places along the way

These are established places in or near the stage towns, from a simple bed to something more comfortable. All except the Almograve hostel appear in the Rota Vicentina accommodation listing; check each one's own site for the season and whether it offers luggage transfers.

- **Porto Covo.** [Hotel Porto Covo](https://hotelportocovo.com/), on Rua Vitalina da Silva, is an aparthotel with studios, one-bedroom apartments and double and twin rooms, inside the natural park and close to the start of the Fishermen's Trail.
- **Vila Nova de Milfontes.** [Orbitur Sitava Milfontes](https://www.orbitur.pt/en/campsites/alentejo/orbitur-sitava-milfontes) is a 30-hectare campsite at Brejo da Zimbreira, about 600 m from the beach, with pitches for tents and rental units.
- **Aljezur.** [Vicentina Hotel](https://www.vicentinahotel.com/), on Avenida General Humberto Delgado, is a 26-room four-star hotel with rooms, studios and apartments, and describes itself as a Rota Vicentina partner.
- **Pedralva.** [Aldeia da Pedralva](https://www.aldeiadapedralva.com/en/), in the municipality of Vila do Bispo, is a hamlet whose ruined houses were restored as holiday cottages after 2006. It has its own restaurant and rents bikes.
- **Almograve.** For walkers on a budget, [HI Sudoeste Almograve](https://www.pousadasjuventude.pt/en/hostels/pousada-do-sudoeste-almograve/) is part of Portugal's official youth hostel network, Pousadas de Juventude.
- **Sagres.** At the end of the coast, [Memmo Baleeira](https://www.memmohotels.com/baleeira/) is a design hotel at Sítio da Baleeira, within the natural park, with sea-view rooms and a spa.

## Before you book

- Decide on the Fishermen's Trail, the Historical Way or a combination, and download the GPX tracks from the official trail pages.
- Avoid July and August; aim for spring or autumn.
- Book accommodation first, and ask for luggage transfers at the same time.
- Check bus timetables to Santiago do Cacém or Porto Covo, and back from Lagos, within 14 days of travel.
- Look at the warning map and IPMA forecast the day before each stage.
- Pack for sand, sun and sudden showers, and carry enough water for the full stage.
