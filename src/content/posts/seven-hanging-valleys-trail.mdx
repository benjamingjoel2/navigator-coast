The path is pale and dusty, the colour of biscuit, and for most of the way it keeps a respectful distance from the edge. Then the scrub opens, the ground simply stops, and you are looking down a sheer wall of honey-coloured rock into water so clear you can see the shadows of boulders on the sand below. A moment later the path turns inland again, dips into a little gully and climbs back out. That rhythm, edge and gully, edge and gully, is the whole story of the Seven Hanging Valleys.

This is the walk that turns up on every list of Algarve highlights, and for once the reputation is earned. It is short, it needs no special kit, and in a morning it shows you the limestone coast at its most theatrical: arches, sea stacks, sinkholes and hidden coves. Go at the right time of day and year, and you will understand why.

## What the name means

The trail runs along the coast of the municipality of Lagoa, between Carvoeiro and Armação de Pêra. The council describes the [Percurso dos 7 Vales Suspensos](https://www.cm-lagoa.pt/conhecer/percursos/sete-vales-suspensos) as a 5.7 km walking route linking Praia de Vale Centeanes with Praia da Marinha. It follows an almost unbroken line of limestone cliffs, interrupted only by small watercourses that mostly reach the sea above sea level. Those streams have cut valleys that end in mid-air, hanging over the shore rather than running down to it, and they give the route its name.

The same page notes that this landscape took more than 20 million years to form, and that the cliff tops carry juniper scrub, while seabirds nest in the rock faces and bats use the caves.

<Figure id="svs-cliff-path" caption="The trail on the cliff top, with sea stacks breaking the line of the coast ahead." />

Lagoa's tourism site goes further, saying the route has been [distinguished as the best walking route in Europe](https://welcometolagoa.pt/288/percurso-dos-7-vales-suspensos). Whatever you make of awards, it is certainly one of the most rewarding few kilometres of coast in Portugal.

## The route at a glance

According to Lagoa's [trail sheet](https://welcometolagoa.pt/288/percurso-dos-7-vales-suspensos), this is a linear Pequena Rota (short route) of medium difficulty. Walked there and back it comes to 11.2 km. The highest point, 45.5 m, is at Vale Centeanes, and the lowest, 7.5 m, is down at Praia de Benagil, which tells you something about the walk: a fair amount of up and down, with one proper descent into the village and a climb out the other side.

Most people walk it in a morning, or in a relaxed half-day with stops. Allow more if you plan to swim, and remember that the return leg is the same distance again unless you have arranged transport.

## Walking it, west to east

**Vale Centeanes.** Lagoa's directions put the start about 2 km east of the lower part of Carvoeiro by road. The beach sits in a deep notch in the cliffs and the path heads east from the top. Almost immediately the views open up, and you will pass the white tower of the Alfanzina lighthouse on the headland.

**Praia do Carvalho.** The first of the classic coves. From above it looks enclosed, a crescent of sand walled by rock, and the cliffs here are pocked with holes and tunnels carved by water. Take the side paths to the viewpoints slowly; this is where the ground is most undercut.

<Figure id="svs-carvalho-cove" size="column" caption="A sheer wall of layered limestone dropping into the sea on the stretch near Praia do Carvalho." />

**Benagil.** The path drops into the small fishing village of Benagil, with its whitewashed cottages and a slipway onto the beach. This is the busiest point on the walk, because the famous sea cave, the Algar de Benagil, sits just along the coast and boats leave from here. It is a good place for a coffee before the climb back up to the cliff tops.

<Pair a="svs-waymark" b="svs-benagil-street" captionA="Yellow and red paint marks on the rock, the standard for a short route." captionB="Low whitewashed houses with coloured trim in Benagil." />

**Praia da Marinha.** The final stretch is perhaps the finest, with sea stacks standing offshore and the coastline broken into arches. Praia da Marinha is the eastern end of the route. Lagoa's directions reach it from the EN125, turning south near the Escola Internacional do Algarve and following the beach signs, which is useful if someone is collecting you.

<Figure id="svs-marinha-shore" caption="Ochre stacks and fallen blocks at the water's edge on Praia da Marinha." />

Some walkers start at Marinha and head west instead. It makes little difference to the scenery, though starting at the end with the better road access can make a pickup easier to arrange.

## About the Benagil cave

You cannot walk into the Benagil cave from the trail, and you can no longer swim in either. Since 2024 the Portimão harbour authority has set rules for the area, and the [Autoridade Marítima Nacional's summary](https://www.amn.pt/Media/Paginas/DetalheNoticia.aspx?nid=5827) lists the main ones: access to the caves by swimming or on floats is banned, landing on or using the sand inside the Algar de Benagil is banned for individuals and companies alike, kayaks may not be rented without a guide in the cave area, and guided groups need one guide kayak for every six kayaks of visitors.

In practice that means seeing the cave from the water on an organised boat or guided kayak trip from Benagil or a nearby beach. Check that any operator you book is working within these rules; our guide to [visiting the Benagil sea cave](/stories/benagil-sea-caves/) goes through the options in more detail.

## When to go

Lagoa recommends [spring and autumn](https://welcometolagoa.pt/288/percurso-dos-7-vales-suspensos) and advises avoiding very hot, rainy or windy days. We would add: go early. There is almost no shade on the cliff tops, and by late morning in summer the path is busy and the light is flat. In the cooler months the trail is far quieter, which is one reason we rate it so highly in our guide to [the Algarve in winter](/stories/algarve-in-winter/).

Check the forecast on [IPMA](https://www.ipma.pt/en/), Portugal's weather service, the evening before, and look for wind as well as rain.

## Cliff safety

This is the part people skip, and should not. The cliffs are soft limestone, they are constantly eroding, and the edges are often undercut. The UK government's [travel advice for Portugal](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/portugal/safety-and-security) warns that falling rocks are a hazard, particularly in the Algarve, and that the authorities can fine people who ignore signs about cliff erosion.

A few rules we follow:

- Stay on the main path and obey every barrier and warning sign.
- Never stand right at the edge for a photograph; the overhang beneath you is invisible.
- On the beaches, do not sit or sunbathe directly under the cliffs.
- Keep children close and dogs on a lead.
- Avoid the walk in strong wind or straight after heavy rain.

Lagoa's own advice is simple and worth repeating: use the existing paths and avoid shortcuts, do not pick plants, take your litter away, wear comfortable shoes suited to the season, and bring water.

## Getting there and back

The trail is easiest with a car, a taxi or a willing friend. Because it is linear, the simplest plan is to be dropped at one end and collected at the other, or to walk out and back. Benagil, roughly in the middle, gives you the option of a shorter half.

Regional buses in the Algarve are run by [Vamus Algarve](https://vamusalgarve.pt/). Services out to the beaches can be sparse, so check current routes and timetables on the operator's site before relying on them. There is no railway on this part of the coast; our [guide to getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) explains how trains and buses fit together in the Algarve and beyond.

If you are staying in [Lagos](/stories/lagos-guide/) or elsewhere in the western Algarve, this makes an easy day out by car, and you could pair it with an afternoon in Carvoeiro or Ferragudo.

## Two shorter walks nearby

If you have time left, Lagoa has two more coastal routes worth knowing.

- **Caminho do Algar Seco.** A [boardwalk of about 600 m](https://welcometolagoa.pt/289/caminho-do-algar-seco) through a maze of ochre rock shaped by rain and sea, just outside Carvoeiro. It is short enough for an evening stroll.
- **Caminho dos Promontórios.** West of Carvoeiro, this [6 km linear route](https://welcometolagoa.pt/290/caminho-dos-promontorios) runs from Praia do Molhe, near the mouth of the Arade river, to Praia do Paraíso, past headlands and small enclosed coves, with interpretive panels on geology and coastal life. Lagoa gives about six hours for the round trip.

If the Seven Hanging Valleys gives you a taste for cliff walking, the Atlantic coast further west is the next step: our guide to [walking the Rota Vicentina](/stories/walking-the-rota-vicentina/) covers the multi-day Fishermen's Trail.

## Before you book

- Decide whether you will walk one way or return, and arrange transport for the end point if needed.
- Check the [IPMA forecast](https://www.ipma.pt/en/) the night before; avoid very hot, wet or windy days, as Lagoa advises.
- Start early in summer and carry more water than you think you need. There is little shade.
- Wear proper walking shoes; the surface is loose and stony in places.
- If you want to see inside the Benagil cave, book a licensed boat or guided kayak trip and read the [current rules](https://www.amn.pt/Media/Paginas/DetalheNoticia.aspx?nid=5827) first.
- If you are relying on buses, check timetables on [Vamus Algarve](https://vamusalgarve.pt/) before you set out.
