Funchal is best understood from the water. From the harbour wall the city rises in one long sweep: white houses and orange roofs stacked up the slopes, church towers, the dark green of gardens, and above it all the mountains, often wearing a collar of cloud by late morning. It is a steep, sunny, slightly theatrical place, and it rewards anyone willing to climb.

Most visitors to Madeira pass through Funchal on the way to the levadas and the peaks. We think it deserves more than a night. The city has a real market, an old town with a seafront fort, a cathedral with one of the finest wooden ceilings in Portugal, and some of the best gardens in Europe, all within a cable-car ride of each other.

## The lie of the land

[Visit Madeira](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/madeira/south-coast/funchal/) describes Funchal as a natural amphitheatre facing the Atlantic. It was raised to city status in 1508 by King Manuel I, and it takes its name from the wild fennel, *funcho*, that grew here when the first settlers arrived. Sugar and later wine made it a trading port in the middle of the ocean, and both still appear on the city's coat of arms.

The practical consequence of the amphitheatre is that everything interesting is either along the seafront or up a hill. The flat strip by the harbour, with Avenida Arriaga, the cathedral and the market, is easy walking. The parishes higher up, including Monte at around 500 metres, are where the views are, and where you will want a cable car, a bus or a taxi rather than your legs.

Getting here is straightforward. According to Visit Madeira's [arrival guide](https://visitmadeira.com/en/travel-info/how-to-get-here/), the island's international airport is in Santa Cruz, east of the city, and the flight from Lisbon takes about 90 minutes. If you are coming from the mainland, our [guide to getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) covers the connections.

## The old town and the seafront

Start in the Zona Velha, the old town, at the eastern end of the seafront. Its narrow lanes are lined with restaurants and small bars, and the area wakes up properly in the evening. At its edge, the [Fortress of São Tiago](https://visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/culture-passionates/heritage/monuments-and-architecture/fortress-of-sao-tiago/) sits above the water. Visit Madeira dates it to the early 17th century, built to defend the city from corsairs, and notes that over the years it has served as a barracks and a shelter after the 1803 flood.

Walk west along the front and you reach the [Mercado dos Lavradores](https://visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/culture-passionates/heritage/monuments-and-architecture/mercado-dos-lavradores/), the farmers' market. It was inaugurated on 24 November 1940 in a building by Edmundo Tavares, and its façade, main door and fish hall carry large tile panels of regional scenes. Go in the morning, when the fruit stalls are at their fullest and the fish hall is at its busiest. If you want to know what to order once you have seen it raw, our [guide to what to eat in Portugal](/stories/what-to-eat-in-portugal/) has a section on Madeira.

A few minutes further on is the [Sé, Funchal's cathedral](https://visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/culture-passionates/heritage/monuments-and-architecture/funchal-cathedral/). Visit Madeira says it was ordered by King João II in 1493 and completed in the early 16th century, and that it has been a National Monument since 1910. Look up once you are inside: the ceiling is carved from the island's own cedar in the Mudéjar style. The [cathedral parish](https://www.catedraldofunchal.com/) asks visitors not to sightsee during Mass and adoration, so check the service times before you go.

## Up to Monte

The [Funchal Cable Car](https://visitmadeira.com/en/means-of-transportation/funchal-cable-car/) leaves from the old town and climbs to Monte in about 15 minutes, rising some 560 metres over a 3,200-metre cable. It replaced a journey once made by steam train, and from the cabin you look down on terraces, swimming pools and banana patches before the city drops away. Tickets are sold at the terminals and on the [Madeira Cable Car website](https://madeiracablecar.com/). Check the operating hours on the day: it can close in bad weather.

<Pair a="fnc-cable-car" b="fnc-hillside-houses" captionA="Cable car cabins climbing from the old town towards Monte." captionB="White houses stacked up a steep hillside on Madeira's south coast." />

Monte has three good reasons to linger. The first is the [Monte Palace Madeira Tropical Garden](https://visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/culture-passionates/gardens/monte-palace-madeira-garden/), which Visit Madeira describes as covering 70,000 square metres, with centenary cycads, lakes, Asian stonework and a collection of tiles spanning the 15th to the 20th centuries. The second is the church of Nossa Senhora do Monte, which the [cable car company](https://madeiracablecar.com/) notes holds the tomb of Charles I, the last Austro-Hungarian emperor, who went into exile on Madeira and is buried there.

The third is the ride down. The [Monte sledges](https://visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/experiencing-the-city-of-funchal/touring-the-city/monte-sledges/) date from the 19th century, when residents used them to get down to the city in a hurry. They are wicker baskets on wooden runners, steered by two *carreiros* in white with straw hats, who brake with the thick rubber soles of their boots. The run is about two kilometres and ends at Livramento, partway down. [Carreiros do Monte](https://www.carreirosdomonte.com/), the drivers' organisation, says it sells tickets on site for the same day only, does not take online bookings, and may close early in bad weather. It does not run on Sundays.

If you would rather continue by cable, a second line, the [Botanical Garden Cable Car](https://visitmadeira.com/en/means-of-transportation/botanical-garden-cable-car/), crosses the valley of the Ribeira de João Gomes from Largo das Babosas in Monte to the botanical garden. Combined tickets for both lines and the garden are available.

## Gardens and wine

The [Madeira Botanical Garden](https://visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/culture-passionates/gardens/madeira-botanical-garden/) opened in 1960 in the grounds of Quinta do Bom Sucesso, an estate established by the Reid family in 1881. It covers about eight hectares and holds more than 2,000 exotic plants. Its terraces look straight down onto the city, which is half the point. If you have a spring trip in mind, our piece on [the best time to visit Portugal](/stories/best-time-to-visit-portugal/) explains why April and May are lovely here.

Back in the centre, [Blandy's Wine Lodge](https://blandyswinelodge.com/en/home/) on Avenida Arriaga is the historic home of the Blandy family's Madeira wine business, which it says has been run by seven generations. It offers guided tours of the ageing rooms and a range of tastings. It is the easiest place in town to understand why a fortified wine, cooked and aged in the warmth, can last for well over a century.

## Festivals through the year

Funchal keeps a busy calendar. [Visit Madeira](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/madeira/south-coast/funchal/) lists Carnival, with its parade through the city centre; the Flower Festival in spring, with flower carpets and a parade; the Atlantic Festival, running through June with concerts and fireworks by the sea; and the Wine Festival around the harvest, between late August and early September.

The biggest night is New Year's Eve, when fireworks over the bay mark the end of a long Christmas season of lights and markets. Visit Madeira notes the display has been listed by Guinness World Records. Accommodation books up very early for it.

## Weather and the sea

The south coast is the sunny side of the island. [Visit Madeira](https://visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/experiencing-the-city-of-funchal/exploring-the-city/funchal-streets/) gives Funchal average temperatures of about 18°C in winter and 25°C in summer, and puts the sea at 18°C to 24°C through the year. Visit Madeira's [weather page](https://visitmadeira.com/en/travel-info/useful-information/weather-in-madeira/) adds that the mountains can be in fog while the coast is sunny, so the hills behind the city may be grey while the seafront basks. Our comparison of [Azores or Madeira](/stories/azores-or-madeira/) goes into the island's microclimates in more detail.

Funchal is not a sandy-beach city. Most sea swimming here is from lidos, rocky platforms and pebble shores along the seafront; for a long stretch of sand, take the ferry to [Porto Santo](/stories/porto-santo/).

## Where to stay

The centre, between the market and the marina, suits a short stay: everything is walkable, and the cable car and buses start nearby. The old town is livelier at night. The hotel zone west of the centre, along Estrada Monumental, is quieter and closer to the sea pools, with a walk or bus ride into town.

At the top of that range is [Reid's Palace](https://www.belmond.com/en/hotels/europe/portugal/reids-palace-madeira), on its clifftop above the ocean. Belmond says it was opened by the Reid family in 1891, and its subtropical gardens and traditional afternoon tea are part of the island's history in their own right. For anything more modest, the official [Visit Madeira accommodation directory](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-stay/accommodations/) is a sensible place to start.

A car is useful for exploring the rest of the island, but in Funchal itself it is mostly a parking problem. The city bus company, [Horários do Funchal](https://www.horariosdofunchal.pt/), runs urban routes up into the hillside parishes and publishes its timetables and service notices online.

## Before you book

- Look at the current hours for the [cable car](https://madeiracablecar.com/) and allow for weather closures.
- Plan the Monte sledges for a weekday or Saturday morning, and remember tickets are sold [on site, for the same day](https://www.carreirosdomonte.com/).
- Book well ahead if you want to be in Funchal for New Year's Eve, the Flower Festival or Carnival.
- Check service times before visiting the [cathedral](https://www.catedraldofunchal.com/).
- If you plan to walk in the mountains during your stay, pack layers: the [weather up high](https://visitmadeira.com/en/travel-info/useful-information/weather-in-madeira/) can be very different from the seafront.
