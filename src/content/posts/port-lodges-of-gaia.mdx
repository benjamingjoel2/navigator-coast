Stand on the Ribeira quay in Porto and look across the water. The opposite bank climbs steeply in tiers of terracotta roofs, and painted across them, in letters big enough to read from the other side of the Douro, are names that wine drinkers know from labels: Taylor's, Graham's, Sandeman, Cálem. That bank is Vila Nova de Gaia, a separate city from Porto, and those roofs cover the lodges where port is aged, blended and, these days, poured for visitors.

An afternoon in Gaia is one of the classic pleasures of a Porto trip. It is also easy to do badly: wander in without a plan, taste too much too fast, and leave remembering only the climb back up the hill. Here is how we would spend it.

## Why the lodges are worth the crossing

Visit Porto's own [city-quarters plan](https://visitporto.travel/storage/01HZER0RKPNVVFD34JJNNK2ZA4.pdf) treats the historic centres of Porto and Gaia as a single quarter, and describes Gaia's historic centre as home to the Port wine cellars. The vineyards themselves are far upriver in the Douro Valley, a train ride and a different world away (our [three days in the Douro Valley](/stories/three-days-in-the-douro-valley/) covers that trip). Gaia is where much of the finished wine waits in cask, and where the big houses have opened their doors.

<Figure id="gaia-lodge-signs" caption="Port house names on the hillside of Vila Nova de Gaia, above the riverside road." />

What you get inside is a guided or self-guided walk through cool, dim cellars lined with casks and vats, some history of the house, and a tasting at the end. The format varies from lodge to lodge, which is the main reason to choose carefully rather than walk into the first open door.

## Getting across the river

The simplest way to arrive is on foot, across the lower deck of the Dom Luís I bridge from the Ribeira. It takes a few minutes and puts you straight on the Gaia quay, with the lodges a short walk uphill.

The [Gaia Cable Car](https://gaiacablecar.com/pt/experiencia/) is the other way to arrive, or to leave. Its lower station is at Cais de Gaia, next to the Mercado Municipal, and its upper station is at Jardim do Morro, on Avenida da República. The route is 600 metres long and takes about five minutes, with views over the river, Porto's historic centre and the rooftops of the wine cellars. Its [official site](https://gaiacablecar.com/) lists daily hours of 10:00 to 18:00 from late October to the end of December; check it for other months. Riding up at the end of the day saves your legs and gives you the view as a reward.

Before you cross, consider an hour at the IVDP's [Port and Douro Interactive Learning Centre](https://www.ivdp.pt/en/tourism/port-and-douro-interactive-learning-centre/), on Rua Ferreira Borges in Porto's historic centre. Run by the Instituto dos Vinhos do Douro e do Porto, the centre pairs a multimedia exhibition with a look at the institute's laboratories and a tasting at its Port Wine Bar, where a card lets you choose from eight ports. It is a neutral introduction before you meet the brands.

## Port in five minutes

A little vocabulary makes every tasting more rewarding. The IVDP's [introduction to Port](https://www.ivdp.pt/en/wines/port-wines/introduction/) describes it as a fortified wine with an alcohol content usually between 19 and 22 per cent, and groups it into a handful of styles.

| Style | What the IVDP says |
|---|---|
| Ruby, Reserve, LBV and Vintage | Kept young and fruity; Vintage and LBV age well in bottle |
| Tawny, Reserve, 10 to 40 years old, Colheita | Aged in casks or vats; Colheita comes from a single year |
| White | From extra dry to very sweet, gaining a golden colour with long cask ageing |
| Rosé | Briefly macerated, meant to be drunk young and chilled |

The useful distinction at the tasting table is between the ruby family, which keeps its deep colour and fresh fruit, and the tawnies, which spend years in wood and turn amber and nutty. Most lodge tastings put one of each in front of you, so you can taste the difference for yourself.

## The lodges

There are many houses in Gaia; these five publish clear visitor information on their own sites, which makes them easy to plan around. Book ahead in every case, and check the current formats, times and prices on the official pages.

**[Taylor's](https://www.taylor.pt/en/visit-taylors/port-cellars)**, which dates itself to 1692, is on Rua do Choupelo, a stiff walk up from the river. Its cellar visit is self-guided: you scan a QR code and follow an audio commentary on your phone, available in 13 languages, so bring your own headphones. Three wines are included, a Chip Dry white, a Late Bottled Vintage and a 10-year-old tawny, and the cellar opens daily from 10:00 to 18:15.

**[Graham's](https://www.grahams-port.com/visit-us)** has aged its wines in its Gaia lodge since 1890. Visits are guided by a port expert and end with a tasting, and the house's Vinum restaurant sits next to the lodge with views over the Douro.

**[Sandeman](https://www.sandeman.com/port-wine/visit/cellars-porto/)** is at Largo Miguel Bombarda, close to the quay. The house says port has aged in the building since 1811, and its shortest tour is led through the vats and casks by "The Don", the caped figure from its labels. Longer visits add more wines, old tawnies or chocolate.

**[Ferreira](https://sogrape.com/en/tourism/caves-ferreira)**, on Avenida Ramos Pinto, tells its story from 1751 through the life of Dona Antónia Adelaide Ferreira. The cellars are on the riverfront in Gaia's historic centre, in a building whose features date back to the early 18th century. Visits run from a 50-minute tour with three ports to longer tastings that include a Vintage.

**[Cálem](https://tour.calem.pt/)**, which dates itself to 1859, is on Avenida Diogo Leite, along the river. Its standard visit combines a guided tour with an interactive museum about port and the Douro, and one option adds a fado performance in the cellars.

## How to plan the afternoon

**Two lodges is plenty.** Most visits end with several pours, and port is strong. One lodge with a self-guided format and one with a guide gives you contrast; adding a third usually blurs the memory of the first.

**Mix high and low.** Some houses sit right on the riverfront and others are a steep climb up narrow, cobbled lanes. Start at the top and work downhill if you can, or use the cable car to save the climb.

**Book a time slot.** Popular tours fill up, especially at weekends and in summer. Every house listed above takes bookings through its own site or booking page.

**Eat something.** A plate of cheese, cured ham or salted almonds at a riverside table between visits does wonders. Our guide to [what to eat in Portugal](/stories/what-to-eat-in-portugal/) covers the snacks and petiscos worth ordering.

**Do not drive.** The EU's [Your Europe guide to Portugal's road rules](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/driving-abroad/road-rules-and-safety/portugal/index_en.htm) gives the blood alcohol limit as 0.5 g/l for most drivers and 0.2 g/l for novice and professional drivers. A Gaia afternoon is a walking afternoon.

## The view from the top

When the tastings are done, climb, or ride the cable car, up to Jardim do Morro. From up here the whole of the Ribeira is laid out across the water: the stacked houses, the cathedral on its hill, the boats on the quay below. Late in the day, as the sun drops downriver towards the Atlantic, the Porto bank glows and the lights begin to come on.

<Figure id="gaia-rabelo-barrels" caption="A rabelo boat loaded with barrels on the Douro, with the Ribeira behind." />

If you still have the energy, walk across the bridge's upper deck back to Porto, high above the river, with the Gaia rooftops and their painted names behind you. It is the perfect full stop to the day, and a fitting way to end the first day of our [Porto in 48 hours](/stories/porto-in-48-hours/) plan. If you are still choosing a base for your trip, our comparison of [Lisbon or Porto](/stories/lisbon-or-porto/) may help.

## Before you book

- Book lodge visits in advance, especially for weekends, and confirm the format: guided, self-guided or audio.
- Check opening hours on each house's official page; some close earlier in winter or on holidays.
- Check the [Gaia Cable Car](https://gaiacablecar.com/) hours for the month you are travelling.
- Wear shoes with grip. The lanes between the lodges are steep and the cobbles can be slippery after rain.
- Plan on two lodges, not four, and leave room for dinner.
- If you are carrying bottles home, check your airline's rules for liquids in hand luggage, and pack them in checked bags if in doubt.
