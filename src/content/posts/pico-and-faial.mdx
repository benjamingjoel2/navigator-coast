From the harbour wall in Horta, Pico fills the sky. The mountain rises straight out of the channel, a near-perfect cone, often wearing a single cap of cloud, and it seems far too big for the island beneath it. Turn around and you are in a small, colourful port town where the quay walls are covered in paintings left by sailors who stopped here on their way across the Atlantic.

That pairing is the whole appeal. The tourism board notes that Pico is just [6 km from Faial](https://mi.visitazores.com/en/node/14), close enough to see across, close enough for a short ferry ride, and yet the two islands feel completely different. Faial is green, softly hilly and outward-looking. Pico is grey lava, stone walls and one enormous volcano.

## Getting between them

The two islands are linked by sea. [Atlânticoline's timetable](https://www.atlanticoline.pt/horarios/) shows crossings between Horta on Faial and Madalena on Pico, and the short hop makes it easy to stay on one island and visit the other for the day, or to split your nights between them. Sailings vary through the year and bad weather can cancel them, so check the timetable for your dates and keep a little slack around any flight.

Our [guide to getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) covers the island flights and ferries more broadly. On each island, a hire car is the practical way to reach trailheads and lookouts.

## Faial: Horta and the marina

Horta has long been a port of call for boats crossing the ocean, and you feel it the moment you walk along the waterfront. According to the tourism board, the [Horta marina](https://mi.visitazores.com/en/node/8) was inaugurated on 3 June 1986 and was the first marina built in the Azores. As well as sheltering Atlantic sailors, its walls are covered with colourful paintings by visiting crews: boat names, dates, crests and cartoons, layered over each other along the breakwater. Walking the length of it, reading the names, is one of the island's small pleasures.

<Figure id="pf-horta-marina" caption="The marina at Horta from above, its pontoons full of ocean-going yachts." />

In town, [Peter Café Sport](https://www.petercafesport.com/), at Rua José Azevedo "Peter" 9, is the sailors' bar of the Atlantic. Its own site describes it as a yacht club and an information point for people crossing the ocean, a place that has also served as currency exchange and post office, and its walls are hung with old photographs and the flags of visiting boats.

For the view that explains Faial's place in the world, drive up to the lookout at Ponta da Espalamaca, which the tourism board describes as looking over the whole Bay of Horta with Pico rising beyond.

## Faial: the Caldeira and Capelinhos

Faial's nickname is the Blue Island, for the hydrangea hedges that line its roads in summer. At its centre is the Caldeira, which the tourism board describes as a crater about 1,450 metres across and 400 metres deep, its walls covered in cedar, juniper, ferns and moss. The island's highest point, Cabeço Gordo, stands on its rim at 1,043 metres, and on a clear day the view takes in all the neighbouring islands.

The far west is another world. The Capelinhos volcano erupted between 1957 and 1958 and added new land to the island; the [Faial page](https://mi.visitazores.com/en/node/8) calls it the last volcanic eruption in the Azores and likens walking there to landing on the moon, with grey ash and lapilli slowly being colonised by plants. The old lighthouse, long switched off, now stands over an interpretation centre that Visit Azores' [geotourism guide](https://www.visitazores.com/en/experience-the-azores/geotourism) says uses films, holograms and multimedia to explain the eruption. The tourism board adds that the visit ends with a climb to the top of the lighthouse.

## Pico: the mountain

Pico's volcano is the highest point in Portugal. Visit Azores' [Pico page](https://mi.visitazores.com/en/node/14) gives its height as 2,350 metres, with a summit crater about 700 metres around and a small cone, Piquinho, rising about 70 metres from one end, with fumaroles still steaming at its base. Climbing it is a serious mountain day, and on a clear one the view stretches to Faial, São Jorge, Graciosa and Terceira.

The ascent is controlled. The regional government's [Casa da Montanha leaflet](https://siaram.azores.gov.pt/centros-interpretacao/casa-da-montanha/Casa-da-Montanha.pdf) describes the mountain house, at around 1,200 metres where the road ends, as a compulsory stop whose main job is to register and control every climb, through an authorisation request made under the regulations in force. Each climber is given a GPS tracking device. The tourism board's Pico page also says climbers should always be accompanied by a guide, and that is good advice for anyone without solid mountain experience: the route is steep, and cloud can roll in quickly.

<Pair a="pf-pico-summit-cloud" b="pf-currais-rock" captionA="Mount Pico above a band of cloud, seen from the island's wooded lower slopes." captionB="Black lava stones stacked into low vineyard walls on Pico's lower slopes." />

Check the weather before you commit, check the [Azores Trails alerts page](https://trails.visitazores.com/en/alerts) for closures, and turn back if conditions change. The mountain will still be there tomorrow.

## Pico: walls, wine and lava

Below the mountain, the island is a maze of black stone. The [Cooperativa Vitivinícola da Ilha do Pico](https://www.acooperativadopico.com/a-vinha/) explains how the basalt that made farming so hard was gathered up and built into heaps and labyrinths of walled plots, the currais, which shelter the vines from salty winds. All the work in these vineyards is done by hand. The cooperative names three noble native grapes that survived inside the walls, Arinto dos Açores, Verdelho and Terrantez do Pico, and says Pico wine once travelled to the West Indies, New England and even the court of the Russian tsars.

<Figure id="pf-currais-mountain" size="full" caption="A track between the black stone walls of Pico's vineyards, with the mountain beyond." />

The cooperative itself was [founded in 1949](https://www.acooperativadopico.com/a-cooperativa/) by growers trying to save the noble grapes and the walled-vineyard tradition, a landscape it notes is now classified as World Heritage by UNESCO. Its winery is on Avenida Padre Nunes da Rosa in [Madalena](https://www.acooperativadopico.com/contactos/), and it offers wine tourism visits with tastings of its wines and regional tapas. The cooperative's vineyard page lists the traditional growing areas as Madalena, Candelária, Criação Velha and Bandeiras, close to the sea, and Santa Luzia on the north coast.

If you liked the terraces of the Douro, Pico is the same story of people against stone, told in black instead of schist. Our [three days in the Douro Valley](/stories/three-days-in-the-douro-valley/) makes an interesting comparison.

The Pico page also points out the island's lava fields, which locals call lajidos or biscoito, and the mistérios, rough lava flows from eruptions in the sixteenth and eighteenth centuries. Underground, the tourism board describes the Gruta das Torres as the longest lava tube in the Azores.

## Whales in the channel

The waters around these islands are among the best in the Azores for whale and dolphin watching. [Visit Azores](https://www.visitazores.com/en/experience-the-azores/whale-watching) says it is possible throughout the year and sets out the code of conduct operators follow, including keeping their distance from the animals and never chasing them. Book with a licensed operator, and expect trips to be called off when the sea is rough. Our [guide to whale watching in the Azores](/stories/whale-watching-azores/) goes into the seasons and species.

## How long to stay

Four or five nights split between the two islands is a comfortable minimum: one or two days on Faial for Horta, the Caldeira and Capelinhos, one for the vineyards and lava coast of Pico, and a spare day for the mountain, to use when the weather allows. If you are starting from São Miguel, our [week on São Miguel](/stories/sao-miguel-itinerary/) covers the bigger island first, and our comparison of [the Azores and Madeira](/stories/azores-or-madeira/) helps if you are still choosing between archipelagos.

## Before you book

- Check the Horta–Madalena sailings for your dates on [Atlânticoline's timetable](https://www.atlanticoline.pt/horarios/), and leave slack before any onward flight.
- If you plan to climb Pico, read the [Casa da Montanha information](https://siaram.azores.gov.pt/centros-interpretacao/casa-da-montanha/Casa-da-Montanha.pdf), allow a spare day for weather, and consider hiring a guide.
- Check the [Azores Trails alerts](https://trails.visitazores.com/en/alerts) the night before any walk.
- Contact the [Pico wine cooperative](https://www.acooperativadopico.com/contactos/) ahead to arrange a visit and tasting.
- Pack for changeable weather: wind, rain and sun can all arrive in the same afternoon.
