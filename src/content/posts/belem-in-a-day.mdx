The river is wider here. By the time the Tagus reaches Belém it has opened out towards the Atlantic, and on a bright morning the water is so full of light that the white stone along its banks almost hurts to look at. This is where Lisbon faced the sea, where ships left for India and Brazil, and where the kings of the age of the Discoveries built monuments to match their ambitions.

Belém is only a short ride west of the centre, but it feels like a different city: broad lawns, palm trees, a long riverside promenade and some of the finest buildings in Portugal, all within walking distance of each other. One day is enough to see the essentials, if you plan around a couple of details.

## Getting there

Belém is easy to reach without a car. Trains on the Cascais line stop at Belém station, and the area is also served by several Carris bus routes and by tram 15, as the [transport guide from Parques de Sintra](https://www.parquesdesintra.pt/en/plan-your-visit/how-to-get-there/) (which runs the riding school in Belém) sets out. The tram is the most atmospheric option and the train is usually the quickest. Our [guide to getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) explains Lisbon's urban trains and tickets.

## The one thing to know: Mondays

Several of Belém's headline sights close on Mondays. The [Jerónimos Monastery and Belém Tower](https://mosteirojeronimos.torrebelem.gov.pt/en) both open Tuesday to Sunday, and so does the [National Coach Museum](https://www.mncoches.gov.pt/en). [MAAT](https://www.maat.pt/en), by contrast, closes on Tuesdays. If Monday is your only free day, you can still walk the riverfront, see the monuments from outside and eat custard tarts, but you will miss the interiors. Any other day works better.

## Jerónimos Monastery

Start here, and start early. The Royal Monastery of Santa Maria de Belém takes its everyday name from the Order of St Jerome, to which it was dedicated. According to its [official history](https://mosteirojeronimos.torrebelem.gov.pt/en/mosteiro-dos-jeronimos), King Manuel I granted the Restelo site to the order in 1496, and the foundation stone was laid on 6 January in either 1501 or 1502. The same page records that Vasco da Gama prayed in the chapel that stood here before setting out on his voyage of 1497.

<Figure id="bel-jeronimos-carvings" caption="Carved limestone columns and arches at the Jerónimos Monastery." />

The result is one of the defining buildings of Manuel I's reign, all honey-coloured limestone carved into ropes, shells, leaves and sea creatures. Visit Lisboa notes that the monastery was [proclaimed a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/p/Heritage). Inside, the cloister is the highlight: arcades of lace-like stone around a quiet garden. The church is vast and cool, a welcome pause on a hot day.

<Pair a="bel-jeronimos-garden" b="bel-jeronimos-cloister" captionA="The monastery seen from its garden, between tall cypresses." captionB="The arcaded cloister, the highlight of a visit." />

Queues build through the morning, especially in summer. Be at the door when it opens, or come late in the afternoon.

## Belém Tower

A pleasant walk west along the river brings you to the tower. It was built, its [official page](https://mosteirojeronimos.torrebelem.gov.pt/en/torre-de-belem) explains, in the second decade of the 16th century, as part of a defensive project begun under King João II and continued by Manuel I, to guard the entrance to Lisbon through the Tagus bar. Like the monastery, it has been a National Monument since 1907 and on the UNESCO list since 1983.

The interior is compact, and if time is short, the exterior is the main event anyway: the carved balconies, the turrets and the stone rope that wraps around the walls are best seen from the riverside lawn.

## Along the river

Between the monastery and the tower, the riverfront is the backbone of the day. The great white prow of the **Padrão dos Descobrimentos**, the Monument to the Discoveries, juts out over the water. Its sculpture group depicts, in the [monument's own words](https://padraodosdescobrimentos.pt/en/), the explorers, authors and historic figures of the age of the Discoveries. It opens every day, with longer hours from March to September than in winter; check the [schedules page](https://padraodosdescobrimentos.pt/en/contacts-schedules/), which also lists the holidays when it closes.

<Figure id="bel-padrao" caption="Carved figures on the Padrão dos Descobrimentos, leaning out towards the river." />

Beyond it, the small marina of the Doca de Belém is a good spot to sit by the water, and the promenade continues east towards the city. If you have the energy, keep walking past the marina to see the river open up under the great suspension bridge.

<Figure id="bel-riverfront" size="full" caption="The promenade at the Padrão dos Descobrimentos, with the marina on one side and the suspension bridge downstream." />

## Museums and gardens

Belém has more museums than one day can hold, so choose by interest.

- **[Museu Nacional dos Coches](https://www.mncoches.gov.pt/en).** The museum describes its holdings as the world's most important collection of ceremonial carriages. It occupies two sites: the old Royal Riding School, begun in 1787 and adapted as a museum in 1904, and a new building by the architect Paulo Mendes da Rocha on Avenida da Índia, opened in 2015.
- **[MAAT](https://www.maat.pt/en).** The Museum of Art, Architecture and Technology sits on Avenida Brasília by the river, with exhibitions spread across several venues on its campus. It opens Wednesday to Monday.
- **[Centro Cultural de Belém](https://www.ccb.pt/en/).** The cultural centre opposite the monastery houses the MAC/CCB contemporary art museum alongside concert halls and gardens. On the first Sunday of each month, it also hosts the CCB Market.
- **[Jardim Botânico Tropical](https://www.museus.ulisboa.pt/jardim-botanico-tropical).** Tucked beside the monastery, this garden was created by royal decree in 1906 and moved to its present site in 1912. The University of Lisbon, which runs it, lists around 600 plant species, an iron greenhouse from 1914 and a scatter of 17th- and 18th-century marble statues. It is the quietest place in Belém.

## Custard tarts on Rua de Belém

No day here is complete without a pastel de nata, and Belém has the most famous of them. **[Pastéis de Belém](https://pasteisdebelem.pt/en/history/)**, at numbers 84 to 92 on Rua de Belém, tells its own story this way: when Portugal's convents and monasteries were shut down in 1834, someone from the Jerónimos Monastery began selling sweet pastries in the shop attached to a sugar cane refinery next door, and in 1837 the baking of the pastries began in the buildings beside it. The recipe, it says, is known only to the master confectioners who make it.

There is usually a queue for takeaway. The tables inside often move faster. Eat them warm, with cinnamon and icing sugar on the side. For more on the pastry and the rest of the country's sweet tradition, see our guide to [what to eat in Portugal](/stories/what-to-eat-in-portugal/).

## Where to stay

Most visitors see Belém as a day out from the centre, and our comparison of [Lisbon and Porto](/stories/lisbon-or-porto/) covers the central neighbourhoods. Staying in Belém itself means quiet evenings by the river and an early start at the monastery before the crowds arrive. Two options:

- **[Palácio do Governador](https://www.palaciogovernador.com/)**, on Rua Bartolomeu Dias, is a boutique hotel on the site of the former house of the governor of Belém Tower, a few minutes from the river.
- **[Pestana Palace Lisboa](https://www.pestana.com/en/hotel/pestana-palace)**, in the Alto de Santo Amaro neighbourhood about 2 km from the tower, occupies the Palácio de Valle Flôr, a late 19th-century palace that reopened as a hotel in 2001 and is classified, with its gardens, as a National Monument.

## A day in Belém

| Time | Plan |
|---|---|
| Opening time | Jerónimos Monastery, cloister first |
| Late morning | Tropical botanical garden, or the Coach Museum |
| Lunch | Around Rua de Belém, then custard tarts |
| Afternoon | Walk the river: Padrão dos Descobrimentos, Doca de Belém, Belém Tower |
| Late afternoon | MAAT or the CCB, then sunset by the water |

If you are in Lisbon for several days, pair Belém with a slow walk through [Alfama, Mouraria and Graça](/stories/lisbon-alfama-mouraria-graca/) on another day. Our [first-timer's guide to Portugal](/stories/first-timers-guide-to-portugal/) has more on fitting Lisbon into a longer trip.

## Before you book

- Avoid Mondays if you want to go inside the monastery, the tower or the Coach Museum; check current hours on the [official monuments site](https://mosteirojeronimos.torrebelem.gov.pt/en).
- MAAT closes on Tuesdays; see [its site](https://www.maat.pt/en) for current exhibitions.
- Arrive at Jerónimos for opening time in summer, or go late in the day.
- Check [Padrão dos Descobrimentos opening hours](https://padraodosdescobrimentos.pt/en/contacts-schedules/) for the season you are visiting.
- Bring sun protection. The riverfront has little shade at midday.
