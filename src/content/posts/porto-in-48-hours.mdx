Porto rewards the early riser. At eight in the morning the granite streets of the old town are still damp and quiet, the smell of coffee drifts out of half-open café doors, and from the top of the hill you can watch mist lifting off the Douro to reveal the port lodges on the far bank. By midday the same streets are full, the river is busy with boats, and the city has switched from moody to lively. Two days is enough to see both sides of it.

This plan is built around walking, with a little help from the funicular, the tram and the cable car. Porto is steep, so the order matters: the idea is to go downhill as often as possible.

## Before you start

Porto's historic centre is compact, and Visit Porto's [city-quarters plan](https://visitporto.travel/storage/01HZER0RKPNVVFD34JJNNK2ZA4.pdf) describes it as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The same plan treats the historic centres of Porto and Gaia, across the river, as a single quarter, which is a good way to think about your first day.

If you are flying in, the [Metro do Porto](https://www.metrodoporto.pt/) runs line E between the airport and Trindade, in the city centre. For everything else about trains, buses and city transport, see our [guide to getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/).

Wear comfortable shoes with some grip. The cobbles are uneven and the hills are real.

## Day 1, morning: towers, tiles and books

Start at São Bento station. Even if you are not catching a train, walk into the entrance hall to see its walls of blue-and-white tiles, best before the crowds arrive. Then climb west towards the Clérigos.

The [Torre dos Clérigos](https://www.torredosclerigos.pt/) has dominated the Porto skyline since 1763. It belongs to the Baroque Clérigos church designed by the Italian architect Nicolau Nasoni, and a visit takes in the tower, with a 360-degree view of the city from the top, the church and a small museum. It opens daily from 09:00 to 19:00, and tickets can be bought at the [online ticket office](https://www.torredosclerigos.pt/en/ticket-office/); go early and the climb is less crowded.

<Figure id="p48-clerigos-tower" caption="The Clérigos tower above the rooftops of central Porto." />

A few minutes away, [Livraria Lello](https://livrarialello.pt/en/) has been a bookshop since 1906. Its neo-Gothic interior, with a red staircase and stained-glass ceiling, is the reason for the queue outside. Entry is by a ticket-voucher bought online, and the price can be deducted from a book you buy in the shop, so pick something to take home. It opens daily from 09:00 to 19:30.

## Day 1, afternoon: down to the river

From Lello, wander down through the old streets towards the river. Take your time on Rua das Flores, a pedestrian street of shops and cafés, and look up at the tiled façades and iron balconies as you go.

<Figure id="p48-capela-das-almas" caption="The blue-and-white tiled wall of the Capela das Almas on Rua de Santa Catarina." />

At the bottom, just above the Ribeira, is the [Palácio da Bolsa](https://palaciodabolsa.com/en/), the Stock Exchange Palace, which is the headquarters of Porto's commercial association, the Associação Comercial do Porto. Tourist visits are by guided tour, with tickets linked from its website.

Nearby on Rua Ferreira Borges, the IVDP's [Port and Douro Interactive Learning Centre](https://www.ivdp.pt/en/tourism/port-and-douro-interactive-learning-centre/) is a good primer before Gaia. The Douro and Port wine institute runs an exhibition here on how the wine is made, plus a tasting bar where you choose from eight ports. It opens from 10:00 to 19:30.

Then the Ribeira itself: the riverside quay, the stacked houses in every shade of ochre, pink and blue, the outdoor tables, the boats. It is the most photographed corner of the city, and it earns it. Find a table, order a glass of white port with tonic, and watch the river.

<Figure id="p48-guindais-funicular" caption="The Guindais funicular climbing the slope below the old city walls." />

## Day 1, evening: across the river to Gaia

Late afternoon is the moment to cross the lower deck of the Dom Luís I bridge to Vila Nova de Gaia, where the port houses keep their lodges. Book one or two cellar visits for the late afternoon, then ride the cable car up to Jardim do Morro for the view back across to Porto as the light fades. Our guide to [the port lodges of Gaia](/stories/port-lodges-of-gaia/) covers the houses, the tasting styles and how to pace it.

Walk back to Porto across the upper deck of the bridge, high above the water, for dinner on the Porto side.

## Day 2, morning: cafés and the market

Start the second day in the Baixa, the downtown area around Rua de Santa Catarina. The [Majestic Café](https://www.cafemajestic.com/pt/Majestic-Cafe/Historia.aspx), on Rua de Santa Catarina, opened to the public in December 1922, and its own history calls its interior the splendour of the Belle Époque. Coffee and a pastry here is a ritual rather than a bargain, and worth doing once.

A short walk away, the [Mercado do Bolhão](https://mercadobolhao.pt/en) is the city's historic market, classified as municipal heritage. It sells fresh produce from its stalls and has shops and restaurants under the same roof. The market opens Monday to Friday from 08:00 to 19:00 and Saturday from 08:00 to 18:00, and closes on Sundays, so plan around that.

<Pair a="p48-ribeira-facades" b="p48-river-sunset" captionA="Tiled and painted houses stacked above the river." captionB="Evening light over the Douro and the rooftops of the old town." />

This is a good morning to try the city's heavyweight dish, the francesinha: a layered sandwich of meats under melted cheese and a rich, beer-based sauce. It is best shared. For more on what to order, see our guide to [what to eat in Portugal](/stories/what-to-eat-in-portugal/).

## Day 2, afternoon: west towards the ocean

In the afternoon, head west. Porto's historic trams still run three lines, 1, 18 and 22, operated by [STCP](https://stcp.pt/pt/porto-tram-city-tour); tickets are sold on board, and the two-day ticket allows unlimited trips on all three. A ride in one of the old cars is a pleasure in itself.

The city's western edge is Foz, which Visit Porto's [quarters plan](https://visitporto.travel/storage/01HZER0RKPNVVFD34JJNNK2ZA4.pdf) describes as "the Porto that joins the river to the ocean", with the old streets of Foz Velha, the Passeio Alegre and beaches along the coast. Walk the seafront as the Atlantic swell breaks on the rocks, and stay for sunset.

If you prefer art to sea air, the [Fundação de Serralves](https://www.serralves.pt/en/) brings together a contemporary art museum, a large park and the Casa de Serralves. The same quarters plan links it to Foz, so you can do one in the afternoon and the other at sunset.

## Where to stay

For a short stay, sleep close to the action so you can walk everywhere.

- **The historic centre.** On pedestrianised Rua das Flores, the [Pousada Porto Rua das Flores](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-porto) occupies an 18th-century building that keeps its vaulted ceilings and original stonework. Expect noise and crowds in summer, and plenty of stairs.
- **Baixa.** The [Grande Hotel do Porto](https://www.grandehotelporto.com/), on Rua de Santa Catarina, describes a classic style dating from 1880 and is about five minutes on foot from São Bento. The Baixa is a little flatter and well connected.

If you are still deciding between the two great cities, our comparison of [Lisbon or Porto](/stories/lisbon-or-porto/) sets out the differences. And if you can stretch to a third day, the Douro Valley or a trip north to [Guimarães and Braga](/stories/guimaraes-and-braga/) are the natural next steps.

## Before you book

- Book Clérigos, Lello and any port lodge visits online before you travel, especially for weekends.
- Remember that the Bolhão market is closed on Sundays.
- Check the latest opening hours on each official site; they change with the seasons.
- Plan your days so that you walk downhill, and use the funicular, tram or cable car to go up.
- Pack a light waterproof outside high summer. Porto is greener than Lisbon for a reason.
