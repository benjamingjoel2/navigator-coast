Step off the ferry at Porto Santo and the colour changes. Madeira, a couple of hours behind you, is green, vertical and wet-shouldered with cloud. Porto Santo is low, dry and golden, its hills the colour of toast, with one enormous beach running along almost the whole of its southern shore. The sea in front of it is a startling, glassy turquoise. It feels less like a neighbour of Madeira than a different climate altogether.

That contrast is the point. Most visitors to the archipelago come for mountains and levadas. Porto Santo is where you go to do almost nothing, very well, for a few days.

## A small, old island

[Visit Madeira](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/porto-santo/porto-santo/) calls Porto Santo the smallest inhabited island of the archipelago, and notes that it is of volcanic origin and older than Madeira. It was settled some 600 years ago and is tied up with the age of the Portuguese discoveries, and particularly with Christopher Columbus, who lived here for a time.

It is compact. The tourism board's [guide to getting around](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/porto-santo/how-to-move-around-porto-santo/) gives the island as 11 km long and 6 km wide. The main town, Vila Baleira, sits in the middle of the south coast, right behind the beach.

Since October 2020 the whole island has been part of UNESCO's World Network of Biosphere Reserves. [Visit Madeira](https://visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/nature-seekers/protected-areas/nature-reserves/porto-santo-biosphere-reserve/) says the reserve covers the beach, the islets around the coast and the surrounding sea, and is home to more than 2,110 species, some of them found only here.

## Getting there

There are two ways across from Madeira, according to Visit Madeira's [arrival guide](https://visitmadeira.com/en/travel-info/how-to-get-here/): a 15-minute flight, or the ferry *Lobo Marinho*.

The ferry is run by [Porto Santo Line](https://www.portosantoline.pt/en/), which puts the island 71 km from Madeira. Its [FAQ](https://www.portosantoline.pt/pt/faqs) gives the crossing as roughly 2 hours 15 minutes, asks foot passengers to check in at the Funchal terminal at least 30 minutes before departure, and asks drivers to be at the car deck an hour before. The ship carries cars, so you can bring a hire car across, though on an island this size you may not need one. Porto Santo Line also sells day-trip packages and packages with a hotel. Sailing times change with the season, and the open Atlantic can be lively, so check the timetable for your dates and pack something for seasickness if you are prone to it.

Flying is quicker but leaves you less time to watch the island grow on the horizon. In summer and around holidays it is wise to book either one early.

## The beach

[Visit Madeira](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/porto-santo/porto-santo-beach/) describes Porto Santo's beach as nine kilometres of golden sand with calm, clear water. It is, in effect, one beach with many names, each stretch with its own character:

- **Fontinha**, next to the Cais Velho in town, is central, well equipped and has a Blue Flag.
- **Pedras Pretas** is close to the centre, with a family atmosphere.
- **Cabeço da Ponta** and **Ribeiro Cochino** are further west along the long, open sweep of sand.
- **Calheta**, at the far western end, finishes the beach with distinctive rock formations.

The sand itself is unusual. Visit Madeira's page on the [Fonte de Areia viewpoint](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/porto-santo/discovering-porto-santo/fonte-de-areia-viewpoint/) explains that it is mostly made of fragments of mollusc shells and calcareous algae, which is why it is soft and pale. The tourism board makes a good deal of its claimed therapeutic properties; we would simply say it is very pleasant to lie on.

## Viewpoints and short walks

The beach is the reason to come, but the island's high points are what make a stay feel complete.

**Portela.** [Visit Madeira](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/porto-santo/discovering-porto-santo/portela-viewpoint-porto-santo/) calls this the most emblematic view on the island: the whole south coast, with Vila Baleira in the middle and the beach running away to the west. It is just over a kilometre and a half from the centre, reached along an avenue of palms past a windmill.

**Pico do Castelo.** At 437 metres, [this viewpoint](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/porto-santo/discovering-porto-santo/pico-do-castelo-viewpoint/) looks over the bay, the town and the islets. It has traces of a 16th-century fort built against pirate raids, and a few cannons. You can drive up, or walk the classified [PS PR 2 Vereda do Pico do Castelo](https://visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/nature-seekers/activities/hiking/ps-pr-2-vereda-do-pico-do-castelo/), which has a 3.2 km option of about an hour and a half and a 4.6 km option of about two and a quarter hours. It skirts Pico do Facho, the island's highest point, and climbs through pine woods; a statue at the top honours António Schiappa de Azevedo, who drove the island's reforestation. The route page says to pay through the official portal, so check whether a booking is needed before you go.

**Pico de Ana Ferreira.** On the west of the island, this 283-metre peak is capped by a set of prismatic basalt columns, formed as volcanic magma cooled slowly. [Visit Madeira](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/porto-santo/discovering-porto-santo/pico-de-ana-ferreira/) says locals call it "the piano", though it looks more like organ pipes. The best view is from the Miradouro da Pedreira, near the golf club.

**Fonte de Areia.** Here wind has carved the old sandstone into strange, soft shapes, with an 18th-century fountain nearby.

## Columbus and Vila Baleira

The town is small and easy to walk, with cafés, restaurants and shops close together. Its main sight is [Casa Colombo, the Porto Santo Museum](https://visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/culture-passionates/heritage/museums/casa-colombo-porto-santo-museum/), on Travessa da Sacristia in Vila Baleira. The building is said to have been Columbus's home on the island, after his marriage to Filipa de Moniz, daughter of the island's first captain, Bartolomeu Perestrelo. Visit Madeira notes a wall with two Gothic pointed-arch windows that the museum believes connects it to Columbus's time. It opened as a museum in 1989 and covers the navigator and the Portuguese and Spanish maritime expansion. It is run by the regional culture directorate, which has [its own page](https://cultura.madeira.gov.pt/casa-colombo-museu-do-porto-santo) for the museum; Visit Madeira lists it as closed on Mondays and public holidays.

Each year the island also hosts the Columbus Festival, which Visit Madeira describes as a re-creation of the age of discoveries and Columbus's stay.

## Getting around

The [Visit Madeira guide](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/porto-santo/how-to-move-around-porto-santo/) makes the case for doing without a car. If you stay in the centre, walking is perfectly feasible. A cycle path runs for almost 5 km along the regional road from Ribeiro Cochino to Praia da Calheta, and there are bike hire companies. Buses connect the town with the port, and a daily tourist bus does a circuit of the island in about two hours. There are taxis from the rank in the centre, and car, scooter and quad hire if you want to explore on your own.

For golfers, the 18-hole course at [Porto Santo Golfe](https://www.portosantogolfe.com/) was designed by Seve Ballesteros, according to Visit Madeira.

## When to go

[Visit Madeira](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/porto-santo/porto-santo/) stresses the island's mild climate all year round, so there is no bad month to come. Summer is the classic beach season and the busiest; spring and autumn are quieter. Whenever you go, remember that the ferry depends on the state of the sea. Our guide to [the best time to visit Portugal](/stories/best-time-to-visit-portugal/) covers the islands' seasons alongside the mainland.

Porto Santo works well as the restful end to a Madeira trip: a few days of walking from [Funchal](/stories/funchal-guide/), then the ferry, a book and the beach.

## Before you book

- Check current sailing times on [Porto Santo Line](https://www.portosantoline.pt/en/) and allow for weather delays at either end of your trip.
- Decide whether you need a car on the island; most visitors staying in the centre don't.
- Book accommodation early for July, August and the Columbus Festival.
- If you plan to walk the Pico do Castelo route, check [its page](https://visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/nature-seekers/activities/hiking/ps-pr-2-vereda-do-pico-do-castelo/) for booking and status.
- Check [Casa Colombo's opening days](https://cultura.madeira.gov.pt/casa-colombo-museu-do-porto-santo) before planning a visit around it.
