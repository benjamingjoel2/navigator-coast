Lisbon in December has a particular light. The sun sits low over the Tagus all day, the limestone pavements shine after a shower, and the smell of roasting chestnuts drifts from the street carts. The crowds of summer are gone, the cafés are full of locals, and on a clear afternoon you can sit outside with a coffee and wonder why more people do not come now.

Winter is Portugal's quiet season, and for the right traveller it is a very good one. It is not beach weather on the mainland, and it does rain. But the cities are at their most lived-in, the south is mild, and out in the Atlantic, Madeira stays green and warm enough for walking in shirtsleeves. This ten-day route strings those pieces together: Lisbon, the Alentejo plains around Évora, the eastern Algarve, then a flight out to Madeira.

## What the weather really does

The best guide is the record kept by IPMA, Portugal's weather service. Its 1991–2020 climate normals show a clear pattern, which the table below summarises for December.

| Place | Average high | Average low | Average rainfall | Days with 1 mm+ of rain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Lisbon](https://www.ipma.pt/opencms/bin/file.data/climate-normal/cn_91-20_LISBOA_GEOFISICO.pdf) | 15.4°C | 9.4°C | 108.5 mm | 10.0 |
| [Beja](https://www.ipma.pt/opencms/bin/file.data/climate-normal/cn_91-20_BEJA.pdf) (inland Alentejo) | 14.8°C | 6.8°C | 80.2 mm | 8.3 |
| [Faro](https://www.ipma.pt/opencms/bin/file.data/climate-normal/cn_91-20_FARO.pdf) | 17.1°C | 9.7°C | 90.0 mm | 7.0 |
| [Funchal](https://www.ipma.pt/bin/file.data/climate-normal/cn_91-20_FUNCHAL.pdf) | 21.0°C | 15.5°C | 94.8 mm | 8.6 |

Three things stand out. Lisbon is wetter than people expect: on that record, November is its wettest month, and December and January still average around ten rainy days each. The inland Alentejo is the coldest stop, with nights in single figures and the occasional frost at Beja in January. And Funchal is in another league, with December highs around 21°C and lows of about 15°C.

Pack for all of it: a light waterproof, a warm layer for the evenings, shoes that grip on wet cobbles, and something you would wear to walk in the sun on Madeira.

## Days 1–3: Lisbon

Spend the first three days in Lisbon, and plan them around the weather rather than a fixed list. On clear days, walk: up through Alfama to the castle viewpoints, along the riverfront to Belém, out to Sintra by train. On wet days, go indoors.

The best rainy-day refuge in the city is the Gulbenkian. The museum, the modern art centre and the garden sit together in a park north of the centre; the foundation's [opening hours page](https://gulbenkian.pt/en/useful-information/opening-hours/) lists the museum and CAM as open 10am to 6pm, until 9pm on Saturdays, and closed on Tuesdays, as well as on 24 and 25 December and 1 January. The garden is open daily from sunrise to sunset.

<Pair a="win-lisbon-rainbow" b="win-lisbon-street" captionA="A rainbow over Lisbon's rooftops as storm clouds break." captionB="A wet avenue in Lisbon after a winter shower." />

For a seasonal treat, head to Praça da Figueira in the Baixa. [Confeitaria Nacional](https://confeitarianacional.com/) opened there in 1829 and says it introduced the *bolo rei*, the crown-shaped Christmas cake studded with candied fruit, to Portugal in 1875. You will see it in every pastry shop window in December; this is the place to try it.

If you have never been to Lisbon before, our guide to [Lisbon or Porto](/stories/lisbon-or-porto/) includes advice on the best neighbourhoods to stay in.

## Days 4–5: Évora and the Alentejo

On day four, take the train east. CP's [Évora page](https://www.cp.pt/info/en/w/alentejo-evora) notes that the city has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1986 and that the station is close enough to walk into the centre, past the public garden, the church of São Francisco with its Chapel of Bones, and the markets, to Praça do Giraldo. Winter suits Évora. The Roman temple, the cathedral and the whitewashed lanes inside the walls are yours, and the cafés on the main square fill with people rather than tour groups.

<Figure id="win-evora-temple" caption="Évora's Roman temple, with the cathedral tower rising behind." />

Dress warmly in the evening: this is the coldest stop on the route. The Alentejo answers the cold with its cooking, which runs to slow-cooked pork, bread-thickened soups and robust red wine. Stay inside the walls if you can. The [Pousada Convento Évora](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-evora), set in a former convent between the cathedral and the Roman temple, is the heritage option, and there are plenty of simpler guesthouses in the old town.

Use the second day for a slow wander, a market morning, or a drive into the cork-oak country if you have hired a car. Our [ten-day itinerary from Porto to the Algarve](/stories/ten-day-portugal-itinerary/) has more on Évora and how it fits into a longer trip.

## Days 6–7: The eastern Algarve

From Évora, head south to the Algarve. A car is the simplest way; without one, our guide to [getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) explains the coach and rail options. Once you are on the coast, the trains are easy: CP says the [whole Algarve line is now electrified](https://www.cp.pt/info/en/w/improved-service-algarve-line), with direct services between Lagos and Vila Real de Santo António.

Base yourself in Tavira or Faro, both real towns that keep going all winter. Tavira, with its river, churches and castle, is the more beautiful; Faro, with its walled old town and the airport close by, is the more practical, and it suits a flight to Madeira. If you were hoping to stay at the [Pousada Convento Tavira](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-tavira), note that its site says it is closed for structural works from 9 November to 22 December 2026.

Winter in the Algarve is for walking, birdwatching and long lunches rather than beach days. The Faro figures above give average December highs in the high teens, and the park authority ICNF's [guide to the Ria Formosa](https://www.icnf.pt/api/file/doc/f61c223d0d89e467) notes that more than 20,000 waterbirds gather there in the wintering season. Our full guide to [the Algarve in winter](/stories/algarve-in-winter/) covers the walks, the almond blossom and Loulé's Carnival in detail.

## Days 8–10: Madeira

End somewhere warm. Madeira is reached by air: Visit Madeira says the [flight from Lisbon takes about 90 minutes](https://visitmadeira.com/en/travel-info/how-to-get-here/), into the international airport at Santa Cruz, a short drive from Funchal. Check whether there is a direct flight from Faro on your dates; if not, connect through Lisbon.

Winter is festive on the island. The regional [events calendar](https://visitmadeira.com/media/1knpekh3/calend%C3%A1rio-de-anima%C3%A7%C3%A3o-turistica_2026-2029_en.pdf) lists the illumination of Funchal's amphitheatre of hills and the Christmas exhibits in the city centre from 1 December 2026 to 10 January 2027, a New Year's Eve firework display on 31 December, and the "Chants of Epiphany" in the Municipal Gardens on 5 January 2027. If you want to be here for New Year, book flights and beds early.

<Pair a="win-funchal-tree" b="win-funchal-lights" captionA="A Christmas tree of lights on Madeira during the festive season." captionB="Seasonal greetings in lights by the marina at Praça da Autonomia, Funchal." />

Spend a morning at the [Mercado dos Lavradores](https://www.visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/culture-passionates/heritage/monuments-and-architecture/mercado-dos-lavradores), Funchal's market hall, opened on 24 November 1940, with tile panels on its façade and fish market and the island's fresh and tropical produce inside. Then get out onto the trails. The island's levada paths are at their best in the cool season, but take the official [advice to walkers](https://visitmadeira.com/en/travel-info/useful-information/advice-to-walkers/) seriously: check whether a trail is closed, turn back in heavy rain or strong wind, and tell someone where you are going. Visit Madeira also notes that non-residents over 12 now pay a fee to use the classified walking routes. For a full comparison of the two Atlantic archipelagos, see our guide to [the Azores or Madeira](/stories/azores-or-madeira/).

For a splurge on the last night, [Reid's Palace](https://www.belmond.com/en/hotels/europe/portugal/reids-palace-madeira), opened by the Reid family in 1891, sits on the clifftop above the sea with subtropical gardens and a traditional afternoon tea.

## Making the route work

- **Flip it if you like.** Fly into Lisbon and out of Funchal, or start on Madeira and finish in Lisbon.
- **Cut a stop if you need to.** With only a week, drop the Algarve and fly from Lisbon to Madeira.
- **Mind the holidays.** Museums, shops and restaurants can close or shorten hours on 24, 25 and 31 December and 1 January. Plan travel days around them.
- **Keep a rain plan.** In Lisbon especially, have an indoor option for each day and move things around as the forecast changes.

## Before you book

- Check IPMA's forecast a few days before travel and pack layers and a waterproof.
- Confirm opening days for any museum you care about, especially over Christmas and New Year.
- Book Madeira flights and accommodation early if you will be there for New Year's Eve.
- Check whether hotels are open on your dates; some use the quiet months for works, as the Pousada Convento Tavira does in 2026.
- Before any walk on Madeira, check trail closures and conditions on [Visit Madeira](https://visitmadeira.com/en/travel-info/useful-information/advice-to-walkers/).
