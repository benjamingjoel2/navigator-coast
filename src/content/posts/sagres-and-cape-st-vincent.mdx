The wind is the first thing. It comes off the Atlantic with nothing to stop it, flattening the scrub on the clifftops and pushing the gulls sideways. Then the scale of the place sinks in: a high, bare plateau that ends in sheer walls of rock, and beyond them nothing but ocean. Out on the last headland, a lighthouse with a red lantern stands above the cliffs, and at sunset half the Algarve seems to have driven out to watch the light go.

This is the south-western corner of mainland Europe. The ancients knew it as the end of the world, and even now, with a car park and a stream of visitors, it does not feel far off. Sagres, the small town on the promontory next door, is a surfers' and walkers' base with a fortress full of stories. Together they make one of the most memorable days, or better, two, in Portugal.

## Where the land ends

The official site of the [Promontório de Sagres](https://fortalezadesagres.pt/) describes this as the south-western limit of Europe, the finisterra of ancient texts, beyond which navigation was considered especially dangerous. The Romans knew it as the Promunturium Sacrum, the sacred promontory, and there are references to a sanctuary here dedicated to Hercules or to Saturn. In 2015 the European Union awarded the Sagres promontory the European Heritage Label.

The landscape explains the legend. The site's [notes on the promontory's wildlife](https://fortalezadesagres.pt/sobre-o-promontorio/fauna/) describe the Ponta de Sagres as a great platform of compact Jurassic limestone ending in vertical cliffs 40 metres high. The whole coast here lies within the [Parque Natural do Sudoeste Alentejano e Costa Vicentina](https://www.icnf.pt/conservacao/rnapareasprotegidas/parquesnaturais/pnsudoestealentejanoecostavicentina), the natural park that protects the wild west coast all the way north into the Alentejo.

## The Fortaleza de Sagres

The fortress fills the neck of the Sagres headland, a long wall cutting it off from the mainland. Museus e Monumentos de Portugal, which runs it, sets out its history on its [Fortaleza de Sagres page](https://www.museusemonumentos.pt/pt/museus-e-monumentos/fortaleza-de-sagres). In 1443, Prince Henry, the Infante D. Henrique, obtained permission from his brother, the regent D. Pedro, to found a town on the promontory, the Vila do Infante. He spent his last days here and died in 1460.

<Figure id="sagres-fortress" caption="The walls of the Fortaleza de Sagres, with the small lighthouse on the point behind." />

Little of that town survives. The museum lists the traces of a saw-toothed wall, a cistern tower, and the so-called rosa dos ventos, a great stone circle uncovered by chance in 1921 whose purpose is still debated. Most of what you see is later: the small Church of Nossa Senhora da Graça, the bastioned wall remodelled in the late 18th century, the old governor's house. But the real monument is the headland itself, and once through the gate you can walk right round it on paths along the cliff edge, with the ocean booming below.

The fortress opens daily, with [published hours](https://www.museusemonumentos.pt/pt/museus-e-monumentos/fortaleza-de-sagres), last entry half an hour before closing and a short list of closed days. It also closes when severe weather warnings are in force: it shut on [11 November 2026](https://fortalezadesagres.pt/eventos-noticias/aviso-encerramento-da-fortaleza-de-sagres-a-11-de-novembro/) when the Faro district was placed on red alert for rain and wind. Check before you go, and check the ticket price on the same page.

## Cabo de São Vicente

West along the coast road, Cape St Vincent is the higher, wilder twin. The lighthouse buildings sit inside old fortified walls right on the edge, and the cliffs fall straight into a dark, restless sea.

<Pair a="sagres-cape-cliffs" b="sagres-cape-lighthouse" captionA="The lighthouse at Cabo de São Vicente, on the edge of sheer cliffs." captionB="The red lantern above the old walls of the cape." />

Sunset here is a ritual, and in summer it is a crowded one. For a quieter experience, come in the morning or out of season, when you may have the clifftop almost to yourself. Whatever the time, stay well back from the edges and do not count on barriers to stop you: the wind can gust hard.

## Birds and wild flowers

Come in autumn and look up. The promontory's [wildlife notes](https://fortalezadesagres.pt/sobre-o-promontorio/fauna/) explain that its position draws hundreds of migrating soaring birds in late summer and autumn on their way to Africa. Many of them are young birds, exploring possible crossing points before eventually using the Strait of Gibraltar. Raptors ride the rising thermals over the plateau, and on a good day the sky above the cape can be busy with them.

The plants are special too. The same site's [flora page](https://fortalezadesagres.pt/sobre-o-promontorio/flora/) describes a windswept heath of species found almost nowhere else, among them a spiny milk-vetch that is endemic to this promontory and threatened with extinction, a thyme endemic to south-west Portugal, and the autumn-flowering wild crocus. Stay on the paths, and look but do not pick.

## Beaches and walks

Sagres sits between two coasts, and its beaches face both. On the sheltered south side, wide bays curve below pale limestone cliffs. On the west, the Atlantic beaches take the full swell, and that is where the surfers go.

<Figure id="sagres-bay" caption="A broad bay near Sagres, sheltered from the worst of the Atlantic swell." />

The cliffs make for superb walking. The Rota Vicentina's [Historical Way](https://rotavicentina.com/en/historical-way/) is part of the long-distance GR 11/E9 route, which the trail network describes as connecting Sagres with St Petersburg, and the network's routes run all the way down to the cape. Our guide to [walking the Rota Vicentina](/stories/walking-the-rota-vicentina/) explains the routes, stages and seasons.

<Figure id="sagres-beach-cliffs" size="full" caption="Limestone cliffs above a long, quiet beach on the Sagres coast." />

Even if you are not doing a long walk, a few hours on the clifftop paths between the beaches and the headlands will show you why people fall for this coast. Carry water and a layer: it is often cooler and windier here than in the rest of the Algarve.

## Getting there

Sagres is at the end of the road, and the railway stops short of it in Lagos. From there, the Rota Vicentina's [region guide](https://rotavicentina.com/en/the-region/) lists national coaches run by Rede Expressos and regional VAMUS Algarve buses to Sagres, Cabo de São Vicente and Vila do Bispo. Check the timetables carefully, especially out of season. Our [Lagos guide](/stories/lagos-guide/) covers the train to Lagos, and our guide to [getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) explains coaches, car hire and motorway tolls.

A car makes it far easier to reach the beaches and to be at the cape for sunrise or sunset. If you drive, park only in marked areas and do not leave valuables in view.

## Planning your day

If you have one day, split it between the two headlands. Spend the morning at the fortress, when the light is clear and the paths around the point are quieter, then take a long lunch in town. In the afternoon, go to a beach or walk a stretch of clifftop, and save the cape for the end of the day.

If you have two, give one to the sea and the other to the land. Use the first for beaches and a swim on the sheltered south side if the water is calm; use the second for a longer walk, binoculars in hand if it is autumn. Either way, build in slack. The weather at this corner of the country changes fast, and the best moments here, a sudden break of sun over the cliffs, a flock of birds turning over the plateau, rarely keep to a timetable.

## Where to stay

- **[Pousada Sagres](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-sagres).** Part of the Pousadas de Portugal group, it sits in the town of Sagres, about ten minutes on foot from the beach according to its website, with views of the Fortaleza de Sagres and the Cabo de São Vicente lighthouse.
- **[Memmo Baleeira](https://www.memmohotels.com/baleeira/).** A contemporary design hotel at Sítio da Baleeira in Sagres, with a spa that includes an indoor pool.

Sagres is small, and much of what there is to do happens outdoors, so it also works as a day trip from Lagos. But staying overnight lets you see the cape at both ends of the day, when it is at its most dramatic. If you are travelling in the cooler months, our guide to [the Algarve in winter](/stories/algarve-in-winter/) explains what to expect from the weather and what stays open.

## Before you book

- Check the fortress's [opening hours, closed days and ticket price](https://www.museusemonumentos.pt/pt/museus-e-monumentos/fortaleza-de-sagres) before you travel.
- Look at [IPMA's forecasts and warnings](https://www.ipma.pt/en/) the day before; the fortress closes during severe weather alerts, and the clifftops are dangerous in high wind.
- If you are relying on buses from Lagos, check the current Rede Expressos and VAMUS Algarve timetables for your date.
- Visiting for the bird migration? Plan for late summer or autumn.
- Pack a warm layer and windproof jacket, even in summer, and wear sturdy shoes for the cliff paths.
