The first glass of Port most people taste is the wrong one. A sticky, anonymous ruby poured at the end of a long dinner, served too warm, from a bottle that has been open since the spring. It is no wonder so many visitors arrive in Porto convinced they do not like it.

Then you cross the river to Vila Nova de Gaia, step into a cool lodge that smells of old wood and wine, and someone pours you a chilled white Port, a fresh young ruby and a 20-year-old tawny side by side. They might as well be three different drinks. Port is not one wine but a family, and once you know who is who, the whole table opens up.

## What makes Port, Port

Port comes only from the Douro valley, the region the IVDP, its regulator, describes as [the oldest demarcated and regulated wine region in the world](https://www.ivdp.pt/en), dating from 1756. Wines of Portugal names the five red grapes singled out for Port, [Touriga Nacional, Touriga Franca, Tinta Roriz, Tinta Barroca and Tinto Cão](https://winesofportugal.com/en/discover/wine-regions/porto-e-douro/), grown on the valley's steep schist terraces.

<Figure id="pws-douro-terraces" size="full" caption="Terraced vineyards on the Douro's steep slopes, where every Port begins." />

The trick that makes it Port happens during fermentation. According to the IVDP's [account of the winemaking](https://www.ivdp.pt/en/wines/port-wines/the-winemaking/), fermentation is halted by adding grape spirit, most often about one part spirit to four parts wine. That stops the yeast before it has eaten all the sugar, so the wine keeps natural sweetness and ends up at 19 to 22 per cent alcohol. Traditionally the grapes were trodden in stone tanks called lagares; many wineries now use robotic treading tanks instead.

The following spring, the winemaker decides where the young wine will age: up in the Douro, or downriver in the [bonded lodges of Vila Nova de Gaia](https://www.ivdp.pt/en/wines/port-wines/the-winemaking/). That ageing, more than anything, decides which style it becomes.

## The two big families

The IVDP's [introduction to Port](https://www.ivdp.pt/en/wines/port-wines/introduction/) sorts the red wines into two families.

- **Ruby style.** The wine keeps a deep red colour and the fruit of youth. Categories run from Ruby to Reserve, Late Bottled Vintage and Vintage.
- **Tawny style.** Blends age in casks or vats, and over time the colour shifts towards tawny, with aromas of dried fruit and wood. Categories run from Tawny to Tawny Reserve, tawnies with an indication of age, and Colheita.

A simple way to remember it: ruby Ports are about fruit and are shaped by the bottle; tawny Ports are about time and are shaped by the wood.

## Ruby styles, step by step

The IVDP's [special categories page](https://www.ivdp.pt/en/wines/port-wines/special-categories/) sets out the ruby ladder:

| Style | What the IVDP says |
|---|---|
| Ruby Reserve | A blend of the best wines of a year, young, powerful and fruity |
| Late Bottled Vintage (LBV) | From a single year, aged four to six years in wood before bottling |
| Vintage | From a single year, bottled two to three years after the harvest; the only Port that ages in bottle |
| Single Quinta Vintage | A Vintage from a single harvest and a single estate |
| Crusted | A blend kept in bottle for at least three years before sale, throwing a deposit, or crust |

**Vintage** is the grand one. Because it ages in bottle, the IVDP says it can develop over 10 to 50 years, and old vintages should be [carefully decanted](https://www.ivdp.pt/en/wines/port-wines/choose-and-enjoy/) before drinking. **LBV** is the everyday answer: a taste of the vintage style for much less waiting.

## Tawny styles, and the magic of age

Tawnies are where many visitors fall for Port. The IVDP describes the Tawny Reserve as oak-aged with elegant flavours, and then the [age-indicated tawnies](https://www.ivdp.pt/en/wines/port-wines/special-categories/) of 10, 20, 30 and 40 years: the 20-year-old with notes of toasted vanilla and dried fruit, the 30-year-old turning towards honey, spice and nuts, and the 40-year-old the most concentrated and complex of all.

These age-indicated tawnies are blends. If you want a single year, look for a **Colheita**: a tawny from one harvest, aged in cask for at least seven years, with the vintage on the label.

Unlike vintage Port, tawnies are ready when they are bottled. The IVDP recommends age-indicated tawnies and Colheitas in particular, and suggests serving 30 and 40-year-old blends slightly cooled in large glasses after a meal.

## White and rosé

**White Port** comes in a range of sweetness levels, which the IVDP lists as [extra dry, dry, semi-sweet, sweet, very sweet and Lágrima](https://www.ivdp.pt/en/wines/port-wines/introduction/). There is also a newer, floral Light Dry White, and white Ports can be aged in wood too: the IVDP lists a Reserve White aged about seven years, white Ports with an indication of age and a White Colheita.

On a hot terrace, white Port's job is the Portonic: the IVDP itself suggests it [chilled with tonic](https://www.ivdp.pt/en/wines/port-wines/choose-and-enjoy/), or as an aperitif with toasted almonds.

**Rosé Port** is the youngest member of the family. The IVDP explains it is made by lightly macerating red grapes, is meant to be drunk young, with cherry, raspberry and strawberry notes, and is best served chilled or over ice.

## Serving it right

Temperature matters more than most people think. The IVDP's [serving guide](https://www.ivdp.pt/en/wines/port-wines/choose-and-enjoy/) gives these ranges:

| Style | Serve at |
|---|---|
| Rosé | 4ºC |
| White | 6 to 10ºC |
| Tawny style | 10 to 14ºC |
| Ruby style | 12 to 16ºC |

Once opened, Port does not last forever, and some styles fade fast. The same guide suggests drinking a vintage within one to two days, an LBV or crusted within four to five, a ruby within eight to ten, and a basic tawny within three to four weeks. Age-indicated tawnies, whites and colheitas keep for one to four months, the younger ones for the shorter time.

As for food, the IVDP pairs LBV with roast meats and well-spiced sauces, Ruby Reserve with cheesecake or mild, creamy cheese, a 10-year-old tawny with strong cheeses, dried fruit and walnuts, and 20-year-old tawny with traditional egg-based desserts and coffee.

## Where to taste

The lodges of Vila Nova de Gaia are the obvious classroom, and many run tours with tastings that let you compare styles side by side.

- [Graham's](https://www.grahams-port.com/visit-us) says its lodge has stood in Vila Nova de Gaia since 1890 and that all its wine ages there; visits include the museum, lodge and bottle cellar, followed by a tutored tasting.
- [Taylor's](https://www.taylor.pt/en/visit-taylors/port-cellars) offers self-guided visits of its cellars in Vila Nova de Gaia, finishing with a Port tasting.
- [Cockburn's](https://www.cockburns.com/visit-us) takes visit bookings at its lodge in Vila Nova de Gaia.

Our guide to [the Port lodges of Gaia](/stories/port-lodges-of-gaia/) covers how to cross the river and plan an afternoon, and [Porto in 48 hours](/stories/porto-in-48-hours/) fits a tasting into a short city break.

<Figure id="pws-pinhao-quinta" size="column" caption="A quinta on its hilltop above the vineyards at Pinhão, in the heart of the Douro." />

To taste where the grapes grow, head upriver: our [three days in the Douro Valley](/stories/three-days-in-the-douro-valley/) covers the train, the quintas and the river.

In Lisbon, the IVDP's own [Solar do Vinho do Porto](https://www.ivdp.pt/en/tourism/solar-do-vinho-do-porto-lisbon/), on Rua de São Pedro de Alcântara, was opened by the IVDP in 1946 and lists around 150 brands to taste and buy; tutored tastings are by reservation. Wines of Portugal also runs a [tasting room at World of Wine](https://www.winesofportugal.com/en/discover/tasting-rooms/) in Gaia, and notes that tasting is not permitted for anyone under 18.

For the bigger picture of where Port sits among the country's wines, see our [beginner's map of Portugal's wine regions](/stories/portugal-wine-regions/).

## Before you book

- Book lodge tours ahead in summer and at weekends, and check what each tasting includes.
- Check the Solar do Vinho do Porto's current opening hours on the [IVDP's page](https://www.ivdp.pt/en/tourism/solar-do-vinho-do-porto-lisbon/) before you go.
- Ask for a spittoon if you are comparing several styles; at 19 to 22 per cent alcohol, Port adds up quickly.
- If you buy a bottle to take home, check your airline's liquid rules and your home country's alcohol allowances before you pack it.
