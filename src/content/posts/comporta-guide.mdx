Drive south from the Tróia ferry and the landscape flattens and opens out. Umbrella pines line the road, then give way to wide, glassy squares of water where rice grows in summer, white storks stalking between the rows. To the west, behind a long wall of dunes, the Atlantic runs almost unbroken for kilometres. To the east lies the Sado estuary, a world of mudflats, salt marsh and wooden fishing piers.

Comporta has a reputation as a stylish hideaway, and it has earned a place on many glossy lists. Look past that and you find something simpler and more lasting: a farming and fishing parish of rice, pine, sand and sky, where the main event is still the light at the end of the day.

## The lie of the land

Comporta is a parish in the municipality of Alcácer do Sal. The [municipality's page on the parish](https://cm-alcacerdosal.pt/municipio/concelho/freguesias/freguesia-da-comporta/) describes it as covering about 155 km² between the ocean coast and the Sado estuary, with fishing and agriculture, above all rice and wine, among its main activities, alongside hotels, shops and restaurants. The parish also takes in the villages of Carrasqueira, Brejos da Carregueira, Murta, Possanco, Torre and Figueiral.

<Pair a="cmp-rice-channel" b="cmp-dunes-sunset" captionA="An irrigation channel through harvested rice fields at Comporta, with an umbrella pine on the ridge." captionB="Sunset over the dunes at Comporta, with hills on the far horizon." />

The wider landscape is shaped by pine and water. The municipality's [natural heritage page](https://cm-alcacerdosal.pt/municipio/concelho/patrimonio/patrimonio-natural/) says Alcácer do Sal has the largest area of stone pine forest in Europe, and that rice is the crop most dependent on the irrigation from the Sado valley's reservoirs. The Sado itself, the same page notes, is one of the few rivers in Europe that flows from south to north, running some 180 km to its mouth near Setúbal.

## Carrasqueira's stilt pier

The single most memorable sight around Comporta is a jetty. At Carrasqueira, on the estuary shore, a maze of wooden walkways on stilts runs out over the mud to the fishermen's boats. The municipality recommends the [palafitic pier of Carrasqueira](https://cm-alcacerdosal.pt/municipio/concelho/freguesias/freguesia-da-comporta/) as a place to visit, and its [visitor page](https://cm-alcacerdosal.pt/o-que-visitar/) calls it unique in Europe.

Come at sunset, when the planks glow and the mudflats turn pink and silver. This is a working pier, so tread carefully on the old boards, keep to the main walkways and give way to anyone who is there to fish.

## The Sado estuary reserve

Much of the land and water east of Comporta lies within the Reserva Natural do Estuário do Sado. According to the [municipality of Alcácer do Sal](https://cm-alcacerdosal.pt/municipio/concelho/patrimonio/patrimonio-natural/), the reserve covers 23,160 hectares of wetland, dry land and small freshwater streams. The white stork has one of its main breeding centres in Portugal here, and the estuary holds an important community of greater flamingos, along with herons, spoonbills, birds of prey and otters. Bottlenose dolphins, which the municipality lists among the species most in need of protection, also live in the estuary.

<Figure id="cmp-sado-stakes" caption="Wooden stakes in the mud of the Sado estuary at low tide, with hills beyond." />

People have lived off this estuary for a very long time. The same page notes that human settlement goes back some five thousand years, and that local life still divides between artisanal fishing, aquaculture, salt-making, shellfish gathering, cattle and farming. Bring binoculars, walk the edges of the rice fields and marshes in the early morning, and you will see why birdwatchers love it.

## The beaches

West of the village, the beaches are the other half of Comporta's appeal: long, open stretches of pale sand backed by dunes and pines, with the Atlantic rolling in. Praia da Comporta is the obvious first stop; drive or cycle further along the coast and you can usually find more space.

<Figure id="cmp-beach-surf" size="full" caption="Atlantic surf on the long sandy beach at Comporta." />

This is open ocean, not a sheltered bay. Swim only where there is a lifeguard in season, respect the flags, and watch children closely in the surf. Keep to the boardwalks and marked paths through the dunes, which are fragile.

If you want to walk this coast properly, the Rota Vicentina begins a little further south; our guide to [walking the Rota Vicentina](/stories/walking-the-rota-vicentina/) covers the trails from Santiago do Cacém and Porto Covo down to the Algarve.

## The village and its market

Comporta village itself is small and low-key, with its parish church, the chapel of São Pedro and a handful of shops and cafés. The municipality notes that local crafts include miniature boats and models of thatched and wooden huts, patchwork blankets, lace and embroidery, and that a traditional market is held on the second Friday of each month, according to its [parish page](https://cm-alcacerdosal.pt/municipio/concelho/freguesias/freguesia-da-comporta/).

Food here leans on what the parish produces: rice, fish and shellfish from the estuary and the sea. Look for rice dishes cooked with seafood, and grilled fish at lunch. For more on Portuguese dishes worth ordering, see our guide to [what to eat in Portugal](/stories/what-to-eat-in-portugal/).

## A slow weekend

Comporta works best at an unhurried pace, and a long weekend is about right. Arrive in time for sunset at Carrasqueira on the first evening, then spend the next morning on the beach while it is still quiet. In the heat of the afternoon, retreat into the shade of the pines, or drive the back roads through the rice fields looking for storks.

On the second day, get up early for the estuary. Walk the edges of the marshes with binoculars, then head for the village for coffee and, if the calendar is on your side, the monthly market. Finish with a long lunch of rice and seafood, and one more swim before the drive home.

If you are combining Comporta with the rest of the Alentejo, it makes a natural last stop before Lisbon.

## Where to stay

Accommodation around Comporta ranges from simple rural rooms and village guesthouses to design hotels hidden among the pines. At the top end, [Sublime Comporta](https://www.sublimehotels.pt/en/stay/sublime-comporta/the-resort/) describes itself as set on a 68-hectare estate of umbrella pines, cork trees and dunes, with rooms, suites and villas scattered through the grounds and several restaurants, including a beach club.

Summer weekends fill early. If you are flexible, aim for May, June or September, and book well ahead for July and August.

## When to go

The coast is far kinder in summer than the interior. IPMA's [1991–2020 normals for Sines](https://www.ipma.pt/opencms/bin/file.data/climate-normal/cn_91-20_SINES.pdf), further down the same coast, show average August highs of about 25°C, compared with more than 33°C at [Évora](https://www.ipma.pt/opencms/bin/file.data/climate-normal/cn_91-20_EVORA.pdf) inland. Winters are mild, with January highs around 15°C at Sines.

That makes Comporta a natural pairing with the hotter Alentejo towns: a few days in [Évora](/stories/evora-guide/), then down to the sea. Our [guide to the best time to visit Portugal](/stories/best-time-to-visit-portugal/) has the month-by-month picture for the whole country.

## Getting there

Comporta is an easy trip from Lisbon, and most visitors drive. The classic route crosses the Sado mouth by ferry: [Atlantic Ferries](https://www.atlanticferries.pt/) runs the public river service between Setúbal and Tróia for cars and foot passengers, with fares and timetables on its site. From Tróia, the road runs south along the peninsula to Comporta.

You will want a car, or at least bikes, once you are here; the beaches, the village, Carrasqueira and the rice fields are spread out and public transport is thin. Our guide to [getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) covers car hire, tolls and the alternatives.

## Before you book

- Check the current Setúbal–Tróia ferry timetable and fares on [Atlantic Ferries' site](https://www.atlanticferries.pt/), and allow for queues on summer weekends.
- Book accommodation well ahead for July and August.
- Plan Carrasqueira for sunset and check the tide; the pier is most striking when the water is low.
- If you go on the second Friday of the month, catch the village market noted on the [parish page](https://cm-alcacerdosal.pt/municipio/concelho/freguesias/freguesia-da-comporta/).
- Swim only at lifeguarded beaches, and pack binoculars for the estuary birds.
