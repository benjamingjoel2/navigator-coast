Madeira looks small on the map, and then you start driving. The road out of Funchal climbs almost at once, past banana terraces and bougainvillea, into eucalyptus and then laurel forest, and twenty minutes later you can be in cloud, with the sea somewhere far below. On the north coast the roads tunnel through headlands and drop to villages squeezed between cliff and surf. Distances are short. Journeys are not.

That is the case for a road trip. Madeira's big sights are spread around the island, its weather changes from one valley to the next, and a car lets you go where the sky is clearest. Five days is enough to see the whole island at a pace that leaves room for long lunches and the odd unplanned viewpoint.

## Before you set off

Visit Madeira's own [guide to getting around](https://visitmadeira.com/en/blog/visit-madeira/everything-you-need-to-know-about-getting-around-madeira/) is candid: buses cover Funchal well, but hidden viewpoints and less accessible trails often need a car. It also warns that roads can be steep, with sharp bends and occasional narrow sections, especially in the mountains. The modern expressways along the south coast are quick; the older mountain roads are where the island shows itself, and where you need to take your time.

The rules are the same as on the mainland. The EU's [Your Europe page on Portugal's road rules](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/driving-abroad/road-rules-and-safety/portugal/index_en.htm) gives a blood alcohol limit of 0.5 g/l for standard drivers and 0.2 g/l for novice drivers, says phones may only be used hands-free, and lists a reflective vest and a warning triangle as compulsory equipment. Check both are in the hire car before you leave the airport. Our [guide to getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) covers hiring a car more generally.

One more thing to plan around is the weather. According to [Visit Madeira](https://visitmadeira.com/en/travel-info/useful-information/weather-in-madeira/), the south is usually warmer and drier, while the north and the mountains are more exposed to wind and rain. In practice you can leave Funchal in sunshine and drive into fog. Keep the itinerary loose, and swap days around if the peaks are clear.

## Day 1: Funchal, Câmara de Lobos and Cabo Girão

Start without the car. Funchal's old centre is compact, and the best way into it is through the [Mercado dos Lavradores](https://www.visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/culture-passionates/heritage/monuments-and-architecture/mercado-dos-lavradores). Visit Madeira dates the market to 24 November 1940 and credits the design to Edmundo Tavares; its façade, main door and fish hall are decorated with tile panels painted with regional scenes. Come in the morning, when the fruit stalls are at their fullest and the fish counters are busy.

In the afternoon, drive west along the coast. Câmara de Lobos is a fishing harbour of brightly painted boats tucked under the cliffs, and a short climb beyond it is [Cabo Girão](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/madeira/south-coast/camara-de-lobos/cabo-girao/). Visit Madeira gives the cape's height as 580 metres and describes the suspended glass platform, the skywalk, as one of the most visited attractions in the area. Look straight down and you will see the fajãs of Rancho and Cabo Girão, small patches of farmland at the foot of the cliff.

Madeira's famous fortified wine is made and aged in Funchal, and the lodges in the centre make a good end to the first day. Our [guide to Madeira wine](/stories/madeira-wine/) explains how it is made and where to taste it, but if you do taste, leave the car at the hotel.

## Day 2: Sunrise at Pico do Areeiro, then Santana

This is the early start, and it is worth it. [Pico do Areeiro](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/madeira/madeira-peaks/pico-do-areeiro) is the third-highest point on the island at 1,818 metres, and a road runs almost to the summit. Visit Madeira calls sunrise there a famous spectacle, weather permitting, and on clear days you can see across to Porto Santo. Its page also lists paid parking at the top.

<Figure id="mrt-cloud-pinnacles" caption="Rock pinnacles rising out of a sea of cloud in Madeira's central mountains." />

From the summit, the PR1 Vereda do Areeiro heads along the ridge towards Pico das Torres and on to Pico Ruivo, the island's highest peak. It is an exposed mountain path with steep stone steps, not a stroll; our [guide to the Pico do Arieiro to Pico Ruivo walk](/stories/pico-arieiro-to-pico-ruivo/) covers it in detail. If you plan to walk it, or any other classified trail, check two things first. Visit Madeira's [list of walks](https://visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/nature-seekers/notice-to-walkers-and-list-of-walks/) shows whether each route is open, restricted or closed, and IFCN's [FAQ on fees and reservations](https://ifcn.madeira.gov.pt/en/atividades-de-natureza/percursos-pedestres-recomendados/faq-s-taxas-percursos-pedestres-classificados/frequently-asked-questions-fees-classified-walking-routes.html) explains that classified trails are booked online by entry time slot, that everyone must register, and that non-resident visitors pay a fee.

<Pair a="mrt-ridges-cloud" b="mrt-mountain-road" captionA="Cloud pouring through the valleys between the high peaks." captionB="A mountain road switchbacking up through Madeira's high moorland." />

Drop down the north side to Santana. The village is known for its triangular thatched houses, which [Visit Madeira](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/madeira/north-coast/santana/casas-de-santana/) describes as built of wood, with steep roofs that shed the rain and thatch that once made use of the wheat and rye harvest. They are painted in bright blue, red and white, and the tourism board points visitors to the Núcleo de Casas Típicas de Santana to learn more.

<Figure id="mrt-santana-house" size="column" caption="A thatched house in Santana, its red window framed in blue." />

The north-east is a good place for a night away from Funchal. [Quinta do Furão](https://www.quintadofurao.com/en/Home) is a hotel with a restaurant on top of a cliff at Achada do Gramacho, in Santana, and is one of several rural places to stay along this coast.

## Day 3: The north coast to Porto Moniz

Today is about the road itself. Head west along the north coast through São Vicente and Seixal, where the old road clings to the cliffs above a black-sand shore and waterfalls spill off the green walls of the valleys. Newer tunnels make the journey faster, but where you can, take the coastal sections and stop at the viewpoints.

Beyond Seixal the road loops down to the sea at Ribeira da Janela, where a tall rock stack stands just offshore, and then you are in Porto Moniz. Its [natural pools](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/madeira/north-coast/porto-moniz/natural-pools-of-porto-moniz/) are the town's calling card: Visit Madeira explains that seawater flows in naturally through the volcanic rock, and lists a children's pool, changing rooms, a bar, a first-aid post and parking. On a rough day the Atlantic breaks over the outer rocks while swimmers float in the calm water behind.

<Figure id="mrt-porto-moniz-pools" size="full" caption="The natural pools at Porto Moniz, where Atlantic swell breaks on black volcanic rock." />

## Day 4: The high plateau and the west

Climb from Porto Moniz onto Paul da Serra, the wide, open plateau at the island's centre. After two days of ravines it feels almost like moorland: big skies, low scrub, wind turbines, and on a fine day views to both coasts. If it is clouded in, it can feel very bleak indeed, so check the sky before you commit.

Rabaçal, on the plateau's edge, is the starting point for some of Madeira's best-known levada walks. When we checked Visit Madeira's [list of walks](https://visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/nature-seekers/notice-to-walkers-and-list-of-walks/) in November 2026, several routes in the Rabaçal network were marked closed, as was the Levada do Caldeirão Verde in the north-east. Statuses change, so look again the evening before, and never walk a closed route.

Spend the afternoon on the west coast, dropping down to the villages of Calheta and Ponta do Sol, which catch the evening sun. The drive back to Funchal along the south coast expressway is quick.

## Day 5: A slow last day

Keep the final day flexible. If the mountains were in cloud on day two, this is your second chance at Pico do Areeiro. If they were clear, spend the morning in Funchal's old town, then drive east towards Machico and the airport for your flight.

We would avoid packing in a long walk on the last day. Mountain weather can close routes at short notice, and you do not want to be rushing to a check-in desk after a trail that took longer than planned. If you are combining Madeira with another island trip, our comparison of [the Azores and Madeira](/stories/azores-or-madeira/) looks at how the two fit together.

## How the days fit together

| Day | Route | Highlights |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Funchal, Câmara de Lobos | Market, harbour, Cabo Girão skywalk |
| 2 | Pico do Areeiro, Santana | Sunrise, ridge views, thatched houses |
| 3 | North coast to Porto Moniz | Seixal, Ribeira da Janela, natural pools |
| 4 | Paul da Serra, west coast | Plateau, Rabaçal (if open), Calheta |
| 5 | Funchal or the peaks | Flexible day before your flight |

If you only have a long weekend, days one, two and three give you the island's essentials. For when to go, our [guide to the best time to visit Portugal](/stories/best-time-to-visit-portugal/) includes the islands.

## Before you book

- Hire the smallest car that fits your group; narrow mountain roads and tight village parking reward it.
- Check that the hire car carries a reflective vest and warning triangle, as listed on [Your Europe](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/driving-abroad/road-rules-and-safety/portugal/index_en.htm).
- Before any walk, check its status on [Visit Madeira's list](https://visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/nature-seekers/notice-to-walkers-and-list-of-walks/) and, for classified trails, book a slot as set out by [IFCN](https://ifcn.madeira.gov.pt/en/atividades-de-natureza/percursos-pedestres-recomendados/faq-s-taxas-percursos-pedestres-classificados/frequently-asked-questions-fees-classified-walking-routes.html).
- Pack layers and a rain jacket, even in summer: the mountains and the north coast can be cool, wet and windy while Funchal is in sunshine.
- Plan one night outside Funchal, on the north coast or in the west, to cut down on long return drives.
