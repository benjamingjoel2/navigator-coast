Twenty minutes inland from the coast, the Algarve changes colour. The ochre cliffs and white apartment blocks give way to orange groves, then to low hills of cork oak and eucalyptus, and finally to a dark, rounded mountain that seems to belong somewhere much further north. On a hot August afternoon, when the beaches are packed, the air up here smells of resin and wild herbs and is noticeably cooler.

<Figure id="ms-silves-castle-walls" size="full" caption="The battlements of Silves castle, once the stronghold of the Algarve's principal city." />

This is the Algarve's hill country, and two places sum it up. Silves, on the Arade river, was once the most important city in the region, and its red sandstone castle still dominates the skyline. Monchique, higher up in the serra that bears its name, is a mountain town of steep lanes, spa waters and long views to the sea. You can see both in a day from the coast, but they reward a slower visit.

## Silves: the old capital

It is hard to believe now, but this quiet riverside town was once the region's principal city. The Diocese of the Algarve's [historical note](https://diocese-algarve.pt/nota-historica/) records that after the Christian conquest the Portuguese created a bishopric at Silves, briefly from 1189 and again from 1251. As Silves declined and the coast grew in importance, the cathedral was eventually transferred to Faro, a move that took effect in 1577.

The town climbs a hill above the Arade, a tumble of whitewashed and pastel houses topped by the castle walls. Lanes lined with orange trees wind upwards towards the old centre, and every turn seems to frame a church tower or a glimpse of the river.

## The castle

Silves castle is the reason most people come, and it does not disappoint. According to the Algarvensis UNESCO Global Geopark, which covers Silves, the castle is an [Islamic fortress mentioned in historical sources from the 10th century onwards](https://geoparquealgarvensis.pt/patrimonio-cultura/56-patrimonio-arquitetonico/839-castelo-de-silves-en). It is irregular in plan, built of rammed earth faced with blocks of the local red sandstone, the Grés de Silves, and has ten towers. Inside, the Geopark notes, you can see a large cistern, storage silos and the ruins of old palace dwellings.

Walk the patrol path along the walls for views over the rooftops, the river plain and the hills rolling north towards Monchique. Go early or late on a summer day: the stone soaks up the heat, and there is little shade on top.

## The cathedral and the old town

Just below the castle, Silves Cathedral is a sober Gothic building. The Geopark describes it as a [13th-century church with three naves](https://geoparquealgarvensis.pt/en/patrimonio-cultura/56-patrimonio-arquitetonico/829-se-de-silves-en), given a Baroque appearance by alterations in the 18th century, and notes that it was the seat of the bishopric until 1577. Among its tombs, the same page singles out that of King João II, who was buried here between 1495 and 1499. The Geopark lists weekday visiting hours; check them before you go, as they can change.

Also in Silves stands the Cruz de Portugal. The Geopark describes it as a [white limestone cross around 3 m high](https://geoparquealgarvensis.pt/en/patrimonio-cultura/56-patrimonio-arquitetonico/838-cruz-de-portugal-en), carved with Christ crucified on one side and a Pietà on the other, in late Gothic style.

Between the castle and the cathedral, on Rua do Castelo, is [Café Inglês](https://cafeingles.com.pt/). Its own site describes it as occupying a late-19th-century building of oriental inspiration in the historic centre, with several terraces; it opens as a café, restaurant and music venue, so check its days before you plan lunch.

## The river and the old bridge

Down at the water's edge, a riverside walk follows the Arade under olive trees. The old bridge here is often called Roman, but the Geopark says the evidence points to a [medieval construction of the mid-14th century](https://geoparquealgarvensis.pt/en/patrimonio-cultura/56-patrimonio-arquitetonico/830-ponte-velha-de-silves-en): it is 76 m long, with five round arches built from the same red sandstone as the castle. It is a lovely spot in the evening, when the walls above catch the last of the sun.

## Into the serra

North of Silves the road climbs into the Serra de Monchique, and the landscape closes in. The Via Algarviana, the [long-distance path](https://viaalgarviana.org/menu/65/gr13---via-algarviana) that crosses the Algarve from Alcoutim to Cape St Vincent, has a sector from Silves to Monchique, and its [route notes](https://viaalgarviana.org/208/setor-10---silves-a-monchique---3210-km) describe a mostly forested landscape of cork oak, eucalyptus, pine and strawberry tree, along with rare and endemic plants such as the Monchique oak. They also note that outcrops of nepheline syenite, the characteristic rock of the serra, start to appear on the way up.

That sector is a serious undertaking: the Via Algarviana gives it as 32.1 km, graded very difficult, with around 1,718 m of ascent, roughly eight hours of walking and no water along the way. Most visitors will drive. If you do want a taste of walking here, our [guide to the Via Algarviana](/stories/via-algarviana/) has more on the route and how it is organised.

## Monchique and Fóia

Monchique town sits in a fold of the mountain, a jumble of white houses, steep cobbled lanes and terraced gardens. It feels more like a hill town in the Beiras than the Algarve, and the air is noticeably fresher.

<Figure id="ms-monchique-town" size="column" caption="Monchique's houses tucked into the wooded slopes of the serra." />

Above it rises Fóia. The Via Algarviana's notes for the [Monchique to Marmelete sector](https://viaalgarviana.org/209/setor-11---monchique-a-marmelete---1480-km) state that the antennas on Fóia mark the highest point of the Serra de Monchique and of the Algarve, at 902 m. A road climbs to the summit area, and on a clear day the view runs out over the hills to the coast. The neighbouring summit of Picota, at 774 m, is described in the [Silves to Monchique notes](https://viaalgarviana.org/208/setor-10---silves-a-monchique---3210-km) as the second-highest point in the Algarve and one of the steepest in the serra, with a 360-degree view from the top.

The mountain has its own flavours, too. The Via Algarviana lists medronho, the spirit distilled from the fruit of the strawberry tree, among the local products, and its walkers' notes end with a suggestion to ask for one after dinner. Treat it with respect: it is strong. For more on what to eat across the region, see our [guide to what to eat in Portugal](/stories/what-to-eat-in-portugal/).

## Caldas de Monchique

Below the town, in a wooded valley, is the spa village of Caldas de Monchique. The [Caldas de Monchique Villa Termal Spa Resort](https://www.monchiquetermalresort.com/) is built around the spa's thermal waters; its own site notes that the therapeutic properties of the waters have been recognised since Roman times, and describes four-star rooms looking out over the pool, the old square or the serra. Even if you are not staying, the shaded square and the cool of the valley make it a welcome stop on a hot day.

## Getting around and staying safe

You will want a car for the serra. Roads are narrow and winding, and the climb to Fóia is steep. The EU's [Your Europe page on Portugal's road rules](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/driving-abroad/road-rules-and-safety/portugal/index_en.htm) gives the drink-drive limit as 0.5 g/l for standard drivers and 0.2 g/l for novice and professional drivers, which matters if medronho is on the table.

Summer brings real fire risk to these forested hills. The UK government's [travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/portugal/safety-and-security) warns of an increased risk of wildfires from April to October, advises following the authorities' instructions and calling 112 if you see a fire, and notes that starting a fire, even by accident, is illegal.

Silves and Monchique are an easy day trip from the western Algarve. Our [Lagos guide](/stories/lagos-guide/) and our [guide to where to stay in the Algarve](/stories/where-to-stay-in-the-algarve/) can help you pick a coastal base.

## Before you book

- Check the cathedral's visiting hours on the [Geopark's page](https://geoparquealgarvensis.pt/en/patrimonio-cultura/56-patrimonio-arquitetonico/829-se-de-silves-en) and plan the castle for early or late in the day.
- Hire a car for the serra, and decide in advance who is driving if you plan to try medronho.
- Pick a clear day for Fóia; cloud can sit on the summit even when the coast is sunny.
- In summer, check the local fire risk and any road closures before heading into the hills.
- If you plan to walk any part of the Via Algarviana, read the sector notes first: some stretches are long, with no water.
