Ten kilometres inland, the Algarve most visitors know simply disappears. The apartment blocks and golf courses fall away behind you, the road narrows, and you are in a different country: rounded hills furred with cistus and cork oak, whitewashed villages with a church, a café and a few dozen houses, and a silence broken mainly by goat bells and wind. In spring the hillsides are purple and white with flowers and the air smells of resin and wild herbs.

The Via Algarviana is the long-distance path that strings this interior together, from the Spanish border to the end of Europe. It is one of Portugal's most rewarding walks and, compared with the coastal trails, one of its least crowded.

## The route at a glance

The Via Algarviana is a waymarked Grande Randonnée, the GR13. According to the [route's official site](https://viaalgarviana.org/en/menu/65/gr13---via-algarviana), it runs for about 300 kilometres from Alcoutim, on the River Guadiana, to Cabo de São Vicente, divided into 14 sectors. On the way it crosses the Algarve's three ranges, the Serra do Caldeirão, the Serra de Monchique and the Espinhaço de Cão, as well as the limestone barrocal and part of the Southwest Alentejo and Vicentine Coast Natural Park.

<Figure id="va-alcoutim-lavender" caption="Wild lavender in flower on the hills near Alcoutim, at the eastern start of the route." />

The official sectors, as listed on the [GR13 page](https://viaalgarviana.org/en/menu/65/gr13---via-algarviana), are:

| Sector | From and to | Distance |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Alcoutim to Balurcos | 24.2 km |
| 2 | Balurcos to Furnazinhas | 14.3 km |
| 3 | Furnazinhas to Vaqueiros | 22.6 km |
| 4 | Vaqueiros to Cachopo | 14.9 km |
| 5 | Cachopo to Barranco do Velho | 29.5 km |
| 6 | Barranco do Velho to Salir | 14 km |
| 7 | Salir to Alte | 16.4 km |
| 8 | Alte to São Bartolomeu de Messines | 20.6 km |
| 9 | São Bartolomeu de Messines to Silves | 29 km |
| 10 | Silves to Monchique | 32.1 km |
| 11 | Monchique to Marmelete | 14.8 km |
| 12 | Marmelete to Bensafrim | 30 km |
| 13 | Bensafrim to Vila do Bispo | 29.7 km |
| 14 | Vila do Bispo to Cabo de São Vicente | 16.4 km |

The [planning page](https://viaalgarviana.org/en/menu/186/planning) says each sector can be done in a day, so walking the whole route takes about 14 days. It can also be ridden by mountain bike, for which the site suggests about five days.

## East: the Guadiana and the Caldeirão

The walk starts quietly. Alcoutim is a small, handsome village above the Guadiana, which forms the border with Spain here, and the first sectors head west into thinly populated hill country of schist, scrub and scattered farms.

<Figure id="va-alcoutim-church" caption="Alcoutim's parish church, near the start of the GR13 on the banks of the Guadiana." />

This is the most remote part of the Via Algarviana. Villages such as Furnazinhas, Vaqueiros and Cachopo are small and far apart, and facilities between them are limited. The reward is a sense of space that is hard to find anywhere else in southern Portugal, and a glimpse of rural life that has changed slowly. Cachopo, deep in the Serra do Caldeirão, lies within the municipality of Tavira, and our [Tavira guide](/stories/tavira-guide/) is a good starting point if you want to combine coast and hills. In town, the [Pousada Convento Tavira](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-tavira) occupies the rebuilt Convent of Nossa Senhora da Graça in the historic centre.

## The middle: barrocal villages and Silves

West of Barranco do Velho the landscape softens. The barrocal is limestone country between the mountains and the coast, with terraces of carob, almond, olive and fig, and villages that feel more prosperous and more visited. Salir and Alte are both lovely places to end a day, with fountains, small squares and views across the hills.

Silves, at the end of sector 9, is the biggest town on the route and a natural rest day. Its red sandstone castle dominates the old town, and the lanes below it are lined with orange trees.

<Pair a="va-silves-lane" b="va-monchique-convent" captionA="An orange tree leaning over a lane in the old town of Silves." captionB="The ruined convent above Monchique, on the wooded slopes of the serra." />

The stage from Silves to Monchique is the longest on the route, at 32.1 kilometres according to the [official sector list](https://viaalgarviana.org/en/menu/65/gr13---via-algarviana), and it climbs steadily into the hills. Plan it carefully, start early and carry plenty of water.

## West: Monchique to the cape

The Serra de Monchique is green and wooded, with cork oaks, chestnuts and eucalyptus, and on clear days you can see the coast from its ridges. Monchique town has a good choice of places to stay and eat, which makes it a useful staging post.

<Figure id="va-monchique-ridges" size="full" caption="Sunset over the forested ridges of the Serra de Monchique." />

Beyond Marmelete the route crosses the Espinhaço de Cão and drops gradually towards the west coast, through Bensafrim and Vila do Bispo, before the final walk out across the windswept plateau to Cabo de São Vicente and its lighthouse. Arriving at the cape after two weeks of hills is a fine way to finish. Our guide to [Sagres and Cape St Vincent](/stories/sagres-and-cape-st-vincent/) covers the area, and the [Pousada Sagres](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-sagres) describes itself as having views of the Sagres fortress and of the lighthouse at Cabo de São Vicente.

## When to walk

The official [planning advice](https://viaalgarviana.org/en/menu/186/planning) recommends walking between September and May and calls spring, without a doubt, the best time of year for hiking. It is equally clear that summers are hot and dry, and not the right time to set off; high temperatures make the route harder, and summer is also the season of greatest fire risk.

Winter can be lovely too, with clear, cool days, though shorter daylight means less flexibility on the long sectors. Our [Algarve in winter](/stories/algarve-in-winter/) piece gives a fuller picture of the cooler months.

## Water, fire and safety

This is the part to take seriously. The [planning page](https://viaalgarviana.org/en/menu/186/planning) advises bringing water and food in double quantities, because many sectors have no supply points. It also warns that over a large stretch of the route there is no mobile network coverage, so maps and tracks should be downloaded before you set off. Tell someone your route each day, and give your emergency contacts to your accommodation so they can raise the alarm if you do not arrive. In an emergency, call 112.

Fire is the other big consideration. The UK government's [Portugal safety advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/portugal/safety-and-security) notes an increased risk of wildfires from April to October, that the authorities may evacuate areas and close roads, and that starting a fire, even by accident, is illegal. The Via Algarviana's own advice is to check the alert level for your day and area on [IPMA's rural fire danger map](https://www.ipma.pt/en/riscoincendio/rcm.pt/), which also shows which activities are restricted in each municipality.

## Waymarks, maps and the app

The route is signposted with direction arrows, signposts and interpretive panels, and the [official route page](https://viaalgarviana.org/en/menu/65/gr13---via-algarviana) notes that GR waymarks, certified by the Federação de Campismo e Montanhismo de Portugal, are also painted on rocks and other natural surfaces. Even so, the planning advice is to carry GPS or other mapping. Guides, maps and track files are available from the [guides and maps page](https://viaalgarviana.org/en/menu/283/guides-and-maps), and the free app works offline.

## Getting on and off the route

You do not have to walk the whole thing. The [official connections](https://viaalgarviana.org/en/menu/66/connections-to-the-via-algarviana) are linking paths from the coast and from railway stations, which make it possible to walk a few sectors as a shorter trip. They include a 10.1-kilometre link from Lagos railway station to Bensafrim, a 28.7-kilometre link from Loulé station to Salir, and links from the stations at Mexilhoeira Grande to Monchique and Parchal to Silves. Our [guide to getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) explains how the Algarve's regional trains fit in.

A popular shorter option is the western third, from Silves or Monchique to the cape, which packs mountain, barrocal and coast into about five days. Walkers who want the full wilderness experience tend to favour the eastern sectors from Alcoutim. If you enjoy this kind of walking, the [Rota Vicentina](/stories/walking-the-rota-vicentina/) on the Atlantic coast makes a natural follow-on.

## Before you book

- Plan for the official season, September to May, with spring the favourite; avoid high summer.
- Book accommodation ahead in the small eastern villages, where options are few.
- Download the app, maps and tracks before you start; coverage is patchy.
- Carry double the water and food you think you need, and check where you can refill each day.
- Check [IPMA's fire danger map](https://www.ipma.pt/en/riscoincendio/rcm.pt/) the evening before each stage.
- Leave your route and emergency contacts with your accommodation, and remember 112.
