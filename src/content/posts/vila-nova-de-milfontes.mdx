At low tide the Mira seems to hesitate before it reaches the sea. It spreads out into a wide, glassy pool, pale green over the sandbanks, with dunes on one side and a white town on the other, and only at the very end does it narrow and slip out through the surf. Fishing boats swing on their moorings. Somebody is always paddling across. Behind it all, the Atlantic keeps up its low roar.

Vila Nova de Milfontes is the best-known town on the Alentejo coast, and in August it is busy. But it is built on a simple, generous idea: two kinds of water side by side, river and ocean, with a beach for every mood. Come in spring or early autumn and you get the place at its gentlest.

## A short history of a river town

Odemira municipality's [page on the parish](https://turismo.cm-odemira.pt/o-concelho/freguesias/vila-nova-de-milfontes) tells the story briskly. The town was founded by royal decree of King João II in 1486 and grew up at the mouth of the Mira around its river-and-sea port, which for centuries served as an outer harbour for Odemira upstream. Life by the sea was not peaceful: pirates and corsairs were a constant threat, and in 1590 a corsair attack destroyed the settlement. The **Forte de São Clemente** was built between 1599 and 1602 to protect the town and its shipping.

The same page records a stranger claim to fame. On 7 April 1924 the aviators Brito Paes and Sarmento Beires took off from the Campo dos Coitos near Milfontes on the first flight between Portugal and Macau. A monument to them stands on the Praça da Barbacã, next to the fort.

## The beaches: river or ocean

This is the decision you will make every morning. The municipality singles out the long sandy beaches of the parish, which it contrasts with the cliff-bound coves further south: **Aivados** at the northern edge of Odemira, **Malhão**, and, close to the town, **Farol**, **Franquia** and **Furnas**.

Some of these face the estuary and some the open sea, and the difference is obvious the moment you arrive: sheltered, still water on one side, rolling surf on the other. The municipality's [beach-season page](https://turismo.cm-odemira.pt/o-concelho/a-seguranca-e-a-sua-praia-epoca-balnear) notes that its 55 km or so of coast lies entirely within the Southwest Alentejo and Vicentine Coast Natural Park, and that it includes Blue Flag beaches, accessible beaches, urban beaches and wild ones. Swim at lifeguarded beaches during the bathing season and take the flags seriously; the Atlantic here is not a lake, even when the river looks like one.

## On the water

The municipality's own suggestion for a visit is simple: the beaches, and a [boat trip on the Mira](https://turismo.cm-odemira.pt/o-concelho/freguesias/vila-nova-de-milfontes). Upstream, the estuary narrows between low wooded banks, and the town quickly falls away behind you. Kayaks and small boats are the natural way to explore, and local activity companies offer both. Paddle with the tide rather than against it, and ask locally about where the current runs strongest near the mouth.

For the view from above, the natural park's own guide makes a point of it: the mouth of the Mira [seen from the lighthouse, or even from the bridge](https://www.icnf.pt/api/file/doc/82e9b8a54a40ef63), is a sight you do not forget. We would add only that sunset is the time to try it.

## Walking the coast

Milfontes sits on the **Fishermen's Trail**, the coastal branch of the Rota Vicentina, which follows the paths that local people have long used to reach beaches and fishing spots. Rota Vicentina's [trail page](https://rotavicentina.com/en/fishermens-trail/) describes 13 one-day stages, walkable in either direction between September and June, and warns that around 60 to 70 percent of it is on sand, which makes it harder than the distances suggest. The cliffs are also eroding, so stay on the marked path and away from the edges.

From Milfontes you can walk north towards Porto Covo or south towards Almograve, or simply do a few hours out and back along the cliffs. Our full guide to [walking the Rota Vicentina](/stories/walking-the-rota-vicentina/) covers stages, signposting and luggage transfers in detail.

## The town itself

Milfontes is small enough to learn in an afternoon. The municipality picks out three sights within the town: the fort with its barbican, the parish church and the Capela de São Sebastião. In between are lanes of low houses, whitewashed and edged in blue or ochre, that run down towards the water. Evenings are for the riverfront, watching the light drain out of the estuary.

## A slow day in Milfontes

If you only have one full day, this is how we would spend it. Start early on the riverfront, when the estuary is still and the boats are heading out. Walk up to the fort and the aviators' monument on the Praça da Barbacã, then down to a river beach for the morning, while the sand is cool and the water calm.

After lunch, drive or cycle north to one of the long ocean beaches the municipality highlights, Malhão or Aivados, where the sand runs on and on and the crowds thin out quickly. Come back for the last light over the river mouth, and dinner late, as the Portuguese do.

## Where to eat

Fish and shellfish dominate, and the Alentejo's pork and bread dishes are never far away. Our guide to [Portuguese seafood](/stories/portuguese-seafood/) is useful for decoding menus. Three places listed on the municipality's tourism site:

- **Tasca do Celso**, on Rua dos Aviadores, has its [own website](https://tascadocelso.pt/); the [municipal listing](https://turismo.cm-odemira.pt/pages/1066?poi_id=325) names specialities including prawn açorda, monkfish rice and carne de porco à alentejana, and notes it closes on Mondays.
- **A Fateixa**, on Largo do Cais by the river, is a café-restaurant whose [listing](https://turismo.cm-odemira.pt/pages/1066?poi_id=330) mentions grilled fresh fish, tuna steak and squid.
- **Porto das Barcas**, on Estrada do Canal outside the centre, is listed for [grilled fish and meat](https://turismo.cm-odemira.pt/pages/1066?poi_id=302); it closes on Tuesdays in the low season.

## Where to stay

- **Casa do Adro da Igreja**, on Rua Diário de Notícias near the parish church, is a turismo de habitação guesthouse; see [its website](https://www.casadoadro.com.pt/).
- **Hotel HS Milfontes Beach** is on the Avenida Marginal above the estuary; the [municipal listing](https://turismo.cm-odemira.pt/pages/883?poi_id=268) has its contact details.

The municipal tourism site also lists campsites and tourist apartments, and our [walking guide](/stories/walking-the-rota-vicentina/) names a campsite on the edge of town.

## Getting there and when to go

Odemira municipality's [travel page](https://turismo.cm-odemira.pt/o-concelho/como-chegar) points to the national coach network Rede Expressos, Rodoviária do Alentejo and CP for public transport, with Lisbon and Faro the nearest airports; Rota Vicentina's [transport page](https://rotavicentina.com/en/transports/) gathers bus links along the whole route. Most visitors drive, and a car makes it easy to reach the quieter beaches north and south of town.

July and August are the busy months. For walking and quieter beaches, spring and early autumn are kinder, as our guide to [the best time to visit Portugal](/stories/best-time-to-visit-portugal/) explains. If you are pairing Milfontes with the south, our [Algarve and Alentejo week](/stories/algarve-and-alentejo-week/) shows how the coast fits together.

## Before you book

- In July and August, book accommodation early and expect crowds on the river beaches.
- Check the sea and weather forecast on [IPMA](https://www.ipma.pt/en/) before swimming or walking the cliffs, and swim only where lifeguards are on duty in season.
- If you plan to walk, read the [Fishermen's Trail notes](https://rotavicentina.com/en/fishermens-trail/) first; much of it is soft sand and it is not recommended for anyone with vertigo.
- Without a car, check current coach times via the [municipality's travel page](https://turismo.cm-odemira.pt/o-concelho/como-chegar) before you commit to a date.
- Restaurants here keep seasonal hours; confirm closing days on the listings before you set out for dinner.
