At first light on Pico do Arieiro, the island disappears. Cloud fills the valleys below the summit like a white sea, and the ridges of the central massif stick up through it as black, broken islands. Then the sun clears the horizon, the rock turns from charcoal to rust, and a thin line of path shows itself, threading along the crest towards a higher, rounder summit a few kilometres away. That is Pico Ruivo, the top of Madeira, and the path between the two is the most famous walk on the island.

It is also the most regulated. The summit's name is spelled both Arieiro and Areeiro; the official route name uses Areeiro. Since 2026 the route runs one way only for most of its length, you need a timed booking, and you have to sort out your own way home from the far end. None of that should put you off. It just means planning this walk properly, rather than turning up on the day.

## Three summits on one ridge

The walk is the classified route **PR1 Vereda do Areeiro**. [Visit Madeira](https://visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/nature-seekers/activities/hiking/pr-1-vereda-do-areeiro/) describes it as a trail connecting the island's three highest peaks: Pico do Areeiro at 1,818 metres, Pico das Torres at 1,851 metres, and Pico Ruivo at 1,862 metres. It is 6.1 km from the Pico do Areeiro viewpoint to Pico Ruivo, rated moderate, and the tourism board allows about three hours.

Pico Ruivo is the highest point in the archipelago. [Visit Madeira](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/madeira/madeira-peaks/pico-ruivo/) calls it the third-highest point in Portugal, and says that on clear days you can see Porto Santo and the Desertas from the top.

You can drive to Pico do Areeiro, which is one reason it is so busy. [Visit Madeira's page on the summit](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/madeira/madeira-peaks/pico-do-areeiro/) notes that sunrise here, weather permitting, draws more and more visitors and photographers, and that the view takes in the central range, Ponta de São Lourenço, Curral das Freiras and, on clear days, Porto Santo.

## The route, stage by stage

**Pico do Areeiro to Pedra Rija.** The first 1.2 km leaves the summit and follows the ridge to the Pedra Rija viewpoint. This section stays two-way, so you will meet people coming back.

**Pedra Rija to Pico das Torres.** Beyond the Pedra Rija viewpoint the route becomes one-way, towards Pico Ruivo. Visit Madeira describes tunnels cut through the volcanic tuff, once used to shelter shepherds and cattle, and steep slopes along the way. The path does not go over Pico das Torres: it goes around it, by what the tourism board calls "a steep staircase carved in the rock". This is the part of the walk your knees will remember.

**The final climb.** Visit Madeira saves its warning for last: "the greatest challenge of Vereda do Areeiro is saved for last", the climb to the Casa de Abrigo do Pico Ruivo, the mountain shelter below the summit. From the shelter, a short path leads to the top.

<Pair a="pkr-pr1-stairs-cloud" b="pkr-pr1-ridge-path" captionA="A railed staircase on PR1, climbing into cloud." captionB="The path climbing a rocky crest between the peaks." />

The landscape is mostly high-altitude heath and bare rock. Visit Madeira notes that the massif is part of the Natura 2000 network and that birds found only in Macaronesia or in the archipelago live up here. Keep an eye out for small birds on the rocks around the shelter.

## The one-way rule, and getting back

This is the single most important thing to understand before you go. According to [IFCN's FAQ](https://ifcn.madeira.gov.pt/en/atividades-de-natureza/percursos-pedestres-recomendados/faq-s-taxas-percursos-pedestres-classificados/frequently-asked-questions-fees-classified-walking-routes.html), PR1 fully reopened on 1 May 2026. It is two-way between Pico do Areeiro and Pedra Rija only. From Pedra Rija to Pico Ruivo it is one-way, and you may not walk back the same way.

From Pico Ruivo you leave by one of two other classified paths:

- **PR 1.2 Vereda do Pico Ruivo**, down to Achada do Teixeira. [Visit Madeira](https://visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/nature-seekers/activities/hiking/pr-12-vereda-do-pico-ruivo/) gives it as 2.8 km, rated moderate. It starts by the rock formation known as the Homem em Pé, the Standing Man, and it is the shortest way off the mountain to a road.
- **PR 1.1 Vereda da Ilha**, a much longer descent from the shelter to Ilha. [Visit Madeira](https://visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/nature-seekers/activities/hiking/pr-11-vereda-da-ilha/) gives it as 8.2 km and about three hours, with a 1,376-metre change in height, dropping from the mountain heath into the Laurissilva forest.

A third exit, PR 1.3 Vereda da Encumeada towards the west, was still closed according to the FAQ.

IFCN is explicit that it does **not** arrange your return to Pico do Areeiro. If you drive up, your car will be on the wrong side of the mountain at the end of the day. The common answers are a taxi or a pre-arranged transfer from Achada do Teixeira, a guided walk that includes transport, or two cars. Agree this before you set off, not at the summit with a flat phone.

Public transport is limited. When we checked in November 2026, the Funchal bus company, [Horários do Funchal](https://www.horariosdofunchal.pt/), had a notice on its site that its Pico do Areeiro service was suspended.

## Booking a slot

PR1 is one of IFCN's classified routes, so the 2026 booking rules apply. The [FAQ](https://ifcn.madeira.gov.pt/en/atividades-de-natureza/percursos-pedestres-recomendados/faq-s-taxas-percursos-pedestres-classificados/frequently-asked-questions-fees-classified-walking-routes.html) sets them out:

- Book a timed entry slot online on the [SIMplifica portal](https://simplifica.madeira.gov.pt/simplifica/). Registration is required for everyone, including children 12 and under, who don't pay.
- There are two PR1 tickets: one for the short out-and-back to Pedra Rija, and one for the full route to Pico Ruivo. The full ticket also covers the exits on PR 1.1 and PR 1.2.
- PR1 is not included in the combined day and multi-day rates that cover other trails.
- The full route starts at Pico do Areeiro, not at the gate near Pedra Rija.
- Refunds and changes are only possible if the authorities close or restrict the trail. Bad weather that does not close it, illness or a missed lift are not covered.

Earlier in 2026, IFCN ran the Pedra Rija to Pico Ruivo section on only some days of the week while the paving was repaired. Check the current position on the FAQ before you build a holiday around a particular day.

Sunrise at Pico do Areeiro is popular, so if you want the dawn view, book as soon as your dates are fixed.

## Weather, light and timing

[Visit Madeira](https://visitmadeira.com/en/travel-info/useful-information/weather-in-madeira/) explains that the mountains are more exposed to wind and rain than the south coast, and that you can be in fog up high while the coast is sunny. Its PR 1.2 page warns of "sudden climate variations" in this area. At over 1,800 metres it can be cold at dawn in any season, and the sun is fierce once the cloud burns off.

Check [IPMA's Madeira forecast](https://www.ipma.pt/en/otempo/prev-sam/?p=RAM) the night before, and check the trail's status on the [notice to walkers](https://visitmadeira.com/en/what-to-do/nature-seekers/notice-to-walkers-and-list-of-walks/) the morning you go. In strong wind or heavy rain, the advice is simple: turn back.

## What to carry

The tourism board's [advice to walkers](https://visitmadeira.com/en/travel-info/useful-information/advice-to-walkers/) asks for walking boots on every walk, and a day pack with waterproofs, a hat, gloves, spare clothing, food and drink and a map. On this route we would add a head torch for the tunnels (essential if you start before dawn), sunscreen, and more water than you think you need. Poles help on the staircases, especially going down.

This is a walk for people with a head for heights. The paths are well made and often railed, but the drops are real. If you are unsure, walk the out-and-back to Pedra Rija first. You will still get the best of the views.

## Before you book

- Book the right PR1 ticket on [SIMplifica](https://simplifica.madeira.gov.pt/simplifica/): out-and-back to Pedra Rija, or the full one-way route.
- Arrange how you will get from Achada do Teixeira or Ilha back to your car or hotel before you start.
- Check [IFCN's FAQ](https://ifcn.madeira.gov.pt/en/atividades-de-natureza/percursos-pedestres-recomendados/faq-s-taxas-percursos-pedestres-classificados/frequently-asked-questions-fees-classified-walking-routes.html) for current restrictions on the Pedra Rija to Pico Ruivo section.
- Check the trail status and [IPMA forecast](https://www.ipma.pt/en/otempo/prev-sam/?p=RAM) the night before and again in the morning.
- Pack warm layers, a head torch, sun protection and plenty of water.
