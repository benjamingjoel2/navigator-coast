In mid-November, all over the Alentejo, people gather around great clay jars and wait for the first taste. The wine has been in them since the harvest, and by long tradition the jars are not opened until St Martin's Day. Then the cellars and taverns fill, and in many of them the new wine runs out quickly.

That ritual tells you most of what you need to know about wine in the Alentejo. It is old, it is sociable, and it is rooted in farming rather than ceremony. The plains south and east of Évora are now home to some of Portugal's most polished estates as well, and the Alentejo Wine Route is the easiest way into both worlds.

## How the wine route works

The route is run by the regional wine commission, and its starting point is a tasting room in the middle of Évora. The commission's [wine route page](https://www.vinhosdoalentejo.pt/en/wine-route/about-wine-route/) describes it sitting among the monuments of the UNESCO-listed historic centre, offering daily tastings, wine to buy and help arranging visits to member wineries. The [visit us page](https://www.vinhosdoalentejo.pt/en/wine-route/visit-us/) gives the address as Rua 5 de Outubro, 88, lists its weekday and Saturday hours (it is closed on Sundays and holidays) and advises booking ahead.

We like to start there on the first afternoon. A tasting across several producers is the quickest way to work out whether you lean towards the region's soft, sun-ripened reds or its fresher whites, and the staff can then point you to estates that suit your taste.

The route also publishes a [list of member wineries](https://www.vinhosdoalentejo.pt/en/wine-route/alentejo-wine-route/), with icons showing which offer visits, tastings, accommodation or a restaurant. It spans a big territory, from Portalegre in the north to Beja and Moura in the south, so pick one or two areas rather than trying to see everything.

## The land and the grapes

The tourism board's [wine page](https://www.visitalentejo.pt/en/alentejo/culture/wine/) makes a few points worth knowing before you taste. Vines were grown here before the Romans arrived, and today the region counts more than 250 producers. The soils are a patchwork of clay, schist, granite, marble and limestone. Many of the grapes are local: for whites, **Antão Vaz**, **Arinto** and **Roupeiro**; for reds, **Alicante Bouschet**, **Aragonez** and **Trincadeira**.

What you will notice on the ground is space. Vineyards share the plains with olive groves, wheat and the cork and holm oaks that give the Alentejo its look, and the estates, called herdades, can be very large. Summers are hot, so most cellar visits feel like a welcome retreat into the cool.

## Talha: wine the Roman way

The Alentejo's oldest wine style is made in **talhas**, large clay amphorae. The tourism board describes [vinho de talha](https://www.visitalentejo.pt/en/alentejo/culture/wine/) as made in the way the Romans made wine more than 2,000 years ago. The wine commission's [talha site](https://vinhodetalha.vinhosdoalentejo.pt/) explains the calendar: the wine is kept in the talha until St Martin's Day, 11 November, when tradition says the jars are opened, and in the St Martin's festivities of the Alentejo, talha wine is king. It can be white, red, or a blend of white and red grapes known as petroleiro.

To see talhas at work, two names on the route are a good start:

- **Adega José de Sousa**, in the centre of Reguengos de Monsaraz, keeps a traditional underground cellar with 114 clay amphorae and two treading troughs alongside its modern winery, according to its [wine route listing](https://www.vinhosdoalentejo.pt/en/producers/adega-jose-de-sousa/). It belongs to José Maria da Fonseca.
- **Gerações da Talha**, in Vila de Frades near Vidigueira, is a small producer [listed on the route](https://www.vinhosdoalentejo.pt/en/producers/geracoes-da-talha/) and named for the amphora tradition.

If you travel in November, ask the tasting room in Évora which villages are holding St Martin's celebrations that year.

## Around Évora

You do not need to go far. **Adega Cartuxa**, the winery of the Fundação Eugénio de Almeida, is at Quinta de Valbom on the edge of the city. Its [wine tourism page](https://www.cartuxa.pt/en/wine-tourism/) says the building lies about 2 km from the historic centre and 200 m from the Cartuxa monastery that gave the wine its name. It was a retreat house of the Jesuits, who taught at the University of Évora, became a wine press in 1776 and was acquired by the Eugénio de Almeida family in the 19th century. Guided visits last about an hour and a half and end with a tasting of wine and olive oil; booking ahead is recommended.

For more on the city itself, see our [Évora guide](/stories/evora-guide/).

## Reguengos de Monsaraz and the lake

South-east of Évora, the land tilts towards the Guadiana and the great Alqueva reservoir, with the walled hill village of Monsaraz above it all. Reguengos de Monsaraz is the main town.

**Herdade do Esporão** is the big name here. The route's [listing for Esporão](https://www.vinhosdoalentejo.pt/en/producers/esporao/) mentions winery tours with tasting, an olive mill tour, trails and cycling, a wine bar and a shop; current options are on [Esporão's visits page](https://esporao.com/en/tourism?type=tastings-and-visits). Pair a visit with an evening in Monsaraz, which we cover in [Monsaraz and Alqueva](/stories/monsaraz-and-alqueva/).

## Estremoz and Borba

North-east of Évora, the marble towns of Estremoz, Borba and Vila Viçosa have their own wine identity. The Borba cooperative winery, **Adega de Borba**, is [on the route](https://www.vinhosdoalentejo.pt/en/producers/adega-de-borba/), and Estremoz is home to several estates, among them [J. Portugal Ramos](https://www.vinhosdoalentejo.pt/en/producers/j-portugal-ramos-vinhos/). This is easy country to combine with sightseeing; our [five-day Alentejo road trip](/stories/alentejo-road-trip/) strings these towns together.

## When to go

Each season has its own case. From mid to late summer, the tourism board says, many wineries offer [harvest experiences](https://www.visitalentejo.pt/en/alentejo/culture/wine/), from treading grapes to cellar tours, though the heat on the plains can be fierce. November brings the opening of the talhas and the St Martin's celebrations. Spring is green, mild and quiet, and arguably the most comfortable time to drive between estates; our guide to [the best time to visit Portugal](/stories/best-time-to-visit-portugal/) compares the seasons in more detail.

## Where to stay

Évora makes the best base for a first visit, with Estremoz a quieter alternative.

- **Pousada Convento Évora** occupies a former convent between the cathedral and the Roman temple; its [own page](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-evora) notes that the rooms were once monks' cells.
- **Pousada Castelo Estremoz** is inside the castle that King Dinis built for Queen Saint Isabel in the 13th century, per [Pousadas de Portugal](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-estremoz).

Several estates on the route also offer rooms; look for the accommodation icon on the [winery list](https://www.vinhosdoalentejo.pt/en/wine-route/alentejo-wine-route/).

## Tasting without driving

Distances here are long and the roads are quiet, which is exactly why it is tempting to drive after a tasting. Don't. The EU's [Your Europe guide](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/driving-abroad/road-rules-and-safety/portugal/index_en.htm) gives Portugal's blood alcohol limit as 0.5 g/l for most drivers and 0.2 g/l for new and professional drivers. Agree a designated driver, use the spittoon, or book a driver for the day. If you have tasted in the Douro, the same rules apply; our [three days in the Douro Valley](/stories/three-days-in-the-douro-valley/) has more on planning tastings around transport.

## Before you book

- Book the [Évora tasting room](https://www.vinhosdoalentejo.pt/en/wine-route/visit-us/) in advance, and remember it is closed on Sundays and public holidays.
- Reserve winery visits ahead, especially at harvest time; the tourism board notes that many wineries run [harvest experiences](https://www.visitalentejo.pt/en/alentejo/culture/wine/) from mid to late summer.
- Plan no more than two estate visits a day, with lunch in between.
- For talha wine at its most festive, aim for the days around 11 November.
- Settle who is driving before the first glass, or do not drive at all.
