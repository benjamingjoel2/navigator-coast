Drive up out of Manteigas on a clear morning and the road keeps climbing long after you think it must stop. The pine woods thin, then give way to broom and heather, then to bare granite: huge rounded boulders, stacked and split, scattered across a plateau that looks less like Portugal than somewhere far further north. The air is thin and cold even in summer. Below you, a valley runs dead straight between steep walls, the unmistakable shape of a place that ice once filled.

<Figure id="estrela-plateau" size="full" caption="Snow on the high plateau of the Serra da Estrela, with ridges fading into the distance." />

This is the Serra da Estrela, the roof of mainland Portugal, and most visitors to the country never see it. That is a shame. It is a short journey from the coast in distance and a long one in feeling, and it rewards anyone who comes for walking, for snow, for cheese, or simply for the silence.

## Why the mountains matter

The headline fact is height. The Torre plateau rises from around 1,500 metres to [1,993 metres at Alto da Torre](https://roteirodasminas.dgeg.gov.pt/en/lista-de-pontos/torre-plateau-geosite-en-1/), the highest summit in mainland Portugal, according to the national geology directorate's geosite notes.

The more interesting story is ice. The same source explains that during recent glacial cycles the Estrela carried glaciers covering up to 66 square kilometres, and that around 30,000 years ago the ice at Alto da Torre was some 90 metres thick. The flat summit let an ice field form and send glaciers down the valleys around it, and the marks are everywhere once you know to look: scooped-out hollows, scattered boulders, moraines and long U-shaped valleys.

That geology is why the range was recognised as a [UNESCO Global Geopark in 2020](https://www.globalgeoparksnetwork.org/sites/default/files/2024-04/Estrela_UGG_Annual_Report_2020-2021.pdf). Turismo Centro de Portugal describes the geopark as [covering 2,216 square kilometres across nine municipalities](https://www.centerofportugal.com/poi/serra-da-estrela-natural-park), and notes that the older Natural Park at its core was the first natural park created in Portugal.

## The Zêzere glacial valley

If you see one thing, make it this. The [Zêzere glacial valley](https://roteirodasminas.dgeg.gov.pt/en/lista-de-pontos/zezere-glacial-valley-geosite-en-1/) runs for about 10 kilometres between Covão da Ametade and the town of Manteigas, with a classic U-shaped cross-section. Its unusually straight line follows a fault that erosion exploited, and upstream it steps through a series of basins, rock bars and hanging side valleys.

<Figure id="estrela-zezere-river" caption="The river Zêzere tumbling over granite boulders in its glacial valley." />

The river Zêzere rises high on the massif, near the peak of Cântaro Magro, and Turismo Centro de Portugal lists a [walking route along the glacial valley](https://www.centerofportugal.com/poi/serra-da-estrela-natural-park) that starts in Manteigas and ends at Covão d'Ametade. Covão d'Ametade itself, a grassy hollow under the cliffs where the young river runs clear and cold, is one of the loveliest places in the range for a picnic.

If you want to understand what you are looking at, the geopark runs three interpretation centres, listed by the [DGEG's geopark page](https://roteirodasminas.dgeg.gov.pt/en/lista-de-locais/estrela-unesco-global-geopark-en-1): CISE in Seia, CITEG at Torre and CIVGLAZ in Manteigas. The last is devoted to the glacial valley on its doorstep.

## Torre, the plateau and the lakes

Torre is the top of the road as much as the top of the mountain, and it can feel busy. Go for the view and the scale of the plateau, then walk away from the car parks. The geopark lists a string of geosites up here, including the jagged [Cântaro Magro, at 1,928 metres](https://roteirodasminas.dgeg.gov.pt/en/lista-de-locais/estrela-unesco-global-geopark-en-1), and the Cântaro Gordo, which stood clear of the ice during the last glaciation.

West of Torre, the plateau is dotted with lakes. Turismo Centro de Portugal names [Lagoa Comprida, Lagoa dos Conchos and Lagoa Escura](https://www.centerofportugal.com/poi/serra-da-estrela-natural-park), several of which were later harnessed for Portugal's early hydroelectric networks, and describes a walking route across the high lagoons. On a still day the water holds the sky perfectly; on a windy one, you will understand why shepherds' capes were made of thick wool.

<Pair a="estrela-lake" b="estrela-conchos" captionA="A lake held in a glacial valley, seen from the granite high ground." captionB="The bell-mouth overflow at Covão dos Conchos, one of the plateau lakes." />

## Walking the Serra

The Estrela is serious walking country, and the routes vary from riverside strolls to long high-level days. Turismo Centro de Portugal's park page describes several, including the [Garganta de Loriga](https://www.centerofportugal.com/poi/serra-da-estrela-natural-park), a steep glacial valley on the south-western flank, a circular route across the Torre plateau, and a long trek across the upper plateau past lagoons, Penhas Douradas and Vale do Rossim.

A few principles hold for all of them:

- **Start early.** Mountain weather changes quickly, and afternoons can bring cloud or storms in summer.
- **Carry layers and water.** It is far colder at the top than in the valleys, and shade is scarce on the plateau.
- **Respect the terrain.** The granite is beautiful and unforgiving; wear proper footwear and stay on marked paths.

If you enjoy long walks, the Estrela makes a fine contrast to the coastal paths in our guide to [walking the Rota Vicentina](/stories/walking-the-rota-vicentina/): granite and ice instead of cliffs and sea.

## Snow, and driving in winter

The Serra da Estrela is where Portugal goes for snow. Turismo Centro de Portugal describes Torre as home to [the only ski resort in the country](https://www.centerofportugal.com/destination/serra-da-estrela), and on winter weekends the roads up from Covilhã and Seia fill with families coming to see it.

Those roads need care. Infraestruturas de Portugal runs a [snow-clearing centre at 1,650 metres](https://www.infraestruturasdeportugal.pt/pt-pt/inverno-em-seguranca) beside the EN 339 that operates around the clock in winter, and notes that the decision to close roads to traffic rests with the GNR, the national guard, on the centre's technical advice. Its winter safety advice is plain: check the weather forecast and road conditions before setting out, and fit winter tyres or carry snow chains if needed.

<Figure id="estrela-torre-snow" caption="The domed towers at Torre, the top of mainland Portugal, after a snowfall." />

Check [IPMA's weather warnings](https://www.ipma.pt/en/otempo/prev-sam/) for the Guarda and Castelo Branco districts before you drive up, and have a plan for the day if the summit road is closed. Our [month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Portugal](/stories/best-time-to-visit-portugal/) has more on how the seasons change across the country.

## Manteigas, Seia and the mountain towns

Manteigas sits at the bottom of the glacial valley, a compact town of stone houses and steep streets, with the walls of the valley rising directly behind it. It is the most dramatic base for walkers.

Seia, on the western side, is the gateway from Coimbra and the place to start if you are heading for the lakes. Covilhã, on the eastern slopes, is a bigger, busier town with a railway station. Turismo Centro de Portugal also singles out the [village of Sabugueiro](https://www.centerofportugal.com/destination/serra-da-estrela), set high on the western side, for its scenery.

The wool tradition survives in burel, a dense felted cloth that Turismo Centro de Portugal describes as the [fabric traditionally used for shepherds' capes](https://www.centerofportugal.com/poi/burel-factory) in central Portugal. [Burel Factory](https://www.burelfactory.com/), in the Penhas Douradas area of Manteigas, was set up in 2010 to give the cloth new life and makes rugs, cushions, bags and wall coverings from it.

## Cheese and mountain food

The mountain's most famous export is its cheese. **Queijo Serra da Estrela** is a sheep's-milk cheese registered as a [Protected Designation of Origin](https://ec.europa.eu/agriculture/eambrosia/geographical-indications-register/details/EUGI00000013223), and at its ripest the paste turns soft enough to spoon. You will find it in markets, shops and on restaurant tables across the range, often served with the top sliced away like a lid.

<Figure id="estrela-cheese" size="column" caption="A whole aged Queijo Serra da Estrela, the mountain's sheep's-milk cheese." />

Turismo Centro de Portugal also lists the region's [sausages and honey](https://www.centerofportugal.com/destination/serra-da-estrela) among its specialities. For the wider picture of regional food, see our guide to [what to eat in Portugal, region by region](/stories/what-to-eat-in-portugal/).

## Where to stay

Base yourself in Manteigas or Seia for walking, or stay high on the mountain for the views and the quiet.

- **[Pousada Serra da Estrela](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-serra-estrela)**, near Covilhã and inside the Natural Park, occupies an old sanatorium restored by the architect Eduardo Souto de Moura, according to Pousadas de Portugal. It has an indoor pool, spa and restaurant.
- **[Casa das Penhas Douradas](https://www.casadaspenhasdouradas.pt/)** is a four-star hotel at around 1,500 metres in the Penhas Douradas mountain area of Manteigas. Its rooms use burel for colour, and it has a spa, a restaurant and its own marked walking trails linking to municipal routes.

## Getting there

The Serra da Estrela is best explored by car; the towns are spread out, and the high roads are the point. From Coimbra, the western approach through Seia is the most direct. From Lisbon, the A23 towards Covilhã gives access to the eastern slopes.

By train, the Beira Baixa line runs from Lisbon to Covilhã. Services were partly suspended after storm damage earlier in 2026; [CP's line page](https://www.cp.pt/info/en/w/beira-baixa-line) says rail services resumed on 20 September 2026 under new timetables, so check the [current timetable](https://www.cp.pt/info/documents/d/cp/comboio-linha-beira-baixa-lisboa-covilha) before you plan around it. You will still need a car or taxi to reach the high ground from the station. Our guide to [getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) covers car hire, tolls and the rail network in more detail.

## Before you book

- Check [IPMA's warnings](https://www.ipma.pt/en/otempo/prev-sam/) and road conditions before driving to Torre, especially from late autumn to spring.
- Carry snow chains or use winter tyres in winter, as [Infraestruturas de Portugal advises](https://www.infraestruturasdeportugal.pt/pt-pt/inverno-em-seguranca).
- Pack warm layers whatever the season; the plateau is far colder and windier than the valleys.
- If you are coming by train, confirm the [Beira Baixa timetable](https://www.cp.pt/info/documents/d/cp/comboio-linha-beira-baixa-lisboa-covilha) and arrange onward transport from Covilhã.
- Book mountain accommodation early for winter weekends and holidays, when the snow draws crowds.
