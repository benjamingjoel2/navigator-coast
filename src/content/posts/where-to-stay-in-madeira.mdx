From the deck of the ferry, or from the window seat as the plane banks over the sea, Madeira looks like a single mountain someone has dropped into the Atlantic. The houses of Funchal climb its southern flank in a white and terracotta amphitheatre; above them the slopes turn green, then grey, then disappear into cloud. Somewhere up there the island changes completely, and on the far side it is wetter, wilder and much quieter.

That is the whole question of where to stay in Madeira. The island is small, but its two faces are very different, and the roads between them, for all their tunnels, are still mountain roads. Pick the right base and your days start at the trailhead or the sea pool. Pick the wrong one and you spend them in the car.

## How to think about it

Visit Madeira's own [accommodation pages](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-stay/) put it simply: Funchal has the largest concentration of hotels, services, restaurants and transport links, while places such as Câmara de Lobos, Santana, Porto Moniz and Calheta offer a more natural atmosphere, closer to the landscapes most people come for. Because distances are short, the tourism board notes, many visitors stay in one place and make day trips; splitting a stay between two regions gives more variety.

We lean towards the split. A few nights in Funchal, then a few on the north coast or in the sunny west, is the shape we suggest to most people, especially walkers. If you only have four or five nights, stay in Funchal and accept some early starts.

Two practical points shape everything else:

- **A car helps.** The tourism board's [guide to moving around](https://visitmadeira.com/en/travel-info/how-to-move-around/) calls car hire the best option for an independent trip. There are no toll roads, but mountain roads have many curves and need care. Buses, taxis, and ride-hailing apps such as Uber and Bolt also operate, and work best around Funchal.
- **The airport is close to everywhere east.** From the airport at Santa Cruz to Funchal takes about 20 to 25 minutes by the Aerobus, taxi, transfer or hire car, according to the same page.

If you are still deciding between the archipelagos, our [Azores or Madeira comparison](/stories/azores-or-madeira/) sets the two side by side.

## Funchal: the easy, sociable base

Funchal is Madeira's capital and its tourist, commercial and cultural centre, set in a bay on the south coast. [Visit Madeira's Funchal page](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/madeira/south-coast/funchal/) singles out the Mercado dos Lavradores, the farmers' market opened in 1940, and the Monte sledges, a form of transport dating from the early 19th century that you can still ride down from Monte.

<Figure id="mstay-funchal-from-above" caption="Funchal in late October, its rooftops running down to the harbour." />

Within the city, the choice is mostly about altitude and noise. The old town and the streets behind the seafront put you among cafés and restaurants, walkable at night. The hotel zone to the west, along Estrada Monumental and around the Lido, is quieter and has more pools and sea access. The hillside neighbourhoods above the centre have the best views and the steepest walks home; check whether your hotel runs a shuttle before you book one up there.

Our [Funchal guide](/stories/funchal-guide/) covers the city itself in more detail. A few long-established addresses worth knowing about:

- **[Reid's Palace](https://www.belmond.com/en/hotels/europe/portugal/reids-palace-madeira)**, now run by Belmond, was opened by the Reid family in 1891. It stands on the cliffs about ten minutes' drive from the centre, with subtropical gardens and a traditional afternoon tea served on a terrace over the ocean.
- **[Quinta da Casa Branca](https://www.quintacasabranca.com/)** is a family-owned hotel on a botanical estate about 2 km from the centre, pairing an early 20th-century manor house with a modern wing of glass and slate.
- **[Casa Velha do Palheiro](https://casa-velha.com/)**, in the hills above the city and about 20 minutes from the airport, was built in 1804 as a hunting lodge for the Count of Carvalhal. It sits within the Palheiro estate and its gardens, next to an 18-hole golf course, and is a member of Relais & Châteaux.

## The sunny south-west: Ponta do Sol and Calheta

West of Funchal the coast gets drier and brighter. Visit Madeira describes Ponta do Sol as [Madeira's sunniest region](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/madeira/west-coast/ponta-do-sol/), known for its beaches and typical villages, where bananas and sugar cane are grown on the terraces. The village itself is small and pretty, tucked into a ravine with a short seafront, and it makes a calm base with Funchal within reach.

Here, the **[Estalagem da Ponta do Sol](https://www.pontadosol.com/)** stands on a clifftop above the village in a renovated old quinta, with contemporary black-and-white rooms, an infinity pool and a programme of concerts and cultural events.

Further west, [Calheta](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/madeira/west-coast/calheta/) has a mild climate all year, late sunsets and a range of beaches, including its sand beach. It is also the gateway to Rabaçal, on the Paul da Serra plateau inside the Madeira Nature Park, where some of the island's best-loved levada walks begin. If walking is the point of your trip, a base around Calheta puts you much closer to those trailheads than Funchal does.

Walkers should note that, according to the tourism board's [advice to walkers](https://visitmadeira.com/en/travel-info/useful-information/advice-to-walkers/), access to the official walking routes is now subject to a fee for non-residents over 12, and trail status changes. Check both before you set out. Our guide to [levada walks for beginners](/stories/levada-walks-for-beginners/) suggests where to start.

## The north coast: Santana, São Vicente and Porto Moniz

Cross the mountains and the island turns green and dramatic: cliffs dropping straight into the sea, waterfalls, laurel forest. This side is cooler and often cloudier, and that is exactly why people love it.

[Santana](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/madeira/north-coast/santana/) is famous for its thatched, triangular houses, the municipality's symbol. Visit Madeira notes that UNESCO declared Santana a Biosphere Reserve in 2011, that its hiking trails run to more than 120 km, and that Pico Ruivo, the highest point in the archipelago at 1,862 metres, lies within it. If you plan to walk the classic Pico do Arieiro to Pico Ruivo route, staying on this side cuts the drive to the trailheads.

On the cliffs at Achada do Gramacho in Santana, **[Quinta do Furão](https://www.quintadofurao.com/pt/Home)** is a hotel and restaurant on top of a sea cliff on the north-east coast, which holds a grape harvest festival and runs wine tastings.

At the island's north-western tip, [Porto Moniz](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/madeira/north-coast/porto-moniz/) is best known for its natural swimming pools in volcanic rock, refilled by the sea. The same page points to the ancient laurel woods of Fanal, part of the Laurissilva forest, a short drive uphill. **[Aqua Natura Madeira](https://www.aquanaturahotels.com/en/aqua-natura-madeira)** sits directly above the pools, between the sea and the forested mountains, with the Madeira Aquarium about 500 metres away.

<Figure id="mstay-porto-moniz-view" caption="Porto Moniz from the hillside road, with its rock pools on the shore and the islet offshore." />

The trade-off is evenings. North-coast villages are small and quiet after dark, and the drive back from Funchal at night is long. Treat this as a base for walking, swimming and early nights.

## The east: Machico and Caniçal

The east end is the most overlooked part of Madeira, and the closest to the airport. [Machico](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/madeira/east-coast/machico/), on a graceful bay, is where Gonçalves Zarco and Tristão Vaz Teixeira landed in July 1419, the historical birthplace of Madeira. Beyond Caniçal is Ponta de São Lourenço, a nine-kilometre peninsula of bare, wind-carved rock at the island's eastern tip, protected as a partial natural reserve.

Machico suits a short first or last night, families who want a calm bay, or anyone who plans to walk São Lourenço early before the day gets hot. It is a working town rather than a resort, which we count as a point in its favour.

## Porto Santo

Madeira's small neighbour is a different kind of trip. Visit Madeira describes [Porto Santo](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-go/porto-santo/porto-santo/) as famous for its nine-kilometre sandy beach; you reach it by the Porto Santo Line ferry from Funchal or by air. Most people come for the beach and very little else, which is the point. Our [Porto Santo guide](/stories/porto-santo/) explains how to fit it in.

## Hotels, quintas or apartments?

Madeira has a wide spread of accommodation. Alongside hotels and aparthotels, the tourism board's [where-to-stay pages](https://visitmadeira.com/en/where-to-stay/) list tourist apartments, rural tourism houses, and the quintas da Madeira, traditional estate houses valued for their architecture and gardens.

A few things to check, whichever you pick:

- **Licensing.** Visit Madeira notes that local accommodation (alojamento local) is a legal, regulated activity on the island, and that properties must be registered and show a valid licence number. Look for it on the listing.
- **Parking.** In Funchal, street parking is colour-coded: per the [moving-around guide](https://visitmadeira.com/en/travel-info/how-to-move-around/), blue lines are paid on weekdays and Saturday mornings, and yellow lines mean no parking. Ask whether your hotel has a space.
- **The slope.** Many Funchal addresses are a stiff climb from the seafront. A map with contour lines tells you more than a listing photo.

For a sense of how the regions link up by road, our [Madeira road trip](/stories/madeira-road-trip/) follows a full loop of the island.

## Before you book

- Decide whether you will hire a car. If not, base yourself in Funchal, where buses, taxis and ride-hailing are easiest.
- If walking matters most, split your stay and put at least part of it near your trailheads in the west or north.
- Check the current trail status and walking-route fee on [Visit Madeira's advice to walkers](https://visitmadeira.com/en/travel-info/useful-information/advice-to-walkers/) before you plan your days around a particular levada.
- For apartments and rural houses, look for the registration number on the listing.
- Allow for the weather. The north coast is often cloudier than Funchal; a day of mist there is normal, not bad luck.
