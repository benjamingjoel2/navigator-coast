Drive south out of Setúbal on the coast road and within minutes the city is gone. On one side, the mountain rises in a wall of dark green scrub and pale limestone. On the other, far below, the sea turns a colour you do not expect this close to Lisbon: clear, shallow turquoise over white sand, the kind of water that looks borrowed from somewhere much further south.

<Figure id="arr-cliff" size="full" caption="A sheltered cove beneath limestone cliffs on the Arrábida coast, the water clear over the rocks." />

This is the Serra da Arrábida, a short ridge of mountain running along the coast between Setúbal and Sesimbra, and it is one of the most beautiful corners of the Lisbon region. Combine it with Setúbal itself, a working port city with a great fish market and a river full of dolphins, and you have two or three days that feel a long way from the capital.

## The mountain and the park

The Serra da Arrábida is classified as a natural park, and Setúbal's tourism office, [Visit Setúbal](https://visitsetubal.com/en/scripts/Arr%C3%A1bida-and-beaches/), describes the pleasure of a visit as a mix of nature and beaches with crystal-clear water and fine sand, tucked into the foothills facing the Atlantic. The mountain's south face drops almost straight into the sea, which is what gives the coast its sheltered coves and its calm, clear water on still days.

The panoramic road over the ridge is the best introduction. Visit Setúbal points out that viewpoints open up all along it, with views over the city, the Sado estuary and the Atlantic. Take it slowly, pull in at the lay-bys, and let the landscape do the work.

<Figure id="arr-coast-road" caption="A track runs along the clifftop above the clear, shallow water of the Arrábida coast." />

## The beaches

The Arrábida beaches are small, sandy and backed by the mountain, and on a calm summer day they are as lovely as any in Portugal. [Visit Setúbal](https://visitsetubal.com/en/scripts/Arr%C3%A1bida-and-beaches/) lists the main ones:

- **Figueirinha**, one of the most popular, which has held a Blue Flag for more than a decade.
- **Galapos and Galapinhos**, smaller and more hidden, for those who want peace and quiet.
- **Creiro**, opposite the rock of Pedra da Anicha, which is protected as a zoological reserve. Behind the beach, the archaeological site of Creiro preserves traces of a Roman fish-salting complex.
- **Portinho da Arrábida**, the little bay with a cluster of white houses, which was named one of the 7 Natural Wonders of Portugal. At its entrance is the Lapa de Santa Margarida, a cave reached by a staircase down the slope, where human remains from the Lower Palaeolithic have been found.

### Getting to the beaches in summer

This is the part to plan carefully. Car access to the Arrábida beaches is restricted in the bathing season under the municipality's "Arrábida sem Carros" (Arrábida without cars) scheme. For 2026, the [Município de Setúbal](https://www.mun-setubal.pt/arrabida-sem-carros/) applied it for the whole season, from 4 June to 15 September, with private cars barred from the limited-traffic zones between 07:00 and 20:00 except for authorised vehicles, and public transport promoted instead.

The same page notes that storms early in 2026 damaged the access road to Albarquel and Figueirinha, and that one lane was closed for repairs. Rules and arrangements change from year to year, so check the municipality's page for the current season before you set off.

## The Arrábida Convent

High on the mountainside, in the middle of the green, sits a cluster of white buildings: the Convento da Arrábida. According to the [Fundação Oriente](https://www.foriente.pt/a-fundacao/convento-arrabida), which runs visits there, the convent was founded in 1542 by Frei Martinho de Santa Maria, a Castilian Franciscan. Its 25 hectares include the Old Convent, at the highest point of the slope, with small chapels and cells cut into the rock; the New Convent halfway down the hillside; a garden; and the Sanctuary of Bom Jesus.

<Pair a="arr-portinho-beach" b="arr-convent" captionA="Portinho da Arrábida's curving bay, with Tróia across the water." captionB="The white buildings of the Arrábida Convent on the mountainside." />

The convent can be visited on scheduled visits and events, which the Fundação Oriente lists on its [convent page](https://www.foriente.pt/a-fundacao/convento-arrabida). Book ahead; you cannot simply turn up.

## Setúbal: fish, market and river

Setúbal is not a pretty postcard town, and that is its charm. It is a busy port city at the mouth of the Sado, with a real working waterfront and a food culture built on the sea.

Start at the market. The [Mercado do Livramento](https://www.mun-setubal.pt/mercado-livramento-150-anos/) has been part of daily life in Setúbal for 150 years, from 1876 to 2026, and the municipality notes that fish takes centre stage in its halls. It opens from Tuesday to Sunday, in the mornings, so get there early to see the counters at their fullest.

<Figure id="arr-setubal-tiles" size="column" caption="An old tiled façade on Rua Tenente Valadim, in Setúbal's historic centre." />

Then eat. [Visit Setúbal's food page](https://visitsetubal.com/en/flavors/) puts fish and seafood at the heart of the city's cooking, with sardines, mackerel, red mullet and oysters among its signatures. The city is also known for sweets made from oranges and for Arrábida honey. Ask for whatever is fresh and grilled, and for choco frito, the fried cuttlefish that Setúbal is proud of.

For a wider view of Portugal's regional food, see our guide to [what to eat in Portugal, region by region](/stories/what-to-eat-in-portugal/).

### Dolphins in the Sado

The Sado estuary is home to something rare: a population of bottlenose dolphins that, according to [Visit Setúbal](https://visitsetubal.com/en/scripts/Moorish-and-Sado-Estuary/), lives there all year round and is easily spotted on guided river trips. The estuary is a nature reserve, and the same page notes flamingos, white storks, marsh harriers and black-winged stilts, depending on the season. The old Mourisca tide mill, on the estuary shore, makes a good starting point for exploring it.

Choose a licensed operator, and expect them to keep their distance from the animals. A good trip is about watching, not chasing.

## Azeitão: cheese, wine and old villages

On the north side of the mountain, Azeitão is the name for a group of villages, of which Vila Nogueira and Vila Fresca de Azeitão are the most interesting. [Visit Setúbal](https://visitsetubal.com/en/azeitao/) recommends exploring on foot, with the Bacalhôa Palace, the church of São Simão and the fountain in Vila Fresca, and in Vila Nogueira the Palace of the Dukes of Aveiro and the house-museum of the [José Maria da Fonseca](https://www.jmf.pt/) wine company.

This is food-and-wine country. Visit Setúbal singles out [Azeitão cheese](https://visitsetubal.com/en/flavors/), a soft sheep's milk cheese, along with the village's tortas and queijadas, and Moscatel, which it calls the king of the Setúbal Peninsula's wines. A glass of chilled Moscatel with a spoonful of Azeitão cheese is about as local as it gets.

## Getting there and around

Setúbal is easy to reach from Lisbon by train. [Fertagus](https://www.fertagus.pt/en) runs trains from Lisbon stations including Roma-Areeiro, Entrecampos and Sete Rios, ending at Setúbal. Check its timetable for your day.

For the mountain and the beaches, a car makes life easier, but remember the access restrictions in the bathing season. From Setúbal, [Atlantic Ferries](https://www.atlanticferries.pt/en/) runs a car ferry and a catamaran across the estuary to the Tróia peninsula, which is a handy way to continue south along the coast towards [Comporta](/stories/comporta-guide/). If you are heading on towards the Alentejo, our guide to [walking the Rota Vicentina](/stories/walking-the-rota-vicentina/) picks up the story further down the coast.

## Before you book

- Visiting between June and mid-September? Check the current [Arrábida sem Carros rules](https://www.mun-setubal.pt/arrabida-sem-carros/) before you plan a beach day.
- Book convent visits in advance through the [Fundação Oriente](https://www.foriente.pt/a-fundacao/convento-arrabida).
- Go to the [Mercado do Livramento](https://www.mun-setubal.pt/mercado-livramento-150-anos/) in the morning, and remember it does not open on Mondays.
- If you are planning a dolphin trip, choose a licensed operator on the Sado.
- Check the [Fertagus timetable](https://www.fertagus.pt/en) for your return train, especially at weekends.
