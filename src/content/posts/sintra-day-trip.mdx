The mist comes first. Even on a clear day in Lisbon, the Sintra hills often wear a cap of cloud, and as the train pulls in you can see it drifting through the trees above the town. Then the shapes appear through the green: a crenellated wall running along a ridge, a palace painted red and yellow on the highest peak, the twin white chimneys of the old royal palace rising over the rooftops.

Sintra is one of the great day trips in Europe, and it can also be one of the most frustrating. The roads are narrow, the hills are steep, and in high season the queues for the famous palaces can swallow a morning. None of that is a reason to skip it. It is a reason to plan.

## Why Sintra is special

Visit Lisboa notes that Sintra has been a [UNESCO World Heritage Site for its cultural landscape since 1992](https://www.visitlisboa.com/en/p/Heritage). The phrase matters. What makes Sintra extraordinary is not any single building but the whole composition: palaces, castles, chapels and gardens scattered through a forested range of hills, designed to be seen from one another.

Most of the big sites are managed by [Parques de Sintra](https://www.parquesdesintra.pt/en/), which runs Pena, the Moorish Castle, the National Palace of Sintra, Monserrate, the Convent of the Capuchos and several others, and sells tickets on its own site. Quinta da Regaleira is run separately.

## Getting there: take the train

Do not drive. Parques de Sintra is unambiguous: [access to the Moorish Castle and Pena is not authorised to private vehicles](https://www.parquesdesintra.pt/en/plan-your-visit/how-to-get-there/), and in the historic centre, private vehicle access is limited to residents. Parking is scarce, and the roads up the hill were never built for modern traffic.

The train is simple. CP's Sintra line runs from Lisbon's Rossio, Oriente and Entrecampos stations to Sintra, according to the [Parques de Sintra travel page](https://www.parquesdesintra.pt/en/plan-your-visit/how-to-get-there/); check the current Lisboa–Sintra timetable on [CP's timetable page](https://cp.pt/info/en/w/trains-timetables-pdf). Rossio, in the Baixa, is the most central departure point for most visitors. Our [guide to getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) explains Lisbon's urban trains and cards.

From Sintra station, the National Palace in the town centre is 10 to 15 minutes on foot. For the hilltop sites, Parques de Sintra lists:

| Destination | How to get there from Sintra station |
|---|---|
| Moorish Castle and Pena | Scotturb bus 434 |
| National Palace of Sintra | On foot, or buses 434 and 435 |
| Monserrate | Carris Metropolitana bus 1253, or Scotturb 435 |
| Convent of the Capuchos | Carris Metropolitana bus 1253 |

Routes and operators do change, so confirm on the [how to get there page](https://www.parquesdesintra.pt/en/plan-your-visit/how-to-get-there/) before you go.

## Choose two or three, not five

The commonest mistake is trying to see everything. The sites are spread across steep hills, each one takes a couple of hours to do properly, and moving between them eats time. Pick two or three that suit you and give them room.

### Pena Palace

The postcard. Parques de Sintra calls Pena [the pinnacle of Romanticism in Portugal](https://www.parquesdesintra.pt/en/parks-monuments/park-and-national-palace-of-pena/), and credits its legacy to Ferdinand II, the "King-Artist". It sits on one of the highest points of the range, a fantasy of towers, domes and tiled walls in red, yellow and blue, surrounded by a vast romantic park of lakes, ferns and exotic trees.

<Figure id="sin-pena-terrace" caption="Pena Palace's yellow ramparts and chimneys, with the wooded hills falling away towards the plain." />

Two practical points. First, palace tickets are sold for a specific date and time slot, so buy them in advance on the [Parques de Sintra site](https://www.parquesdesintra.pt/en/plan-your-visit/faqs/) rather than hoping for space on the day. Second, the park is large and steep. There are two entrances, the Main Entrance and the Lakes Entrance, and a Pena shuttle runs between the park gate and the palace, per the [Pena page](https://www.parquesdesintra.pt/en/parks-monuments/park-and-national-palace-of-pena/).

### The Moorish Castle

Just below Pena on a neighbouring peak, and on the same bus. Parques de Sintra says the castle was [founded in the 10th century](https://www.parquesdesintra.pt/en/parks-monuments/the-moorish-castle/), during the period when the Moors occupied the Iberian Peninsula. Its walls snake across the hills, linking granite boulders and cliffs, and the parapet walk looks over the town, across to Pena and, on a clear day, out to the Atlantic. It is less crowded than Pena and, for many people, more memorable.

<Figure id="sin-moorish-castle" size="full" caption="The walls of the Moorish Castle following the ridge above Sintra." />

### The National Palace of Sintra

The easiest to reach, right in the historic centre on Largo Rainha D. Amélia. According to [Parques de Sintra](https://www.parquesdesintra.pt/en/parks-monuments/national-palace-of-sintra/), the first structure here probably dates from the 10th or 11th century, and the palace was a favoured royal retreat at the end of the Middle Ages. Its skyline is shaped by two conical chimneys crowning the royal kitchen. If you arrive late or the weather closes in, this is the one to do.

<Figure id="sin-national-palace" caption="Carved stone window frames on a wing of the National Palace, in the centre of Sintra." />

### Quinta da Regaleira

A short walk from the centre, and a different world. Its [official site](https://www.regaleira.pt/en/quinta-da-regaleira) explains that the estate was designed and built in the late 1800s for António Augusto de Carvalho Monteiro, working with the Italian architect and scenographer Luigi Manini, as an eclectic ensemble drawing on Manueline, Renaissance, medieval and classical styles. The gardens hide grottoes, towers and the famous Initiatic Well, a spiral stair descending into the ground. Regaleira is managed by the Fundação Cultursintra, not Parques de Sintra, and sells its own timed tickets online.

<Figure id="sin-regaleira-well" size="column" caption="Looking down the spiral of the Initiatic Well at Quinta da Regaleira." />

### Monserrate

The quiet alternative. Parques de Sintra describes how the 19th-century British industrialist and art collector Francis Cook turned [Monserrate](https://www.parquesdesintra.pt/en/parks-monuments/park-and-palace-of-monserrate/) into a romantic estate, with a palace designed by James Knowles Jr. and gardens filled with exotic species from around the world. It lies outside the town on the road towards Colares, so it needs a bus, but it repays the effort with space and calm.

## Timing your day

Start early. Aim to be on one of the first trains from Rossio and head straight up the hill to your timed Pena slot, then walk or ride down to the Moorish Castle. Have lunch in the town, see the National Palace or Regaleira in the afternoon, and catch the train back before the evening rush.

Check hours on the [opening times page](https://www.parquesdesintra.pt/en/plan-your-visit/opening-times-and-prices/) for each site, which differ between monuments; some ticket offices close for lunch, with machines available. The [FAQs](https://www.parquesdesintra.pt/en/plan-your-visit/faqs/) say the parks are open every day except 24 and 25 December, though monuments can close at short notice for state events, fire risk or extreme weather.

As for season, spring and autumn are the sweet spots. Summer brings the biggest crowds, and the hills are often cooler and damper than Lisbon, so bring a layer whatever the forecast. Our [guide to the best time to visit Portugal](/stories/best-time-to-visit-portugal/) goes through the months in detail.

## A sweet stop in town

Sintra has its own pastries, and the place most associated with them is **[Piriquita](https://piriquita.pt/)**. The bakery says it was founded in 1862, and its signature is the travesseiro, a pastry "pillow" that it calls an emblem of the town. Its history also credits King Carlos I with encouraging the family to make queijadas, the small cheese tarts that are Sintra's other speciality.

## Staying overnight

Most people visit Sintra as a day trip, but staying one night lets you be in the hills before the first train arrives and after the last one leaves. **[Lawrence's Hotel](https://www.lawrenceshotel.com/)**, on Rua Consigliéri Pedroso, says it has welcomed guests since 1764; its site lists Lord Byron and Eça de Queiroz among past visitors and notes that the hotel appears in Eça's novel *Os Maias*.

## Fitting Sintra into a longer trip

From Lisbon, Sintra pairs naturally with the coast. Parques de Sintra lists bus 1624 linking Cascais with Cabo da Roca, the windswept cape west of the hills, so a longer loop through the area is possible without a car. ## Before you book

- Buy Pena tickets in advance for a specific date and time slot from [Parques de Sintra](https://www.parquesdesintra.pt/en/).
- Book Quinta da Regaleira separately through [its own site](https://www.regaleira.pt/en/quinta-da-regaleira).
- Check the Lisboa–Sintra train times on [CP's timetable page](https://cp.pt/info/en/w/trains-timetables-pdf) and the bus routes on the [how to get there page](https://www.parquesdesintra.pt/en/plan-your-visit/how-to-get-there/).
- Do not plan to drive to Pena or the Moorish Castle; private cars are not allowed up there.
- Wear good walking shoes and bring a waterproof layer, even in summer.
- Choose two or three sites and leave time to wander between them.
