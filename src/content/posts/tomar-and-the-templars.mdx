From the square in the old town, you cannot miss it. Above the red roofs and the slow green Nabão, a long run of walls climbs the wooded hill, and behind them rises a jumble of towers, buttresses and carved stone: a castle that became a monastery, then a palace of an order of knights, built and rebuilt over four centuries. Walk up through the pines and the scale of it only grows.

Tomar is one of the most rewarding small towns in central Portugal, and it is still surprisingly quiet. The Convent of Christ alone deserves half a day. Add the town below, a lunch, a pastry and a walk by the river, and you have a full and very good day out of Lisbon or Coimbra.

## From Templar castle to Order of Christ

The story begins with the Knights Templar. Turismo Centro de Portugal's [page on the Convent of Christ](https://www.centerofportugal.com/poi/convent-of-christ-unesco-world-heritage) dates Tomar Castle to the 12th century and notes that it served as the headquarters of the Order of the Temple from 1160, at a time when the young Portuguese kingdom was pushing its frontier south.

When the Templars were suppressed in the early 14th century (the same page records the arrest of the Knights Templar in France on 13 October 1307), Portugal's King Dinis found a way to keep their wealth and their men: in 1318 he founded a new order, the Order of Christ, which in time took over Tomar. By 1417 Prince Henry, better known as Henry the Navigator, was already its governor.

That long history is written in stone. The site was inscribed on [UNESCO's World Heritage List in 1983](https://www.centerofportugal.com/poi/convent-of-christ-unesco-world-heritage), and each phase of building is still visible as you walk through it.

## The Charola

Start where the Templars did. The Charola is the round church at the heart of the castle, and Turismo Centro de Portugal notes that its [octagonal structure is the original one](https://www.centerofportugal.com/poi/convent-of-christ-unesco-world-heritage), though its entrance was later moved. Inside, a central drum of arches rises within a ring-shaped ambulatory, every surface painted and gilded.

<Figure id="tomar-charola" caption="Painted decoration and carved stone on an arch inside the Charola." />

Stand in the middle and look up. The effect of colour and gold in a circular space is unlike anything else in Portugal, and it is easy to spend far longer here than you planned.

## The Manueline window

The most famous single feature of the convent is outside. The Chapter Window, designed by Diogo de Arruda and built, according to Turismo Centro de Portugal, [between 1510 and 1513](https://www.centerofportugal.com/poi/convent-of-christ-unesco-world-heritage), is the great showpiece of the Manueline style: a window framed in carved stone ropes, knots, coral, seaweed and nautical gear, topped by the cross of the Order of Christ.

<Pair a="tomar-window-detail" b="tomar-church-portal" captionA="Carved ropes, knots and the cross of the Order of Christ on the convent walls." captionB="Pinnacles and a carved rose window on the convent church." />

It is best seen from the roof terrace above the Cloister of Santa Bárbara. Take your time with it; there is a new detail every time you look.

## The cloisters and the aqueduct

In the 16th century the convent was transformed again. Turismo Centro de Portugal notes that the 1528 plan arranged the new monastic buildings around [five Renaissance cloisters](https://www.centerofportugal.com/poi/convent-of-christ-unesco-world-heritage), and walking from one to the next is a lesson in how quickly taste changed, from the flamboyance of the Manueline to calm classical proportion.

<Figure id="tomar-cloister" caption="The Cloister of João III, with its fountain at the centre." />

The convent's water arrived by aqueduct. The Pegões Aqueduct, which crosses the valley west of the convent, is [6 kilometres long with 180 round arches](https://www.centerofportugal.com/poi/convent-of-christ-unesco-world-heritage), and its tallest stretch, striding over a valley on two tiers of arches, is worth a short drive or a long walk.

## Visiting the Convent of Christ

The convent is one of the monuments run by [Museus e Monumentos de Portugal](https://www.museusemonumentos.pt/pt), and tickets can be bought through its [online ticketing site](https://mmp.bymeoblueticket.pt/Eventos). Turismo Centro de Portugal lists opening hours of [09:00 to 17:30 from October to March](https://www.centerofportugal.com/poi/convent-of-christ-unesco-world-heritage), with last entry at 17:00, and 09:00 to 18:30 from April to September, with last entry at 18:00. It notes closures on 1 January, 1 May and 25 December. Hours and closures can change, so check before you go.

A few practical tips:

- **Arrive at opening time** if you can, especially on weekends and in summer, and see the Charola before the groups.
- **Allow at least two to three hours.** The complex is large, and the route covers several levels and roof terraces.
- **Wear good shoes.** There are steps, uneven floors and a steep walk up from town.

Below the convent, the woods of the [Mata dos Sete Montes](https://www.centerofportugal.com/poi/mata-dos-sete-montes) make a shady way down to the town. Turismo Centro de Portugal describes the walled woodland as land that once belonged to the Templars, later used by the Order of Christ as a place of seclusion, with centuries-old cypresses, oaks and olive trees.

## The town below

Tomar's old town is a grid of low, whitewashed streets running down to the Praça da República, where the town hall faces the Church of São João Baptista. Turismo Centro de Portugal describes the church as [fascinating for its multitude of symbols](https://www.centerofportugal.com/destination/tomar), and it is worth stepping inside.

<Figure id="tomar-square" caption="The Praça da República and its town hall, with the castle walls on the wooded hill behind." />

A few streets away, on Rua Joaquim Jacinto, is the town's synagogue. Turismo Centro de Portugal dates it to the mid-15th century and describes it as [the oldest synagogue in Portugal](https://www.centerofportugal.com/destination/tomar). It is small and plain, and all the more moving for it.

The river Nabão runs along the edge of the old town, and the Mouchão, a park on an island in the river, is the place to sit in the shade on a hot afternoon.

## Sweets and lunch

Tomar has its own convent sweets. Turismo Centro de Portugal says the **fatias de Tomar**, egg-yolk slices soaked in syrup, were [created in the Convent of Christ](https://www.centerofportugal.com/article/eating-in-tomar-2), with a first written reference in 1876, and are baked in a special pot. The doçaria **[Estrelas de Tomar](https://www.estrelasdetomar.pt/)**, on Rua Serpa Pinto, makes them along with queijinhos doces, little almond shells filled with sweet egg cream, and beija-me depressa, whose name means "kiss me quickly".

If you are new to Portugal's egg-and-sugar pastries, our guide to [what to eat in Portugal, region by region](/stories/what-to-eat-in-portugal/) is a good primer.

## The Festa dos Tabuleiros

Every four years, Tomar hosts one of Portugal's most spectacular festivals. The Festa dos Tabuleiros returns from [3 to 12 July 2027](https://www.centerofportugal.com/article/trays-feast-in-tomar), according to Turismo Centro de Portugal. Residents spend thousands of hours making paper flowers to decorate the streets, and on the final Sunday a long procession of women, many dressed in white, carries tall decorated trays on their heads from the Mata dos Sete Montes to the Praça da República.

If you want to see it, book early: the town is small and fills up. Our [month-by-month guide to the best time to visit Portugal](/stories/best-time-to-visit-portugal/) puts it in the context of the summer calendar.

## Where to stay

Tomar works well as a day trip, but an overnight stay lets you see the convent at opening time and the town after the day visitors have left.

- **[Hotel República](https://www.hotelrepublica.pt/)** is on the Praça da República itself, and its suites look over the square and the Church of São João Baptista.
- **[Estalagem de Santa Iria](https://estalagemsantairia.pt/)** stands on the Mouchão park island beside the Nabão, with a spa, gardens and a restaurant open to the public.

## Getting there

Tomar is on its own branch of the railway. CP describes the [Tomar branch line](https://www.cp.pt/info/en/w/institutional/tomar-branch-line) as a 14.7-kilometre electrified single track that joins the main North line at Lamarosa; it opened in 1928 and is served by CP's Regional and InterRegional trains. Check the current [Lisbon to Tomar timetable](https://cp.pt/info/documents/d/cp/comboios-regionais-lisboa-tomar) before you travel. From Tomar station, the old town is a short walk and the convent a steep climb beyond it.

By car, Tomar combines easily with the monasteries at Batalha and Alcobaça, covered in our guide to [Batalha and Alcobaça in a day](/stories/batalha-alcobaca-monasteries/). For the bigger picture on trains, buses and hire cars, see our guide to [getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/).

## Before you book

- Check the convent's current hours and buy tickets through the [Museus e Monumentos de Portugal ticketing site](https://mmp.bymeoblueticket.pt/Eventos).
- Confirm train times on the [CP timetable](https://cp.pt/info/documents/d/cp/comboios-regionais-lisboa-tomar), and plan your return before you set off.
- Allow time for the climb from town to the convent, and wear comfortable shoes.
- If you are coming for the Festa dos Tabuleiros in July 2027, book accommodation as early as you can.
