There used to be a small ritual at the end of every flight into Lisbon or Porto: the queue, the glance at your face, the thud of the rubber stamp. That stamp has gone. Since spring 2026, Europe's borders have recorded visitors digitally instead, with fingerprints and a photo, and for many travellers the first trip under the new system brings a few surprises.

This guide is for visitors who do not hold an EU passport. It covers what you need to get into Portugal as of November 2026, what the Entry/Exit System actually involves at the airport, and where the long-discussed ETIAS travel authorisation stands. Rules depend on your nationality and they do change, so treat this as a map, then check your own government's advice before you travel.

## Portugal and the Schengen area

Portugal is one of the 29 countries of the Schengen area, which the European Commission lists as [25 EU member states plus Iceland, Norway, Switzerland and Liechtenstein](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/schengen/schengen-area_en). Checks at the internal borders between them have been abolished, which is why you can cross from Portugal into Spain by road or train without a passport check. Our itinerary for [Lisbon and Seville](/stories/lisbon-and-seville/) does exactly that.

It also means that your time in Portugal is counted together with your time everywhere else in Schengen. That matters for the most important rule of all.

## Visas and the 90/180-day rule

Many nationalities, including British and Canadian citizens, can visit the Schengen area without a visa for short stays. Others need a Schengen visa before they travel; the Commission's [visa policy page](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/schengen/visa-policy_en) explains who.

Visa-free visitors can stay for [up to 90 days in any 180-day period](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/portugal/entry-requirements). It is a rolling window: on any given day, look back 180 days and add up every day you spent anywhere in Schengen. GOV.UK warns that overstaying can lead to a ban of up to three years. Canada's advice makes the same point, that [stays are cumulative across all Schengen countries](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/portugal).

If you are planning a long trip, a winter escape or a few weeks in the Algarve followed by Spain and France, count the days before you book the flights, not after.

## Passport rules

Passport validity is where most travellers get caught out, and the rule has two parts. According to [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/portugal/entry-requirements), for British citizens your passport must:

- have been **issued less than 10 years before** the day you arrive, and
- be **valid for at least 3 months after** the day you plan to leave the Schengen area.

The first part trips up people whose passports were issued with extra months carried over from an old one. GOV.UK notes that passports renewed before 1 October 2018 may fall foul of it. Canada's advice sets the same three-month validity rule for Canadians and adds that airlines may apply [stricter rules of their own](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/portugal).

At the border you may also be asked to show an onward or return ticket and that you have enough money for your stay, so keep those handy.

## The Entry/Exit System, explained

The EES is the EU's digital border system. According to the European Commission, it started on 12 October 2025 with a progressive roll-out across 29 European countries and became [fully operational on 10 April 2026](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/news/entryexit-system-will-become-fully-operational-10-april-2026-2026-03-30_en). eu-LISA, the EU agency that runs it, confirmed that by that date [every Schengen country had deployed it](https://www.eulisa.europa.eu/news-and-events/news/entryexit-system-fully-deployed-across-eu) at its external borders.

### Who it applies to

It covers non-EU nationals travelling for a short stay. If you hold an EU passport, it does not apply to you.

### What it records

The Commission's [EES page](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/schengen/smart-borders/entry-exit-system_en) lists your name, travel document details, fingerprints and facial image, and the date and place of each entry and exit. It also records refusals of entry. In place of stamps, the system counts your days automatically, which is how overstays are now detected.

### What happens at the airport

[GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/portugal/entry-requirements) sets out the traveller's-eye view:

- You do not need to do anything before you arrive. There is no pre-registration.
- On your first visit under the system, you may be directed to a special booth to register your details before you reach the immigration desk. Registration is free.
- You may be asked for biometrics on each entry and exit, including a fingerprint or photo when you leave.
- Children aged 11 or under do not have their fingerprints scanned, but may have their photo taken.
- Your EES record is valid for 3 years.

The practical consequence is time. The first crossing can take longer than you are used to, and busy arrival waves can build queues. Allow for it: do not book a tight connection or a timed entry ticket for the hour you land. Our guide to [arriving at Lisbon airport](/stories/arriving-at-lisbon-airport/) covers what comes after passport control.

## ETIAS: not yet

ETIAS, the European Travel Information and Authorisation System, will be an online pre-travel authorisation for visa-exempt visitors. It has been announced, delayed and re-announced so often that it is easy to assume it is already needed.

It is not, as of this writing. The European Commission's [ETIAS page](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/schengen/smart-borders/european-travel-information-authorisation-system_en) says the system is currently not in operation and no applications are being collected, and that the start date will be announced several months before launch. When it does start, the Commission says the fee will be EUR 20 and that applications will be made through an official website or app. Canada's advice likewise notes that [applications are not yet open](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/portugal).

Two things follow:

- **Do not pay anyone for an ETIAS today.** There is nothing to apply for. When it launches, use the [official EU site](https://travel-europe.europa.eu/en/etias/about-etias/what-is-etias) and nothing else.
- **Check again before trips booked for 2027.** A start date is expected to be announced months in advance, so a quick look before you travel will tell you whether it applies.

## Travelling with children

Portugal has a specific rule for minors. GOV.UK explains that children aged 17 and under travelling alone, or with an adult who is not their parent or legal guardian, [must carry a letter of authorisation](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/portugal/entry-requirements) signed by a parent or guardian, with the signature certified, and a copy of the parent's passport. Canada's advice describes a similar notarised letter, including travel dates, the address of the stay and details of the responsible adult. Without it, entry may be delayed or refused. Start this early; certification takes time.

## Customs and cash

Two customs points catch visitors out:

- **Cash.** You must declare cash of [EUR 10,000 or more](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/carry/carrying-cash/index_en.htm), or the equivalent in other currencies, when you enter or leave the EU. Cash includes banknotes and coins and also things like traveller's cheques.
- **Food.** GOV.UK notes that meat, milk and products containing them are generally prohibited when arriving from outside the EU, with exceptions such as baby food. Leave the sandwiches on the plane.

## Before you book

- Check both passport rules: issued less than 10 years before you arrive, and valid for at least 3 months after you leave Schengen.
- Count your Schengen days across the previous 180, including other countries.
- Find out whether your nationality needs a visa on the [Commission's visa policy page](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/schengen/visa-policy_en).
- Allow extra time at the border for the EES on your first crossing, on the way in and on the way out.
- Ignore any website offering ETIAS today, and check the official EU site again before 2027 trips.
- If a child is travelling without both parents, arrange the certified letter well in advance.
- Read your own government's latest travel advice in the week before you fly.
