Óbidos is best at the end of the day. The coach parties have gone, the lanes inside the walls fall quiet, and the low sun turns the old stone the colour of honey. An hour up the coast, Nazaré is doing something completely different: on a winter swell, the sea below its headland heaves itself into walls of water that draw surfers and onlookers from around the world.

They make an unlikely pair, a medieval hill town and a fishing resort famous for giant surf, but they sit close together north of Lisbon and fit neatly into two unhurried days. Spend the night in Óbidos, and you get its best hours to yourself.

## Day 1: Óbidos

### Inside the walls

The [Center of Portugal tourism board](https://www.centerofportugal.com/destination/obidos) describes Óbidos as encircled by a ring of medieval walls, crowned by a Moorish castle rebuilt by King Dinis. It also notes a detail that explains a lot about the town's charm: until 1883 Óbidos was the exclusive property of the queens of Portugal.

<Figure id="obnz-castle" caption="The castle of Óbidos above the whitewashed houses and church towers of the town." />

You enter, as most visitors always have, through the southern Santa Maria gate, which the tourism board notes is lined with eighteenth-century azulejos. Beyond it, the main street climbs towards the castle between whitewashed houses trimmed in blue and ochre, with bougainvillea and climbing plants spilling over doorways. Take the side lanes as well; they are quieter and often prettier.

<Pair a="obnz-wall-walk" b="obnz-lane" captionA="The crenellated wall curving across the hillside." captionB="A cobbled lane inside the walls, trimmed in blue and yellow." />

Walking a stretch of the walls themselves is the classic Óbidos experience, with views over the rooftops on one side and the farmland on the other. The path along the top is narrow and uneven, so watch your footing, and keep a close hand on children.

### Santa Maria and the bookshops

The town's main church sits on Santa Maria square. [Center of Portugal](https://www.centerofportugal.com/poi/saint-marys-church-in-obidos) singles out its interior tiles as among the finest tile compositions in the country, and its altarpiece of Saint Catherine of Alexandria by the painter Josefa d'Óbidos, possibly the only one of her works still in its original place.

Óbidos also has an unusual second identity as a town of books. The tourism board's [Óbidos page](https://www.centerofportugal.com/destination/obidos) describes the Literary Town project, begun in 2013 by the town hall and the bookseller Ler Devagar, and the town is a UNESCO Creative City of Literature, as the project's own site, [Óbidos Vila Literária](https://obidosvilaliteraria.com/), makes clear. Bookshops have taken over some surprising buildings here; browse whatever you find open.

### Ginja and where to stay

Ginja, a sour cherry liqueur, is the drink of Óbidos, and you will see it sold alongside small chocolate cups, to be eaten once the ginja is gone. [Ginja de Óbidos Oppidum](https://ginjadeobidos.com/), a family producer based at Sobral da Lagoa near the Óbidos lagoon, says it has been making ginja from locally grown cherries since 1987, and sells bottles together with chocolate cups. It is strong; one is usually enough.

For the night, the obvious and memorable choice is the castle itself. [Pousada Castelo Óbidos](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-obidos) occupies the castle at the top of the town; the pousada notes that King Dinis gave the castle to Queen Isabel as a wedding present, and that it has operated as a pousada since 1951. It has rooms both in the castle and in a house beside it.

The town's calendar is busy. The tourism board lists the International Chocolate Festival, Óbidos Christmas Town and FOLIO, the international literary festival, among its main annual events; check dates before you go, as they bring big crowds.

## Day 2: Nazaré

### The beach and the market

Nazaré begins down on the sand. The town spreads along a wide, curving beach, and fishing still shows through its holiday-resort surface. The [Center of Portugal](https://www.centerofportugal.com/destination/nazare) page describes two of its most familiar sights: women in the traditional seven skirts, and horse mackerel, carapau, laid out to dry in the sun.

<Figure id="obnz-nazare-bay" size="full" caption="Nazaré's long beach curving away below the cliffs, seen from above at sunset." />

You can see both properly in two places. The [Municipal Market](https://www.centerofportugal.com/poi/municipal-market-of-nazare), near the beach on Avenida Vieira Guimarães, has fish sold by women in the traditional dress, plus fruit, vegetables, dried fruit and flowers. The [Dry Fish Museum](https://www.centerofportugal.com/entity/dry-fish-museum-of-nazare), set up by the town hall on Avenida Manuel Remígio, is a living museum built to keep the fish-drying tradition going, with drying areas and an interpretation centre. Our guide to [what to eat in Portugal](/stories/what-to-eat-in-portugal/) has more on the country's love of fish.

### Up to the Sítio

Then go up. The Sítio is the old quarter on the clifftop, and the [tourism board](https://www.centerofportugal.com/poi/sitio-in-nazare) calls it the best viewpoint in the area, reached by funicular. The view down over the beach and the red roofs of the lower town is the one you have seen in photographs.

The Sítio is also a place of pilgrimage. The tourism board's [Nazaré page](https://www.centerofportugal.com/destination/nazare) tells the legend: an image of Our Lady, said to come from Nazareth, was found in a small cave here in 1182. Its [Sítio page](https://www.centerofportugal.com/poi/sitio-in-nazare) adds the story of Dom Fuas Roupinho, a twelfth-century commander who, while chasing a deer, found himself on the edge of an abyss and called on Our Lady; he built the small Ermida da Memória in thanks. The larger Sanctuary of Our Lady of Nazaré dates from the eighteenth century, and big festivities are held in her honour in September.

### The fort and the giant waves

From the Sítio, follow the road down towards the headland and the Fort of São Miguel Arcanjo. [Nazaré Qualifica](https://www.nazarequalifica.pt/en/praia-norte/forte), which runs it, says the fort was built in 1577 under King Sebastião to defend the coast against pirates and privateers, and that a lighthouse was installed there in 1903 and still works.

<Figure id="obnz-nazare-fort" caption="The Fort of São Miguel Arcanjo and its lighthouse on the headland above the sea." />

The fort is also the grandstand for Praia do Norte, the beach to the north where the giant waves break. The cause is under the water: the [tourism board](https://www.centerofportugal.com/destination/nazare) describes the Nazaré Canyon as around 5,000 metres deep and 230 km long, and says it is the reason the waves here are so big. In November 2011 Garrett McNamara rode a wave there recorded at 23.77 metres, according to the tourism board's [Togetherness feature](https://www.centerofportugal.com/togetherness/edition/edition-42).

Inside the fort, Nazaré Qualifica lists an interpretation centre on the canyon, opened in 2015, and the Surfer Wall, created in 2016, which displays boards used in memorable big-wave sessions. The fort is open all year; check the current hours on its page.

Timing is everything. The [tourism board](https://www.centerofportugal.com/destination/nazare) says the big-wave season runs from November to February, but the giants need the right swell, and on many winter days the sea is merely impressive. Watch the forecasts, and if a big day is coming, arrive early.

<Figure id="obnz-nazare-boat" size="column" caption="A fishing boat pulled up on the sand, with the cliffs of the Sítio behind." />

Above all, respect the sea. These are among the most dangerous waters on the coast. Stay behind barriers, keep well back from the edge of the rocks, and do not go down onto Praia do Norte when the swell is up. Check [IPMA's weather warnings](https://www.ipma.pt/en/alerta/) before you go, and obey any closures or instructions from the authorities on the day.

## Getting there and around

The [tourism board](https://www.centerofportugal.com/destination/obidos) puts Óbidos about 80 km from Lisbon, and Nazaré lies further up the same coast. A car makes this trip easiest, especially for linking the two towns. Portuguese motorways are tolled, and some use electronic tolling only: [Portugal Tolls](https://www.pttolls.com/en), run by Infraestruturas de Portugal, explains the payment options for foreign-registered cars, and your hire company will tell you how its own cars are set up.

Without a car, look at the coach options; our [guide to getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) explains the national coach network and how to book. If this is your first trip and you are still shaping the route, our [first-timer's guide to Portugal](/stories/first-timers-guide-to-portugal/) shows how the Centro fits around Lisbon and Porto.

## When to go

The two halves of this trip pull in different directions. Óbidos is lovely and quieter in spring and autumn, and its winter events bring their own crowds. Nazaré's beach is a summer place, but its waves belong to winter. If you want to see the giants, go between November and February and keep your plans flexible. Our guide to the [best time to visit Portugal](/stories/best-time-to-visit-portugal/) covers the seasons across the country.

## Before you book

- If you want to sleep inside the castle, book [Pousada Castelo Óbidos](https://www.pousadas.pt/en/hotel/pousada-obidos) well ahead.
- Check dates for the Óbidos festivals listed by the [tourism board](https://www.centerofportugal.com/destination/obidos), whether you want to catch one or avoid the crowds.
- For big-wave watching, plan for November to February and check the swell forecast and [IPMA warnings](https://www.ipma.pt/en/alerta/) a day or two before.
- Check the current opening hours of the [Fort of São Miguel Arcanjo](https://www.nazarequalifica.pt/en/praia-norte/forte).
- If you are driving, sort out electronic tolls before you set off, using [Portugal Tolls](https://www.pttolls.com/en).
