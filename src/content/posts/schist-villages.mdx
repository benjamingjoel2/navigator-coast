The first schist village you see will probably stop you in your tracks. One moment the road is winding through chestnut and pine; the next, a cluster of dark stone houses appears on the slope opposite, roofs of grey slate, walls the colour of the hillside itself, as if the mountain had folded itself into shelters. Up close, the stones are laid in thin, uneven layers, warm brown in the sun and almost black after rain. Woodsmoke drifts from a chimney. A stream runs somewhere below.

These are the Aldeias do Xisto, the schist villages of central Portugal, scattered through the hills between Coimbra and the Spanish border. Many were nearly abandoned in the last century as people left for the cities and abroad. Today a good number have been carefully restored, with guesthouses, craft workshops and small cafés, while keeping the quiet that makes them special.

## What the Schist Villages network is

The [Aldeias do Xisto network](https://www.aldeiasdoxisto.com/en/about-us/our-network/) began in 2001 as a regional programme and is now run by ADXTUR, an agency for the villages' tourism development created at the end of 2007. The network counts 27 villages in the Centre Region, which [Center of Portugal](https://www.centerofportugal.com/destination/schist-villages) groups into four areas: the Serra da Lousã, the Serra do Açor, the Zêzere river valley, and the Tagus and Ocreza rivers.

The network's [own site](https://www.aldeiasdoxisto.com/en/) has a page on each village, along with walking and cycling routes, river beaches and a directory of local places to stay and eat. It is the best place to start planning.

Not every schist village belongs to it. Piódão, the most famous of all, is part of a different network, the Historical Villages of Portugal, and we cover it below too.

## The Serra da Lousã villages

The Lousã hills, south-east of Coimbra, hold the largest cluster of schist villages and the easiest to reach. They make a natural first visit.

### Talasnal

Talasnal is the one on the postcards. According to its [network page](https://www.aldeiasdoxisto.com/en/villages/lousa/talasnal/), it sits on the western slope of the Serra da Lousã, about 12 km from Lousã, with dark schist houses whose facades are almost all left unrendered. Permanent settlement began in the late seventeenth or early eighteenth century; the population peaked at 129 in 1911, and by 1981 just two permanent residents remained. Today its houses are second homes, places to stay and small businesses.

<Pair a="sxv-talasnal-wall" b="sxv-piodao-green" captionA="Layered schist walls in Talasnal, with the wooded valley glimpsed between the houses." captionB="Buildings on the ridge above Piódão, framed by autumn-coloured scrub." />

The village's page describes access by an unpaved forest track from Cacilhas, and several of the signposted Lousã schist trails pass through. Look out for talasnicos, small honey-and-chestnut cakes that the network associates with the village.

### Candal and Cerdeira

[Candal](https://www.aldeiasdoxisto.com/en/villages/lousa/candal/), about 10 km from Lousã, is the easiest of the Lousã villages to reach, because the N236 road to Trevim, the highest point of the range, runs right through it. Below the road, the village tumbles down to the Ribeira de Candal, where five watermills were built in the 1920s and a water-powered olive press from 1919 has been restored to working order.

[Cerdeira](https://www.aldeiasdoxisto.com/en/villages/lousa/cerdeira/) has become a creative retreat. Its network page describes a School of Arts and Crafts, ateliers, a library, a gallery and a community oven, and each July the Elementos à Solta festival turns the village into an open-air gallery. One of its buildings was rebuilt using schist, clay mortar and cork insulation as part of an eco-rehabilitation programme.

### Casal de São Simão

On the south-western edge of the range, [Casal de São Simão](https://www.aldeiasdoxisto.com/en/villages/lousa/casal-de-sao-simao/) is the exception that proves the rule: its main building stone is quartzite, not schist, and the village is a single street running along a ridge. A path leads down to a small river beach in a cove of the Fragas de São Simão, a set of tall, rugged cliffs. The village chapel dates from the fifteenth century.

## Piódão and the Serra do Açor

Further east, the Serra do Açor is wilder and higher, and the drive in is half the experience.

<Figure id="sxv-piodao-terraces" size="full" caption="Piódão's grey schist houses stacked on terraces in the Serra do Açor, with the white parish church at their edge." />

[Piódão](https://aldeiashistoricasdeportugal.com/en/aldeia/piodao/), in the municipality of Arganil, is one of the 12 Historical Villages of Portugal. The network describes its houses laid out on terraces in a "schist nativity scene", built of schist with schist-slab roofs and doors and window frames painted in strong colours. The village has been classified as a Place of Public Interest since 1978. Its parish church dates from the seventeenth century and was remodelled at the end of the nineteenth, with a Neo-Baroque pediment and conical spires that stand out white against the grey.

For a night here, the [Hotel INATEL Piódão](https://aldeiashistoricasdeportugal.com/en/local/hotel-inatel-piodao/) is a four-star hotel built in schist, with 27 rooms, according to the Historical Villages network.

Across the range, [Fajão](https://www.aldeiasdoxisto.com/en/villages/acor/fajao/), in Pampilhosa da Serra, sits at about 720 metres beside the quartzite outcrop of the Penedos de Fajão, close to the source of the River Ceira. Its old town hall, courthouse and prison have been turned into tourist accommodation, and the [O Pascoal](https://www.aldeiasdoxisto.com/en/about-us/directory/o-pascoal-restaurant/) restaurant is listed in the village by the network.

## The Zêzere valley

The villages along the Zêzere have a gentler, more riverside character. [Janeiro de Cima](https://www.aldeiasdoxisto.com/en/villages/zezere/janeiro-de-cima/), on the river's left bank in the municipality of Fundão, is the one to see: its house walls mix schist with round, white stones from the riverbed, giving the village a pattern all its own. The Casa das Tecedeiras, the Weavers' House, works to keep the local weaving tradition alive, and you can take a ferry ride upstream on the river that villagers once crossed by calling for the boat. [Casa de Janeiro](https://www.aldeiasdoxisto.com/en/about-us/directory/casa-de-janeiro/), a guesthouse in the village with schist and pebble walls, is set up for cyclists.

## Walking, cycling and river beaches

This is outstanding walking country, with signposted short routes around almost every village. The network's trail platform, [My Xisto Trails](https://www.myxistotrails.pt/en/), gathers the routes, from village loops to long climbs.

The big one is the [Grande Rota do Zêzere](https://www.aldeiasdoxisto.com/en/visit-enjoy/active-nature/grande-rota-do-zezere-trail/), the GR33, which follows the river for 370 km from its source at Covão d'Ametade in the Serra da Estrela to Constância. It is split into nine sectors and can be done on foot, by mountain bike or by canoe, passing the schist villages of Barroca, Janeiro de Cima, Janeiro de Baixo and Álvaro. If the source tempts you, our guide to the [Serra da Estrela](/stories/serra-da-estrela/) covers the high country where it begins.

In summer, the villages' [river beaches](https://www.aldeiasdoxisto.com/en/visit-enjoy/leisure-nature/river-beaches/) come into their own. The network lists more than 50, from natural pools with diving boards to quiet bends in the river, including Fragas de São Simão, Janeiro de Baixo, Piódão and Benfeita.

## Eating in the hills

Mountain food here is hearty. The signature dish of the Lousã hills is chanfana, which the network calls one of the most iconic dishes of Miranda do Corvo and the Serra da Lousã. The [Museu da Chanfana](https://www.aldeiasdoxisto.com/en/about-us/directory/museu-da-chanfana/), a restaurant inside the Parque Biológico da Serra da Lousã in Miranda do Corvo, builds its menu around it, alongside local soups and traditional dishes. For more of the region's cooking, see our guide to [what to eat in Portugal](/stories/what-to-eat-in-portugal/).

## Getting there

Coimbra is the gateway. Since December 2025, the [Metro do Mondego](https://portugal.gov.pt/gc25/comunicacao/noticias/metro-do-mondego-liga-coimbra-a-serpins-a-partir-de-dia-16-dezembro-e-sera-gratuito-ate-final-do-mes) metrobus has linked Coimbra with Miranda do Corvo, Lousã and Serpins, which puts the foot of the Serra da Lousã within easy reach without a car. From Lousã, the villages are a steep walk or a short taxi ride up the mountain.

Beyond Lousã, you really need a car. The roads are narrow and winding, and the distances between villages are longer than they look on the map. Our [guide to getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) covers car hire and driving rules, and our [Coimbra guide](/stories/coimbra-guide/) makes a good base for a first day trip into the hills.

## When to go

<Figure id="sxv-piodao-mist" size="column" caption="Low cloud settling over Piódão and its terraces." />

Spring and autumn are the best seasons for walking, with green hills, running streams and comfortable temperatures. Summer is the time for river beaches, but the hills get hot, and this is wildfire country. Before walking in summer or early autumn, check the [fire risk](https://www.ipma.pt/en/riscoincendio/rcm.pt/) published by IPMA and follow any local restrictions. Winter can be cold, wet and misty in the mountains, though a schist village under low cloud, woodsmoke rising, has its own appeal. Our overview of the [best time to visit Portugal](/stories/best-time-to-visit-portugal/) puts the seasons in context.

## Before you book

- Plan on a car for anywhere beyond Lousã.
- Check the network's village pages and [My Xisto Trails](https://www.myxistotrails.pt/en/) for routes, and download maps before you go.
- Book accommodation ahead, especially for summer weekends; many villages have only a handful of places to stay.
- In the warm months, check IPMA's [fire risk](https://www.ipma.pt/en/riscoincendio/rcm.pt/) before walking.
- Wear proper footwear. Village lanes are steep, uneven and slippery when wet.
