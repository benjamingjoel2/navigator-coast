You step up to the counter of a neighbourhood pastelaria, the glass case full of custard tarts and sugar-dusted cakes, and the person behind it looks up. This is the moment. You could point and say "coffee", and you would probably get one. Or you could say "Bom dia, um café, por favor", and watch the conversation change temperature by a few degrees.

Portuguese is not an easy language to pick up by ear. Written down, it looks close to Spanish; spoken, it sounds quite different, with swallowed vowels and soft "sh" sounds that blur words together. You will often find English spoken in the big cities and tourist areas, but a handful of phrases goes a long way, especially in smaller towns, markets and family-run places. Here are the ones we would learn first.

## A note on pronunciation

You do not need to sound Portuguese. You only need to be understood, and to show you tried. A few patterns help:

- **Unstressed vowels shrink.** "Obrigado" comes out closer to "o-bri-GAH-doo", with the final "o" barely there.
- **S before a consonant or at the end of a word** often sounds like "sh". "Dois cafés" sounds roughly like "doysh ka-FESH".
- **"ão" is nasal**, as in "pão" (bread). Say "ow" through your nose and you are close enough.
- **"lh" and "nh"** are like the "lli" in "million" and the "ny" in "canyon": "Bolhão", "vinho".

Do not worry about the accent marks too much. People will meet you more than halfway.

## The essentials

| Portuguese | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bom dia | Good morning | Until lunch |
| Boa tarde | Good afternoon | After lunch until dark |
| Boa noite | Good evening, good night | Used as both hello and goodbye after dark |
| Olá | Hello | Friendly, works any time |
| Por favor | Please | |
| Obrigado / Obrigada | Thank you | A man says obrigado, a woman says obrigada |
| De nada | You're welcome | |
| Desculpe | Sorry, excuse me | For getting past someone or getting attention |
| Com licença | Excuse me | When you need to squeeze through |
| Sim / Não | Yes / No | |
| Fala inglês? | Do you speak English? | Polite opener before switching languages |
| Não percebo | I don't understand | |
| Pode repetir, por favor? | Could you repeat that, please? | |
| Adeus / Até logo | Goodbye / See you later | |

The thank-you rule surprises people. "Obrigado" agrees with the speaker, not the person being thanked, so a woman travelling alone says "obrigada" all trip. Get it slightly wrong and nobody will mind; get it right and you will occasionally see a smile.

## Coffee and pastries

Coffee is a daily ritual, ordered at the counter and drunk quickly, and the vocabulary is worth having. A plain "um café" gets you a short, strong espresso. In Lisbon you will also hear it called "uma bica". "Um galão" is a tall glass of milky coffee; "uma meia de leite" is a cup of half coffee, half milk. "Um descafeinado" is decaf.

<Figure id="phr-porto-coffee" caption="Coffee and a croissant at a café table in Porto, the usual start to the day." />

With your coffee, point at the case and ask for "um destes, por favor" (one of these, please). The star is "um pastel de nata", the custard tart, plural "pastéis de nata". In Belém, the bakery [Pastéis de Belém](https://pasteisdebelem.pt/en/) on Rua de Belém says it has made its own version since 1837, from a recipe linked to the nearby Jerónimos Monastery, and sells it as the "pastel de Belém". Our [guide to Belém in a day](/stories/belem-in-a-day/) tells you what else to see nearby.

In the Baixa, the [Confeitaria Nacional](https://www.confeitarianacional.com/) on Praça da Figueira dates from 1829 and says it introduced the Christmas cake bolo-rei to Portugal in 1875. In Porto, the [Majestic Café](https://www.cafemajestic.com/) on Rua de Santa Catarina keeps a Belle Époque main room and a winter garden. Both are places where knowing how to order makes the visit easier when the room is busy. For a fuller tour of the cake counter, see our [guide to Portuguese pastries](/stories/portuguese-pastries/).

| Portuguese | English |
|---|---|
| Um café, por favor | An espresso, please |
| Um galão | A tall milky coffee |
| Com leite / sem açúcar | With milk / without sugar |
| Um destes | One of these |
| Para levar | To take away |
| Para comer aqui | To eat here |
| Quanto custa? | How much is it? |

## At the market

Portuguese markets are where phrases earn their keep, because stallholders are busy and the produce changes with the season. In Porto, the [Mercado do Bolhão](https://www.mercadobolhao.pt/) has been part of the city for well over a century, and its website lists opening hours, with the market closed on Sundays. It also posts notices there, including occasional weather closures, so check before you go. Lisbon's markets are covered in our [guide to Lisbon markets](/stories/lisbon-markets/).

| Portuguese | English |
|---|---|
| Bom dia, queria... | Good morning, I'd like... |
| Um quilo de... / meio quilo de... | A kilo of... / half a kilo of... |
| Isto, por favor | This one, please |
| Posso provar? | Can I taste? |
| É tudo, obrigado | That's everything, thanks |
| Aceita cartão? | Do you take cards? |

"Queria" (I would like) is the most useful verb in the language for a visitor. It is polite without being stiff, and it works in shops, markets, ticket offices and restaurants alike.

## In restaurants

Menus in tourist areas often come in English. Off the beaten track, they may not. Our guide to [eating out in Portugal](/stories/eating-out-in-portugal/) explains how meals are structured; these are the words that get you through one.

| Portuguese | English |
|---|---|
| Uma mesa para dois, por favor | A table for two, please |
| A ementa, por favor | The menu, please |
| O prato do dia | The dish of the day |
| Peixe / carne | Fish / meat |
| Sou vegetariano / vegetariana | I'm vegetarian |
| Tenho alergia a... | I'm allergic to... |
| Água sem gás / com gás | Still / sparkling water |
| Um copo de vinho tinto / branco | A glass of red / white wine |
| A conta, por favor | The bill, please |

A word on the small plates that may appear when you sit down: bread, butter, olives, cheese. These starters are the "couvert", and our [eating-out guide](/stories/eating-out-in-portugal/) explains how they work. If you do not want them, a polite "não, obrigado" is all you need to say.

If you have a serious allergy, carry a written card in Portuguese naming the ingredient. A phrase spoken in a noisy dining room is easy to mishear; a card can be shown to the kitchen.

## Getting around

Signs at stations and stops are usually clear, but these words help with tickets and directions.

| Portuguese | English |
|---|---|
| Onde fica...? | Where is...? |
| A estação | The station |
| A paragem | The bus or tram stop |
| Um bilhete para... | A ticket to... |
| Ida e volta | Return |
| À esquerda / à direita / em frente | Left / right / straight on |
| É longe? | Is it far? |
| A casa de banho | The toilet |

"Saída" means exit and "entrada" means entrance, and you will see both everywhere. If you are driving, "portagem" means toll, a word worth recognising on motorway signs. For more on stations, cards and tickets, see our [guide to getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/).

## When you need help

Learn these even if you learn nothing else. GOV.UK's [Portugal travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/portugal/safety-and-security) gives 112 as the number to call in an emergency.

| Portuguese | English |
|---|---|
| Socorro! | Help! |
| Preciso de ajuda | I need help |
| Chame uma ambulância | Call an ambulance |
| Onde fica a farmácia? | Where is the pharmacy? |
| Perdi o meu passaporte | I've lost my passport |
| Fui roubado / roubada | I've been robbed |

## Before you book

- Practise the greetings and "obrigado" or "obrigada" until they come without thinking.
- Write down any allergy or dietary need in Portuguese on a card you can show.
- Save 112 as the emergency number, as listed in [GOV.UK's travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/portugal/safety-and-security).
- If a market visit is part of the plan, check its own website for opening days, as the [Mercado do Bolhão](https://www.mercadobolhao.pt/) does with its hours and notices.
- Download an offline Portuguese dictionary or translation app before you lose signal in the countryside.
