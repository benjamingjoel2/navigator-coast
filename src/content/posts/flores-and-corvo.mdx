Stand on the western shore of Flores in the late afternoon and there is nothing between you and North America but water. Behind you, a green wall of cliff rises almost straight from the fields, threaded with white lines of falling water. Below, the sea works at black lava. It is about as far west as Europe goes, and it feels it.

Flores and its tiny neighbour Corvo make up the Western Group of the Azores. They are the islands most visitors never reach, which is a large part of their appeal. They are also the islands where the weather has the final say, so they reward travellers who come with time, patience and a loose plan.

## Two islands, one group

According to [Visit Azores](https://www.visitazores.com/en/the-azores/flores), Flores is 16.6 km long and 12.2 km at its widest, and Corvo lies 17.9 km away across the channel. Together they form the archipelago's western group, and the westernmost point of the European continent is here: the Monchique islet, a black rock off the north-west tip of Flores, best seen from the Vigia da Ponta Negra near the Albarnaz lighthouse.

Corvo is smaller again. The tourism board's [Corvo page](https://www.visitazores.com/en/the-azores/corvo) describes it as the smallest island in the Azores, a single volcano with a collapsed crater at the top and one settlement, Vila do Corvo, on a lava platform by the sea.

The two islands also share a history. Visit Azores notes that Flores and Corvo were granted as a single fiefdom in 1453, and that for centuries their remoteness made them a lookout and supply point for ships crossing the Atlantic.

## Flores: the island of water

The name probably came from the wild flowers early settlers found here, and in summer the hydrangea hedges still turn the roadsides pink and blue. But what you remember is the water. Visit Azores counts seven volcanic craters on the island that have filled to become lakes, and describes the central plateau as a place where streams wander slowly before dropping off the edge in waterfalls.

### Fajã Grande and Fajãzinha

The west coast between Fajãzinha and Fajã Grande is the scene everyone comes for. Visit Azores describes nearly twenty waterfalls spilling down the long cliff here, with the Ribeira Grande fall reaching 300 metres. At the foot of the cliff are permanent pools such as the Poço da Alagoinha, also called the Lagoa dos Patos, and the [Poço do Bacalhau](https://www.visitazores.com/en/explore/poco-do-bacalhau), a small pool beneath a waterfall of about 90 metres where, per Visit Azores, you can swim.

<Pair a="flco-waterfall" b="flco-town-coast" captionA="One of the slender falls on the green walls of western Flores." captionB="Houses scattered across a green fajã above a restless sea." />

The word fajã is worth learning. It means a flat strip of land at the base of a cliff, made either by a landslide or a lava flow, and many of the Azores' most beautiful villages sit on one. On Flores, Visit Azores mentions the lava fajãs of Santa Cruz, Lajes and Fajã Grande, where on calm days you can see the sea floor through the clear water.

### Rocha dos Bordões and the crater lakes

Inland, the road climbs to the plateau and its lakes. Visit Azores names the Caldeira Rasa and Caldeira Funda, close together but at different heights, and the Caldeira Negra, 105 metres deep. On the way south you pass the Rocha dos Bordões, a hillside of vertical basalt columns that Visit Azores compares to a pipe organ. The colour of the rock shifts through the day as the light moves across its moss and lichen.

### Santa Cruz and Lajes

The two towns are small and quiet. Santa Cruz das Flores has the airport, the main harbour and the [Flores Museum](https://www.visitazores.com/en/explore/flores-museum), which Visit Azores describes as spread across three buildings: the Franciscan Convent of São Boaventura, the Pimentel de Mesquita house and the old whale factory. The tourism board's island page adds that the museum covers farming, scrimshaw and maritime life, as well as objects recovered from the Slavonia, a ship wrecked off Flores in 1909.

Lajes das Flores, on the south-east coast, has the 18th-century church of Nossa Senhora do Rosário with its tiled façade and, per Visit Azores, a bridge over the Ribeira dos Morros dating from 1743. Both towns once had whaling stations, and you can still find traces of them by the water.

## Walking on Flores

Flores is made for walking, but it is also the island where trails are most at the mercy of the weather. The [Azores Trails page for Flores](https://trails.visitazores.com/en/islands/flores) lists the official routes. A few to know:

| Trail | Type | Length | Time (official estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fajã de Lopo Vaz | Circular, medium | 3.4 km | 2 hours |
| Miradouro das Lagoas – Poço do Bacalhau | Linear, hard | 7 km | 3 hours |
| Lajedo – Fajã Grande | Linear, medium | 13.2 km | 3 hours 30 |
| Grande Rota das Flores | Linear, hard | 47 km | 18 hours |

The Fajã de Lopo Vaz walk is the only access to that stretch of coast, down a stone staircase the trails site credits to an early settler. The Miradouro das Lagoas route starts on the plateau among the crater lakes and drops west to finish near the Poço do Bacalhau.

Check before you lace up. When we looked in November 2026, the official [alerts page](https://trails.visitazores.com/en/alerts) listed the Ponta Delgada – Fajã Grande coastal path (PR01FLO) as temporarily closed, and the Grande Rota das Flores was marked with its second stage closed. Landslides on these cliffs are part of how the island works, so closures can appear and lift at short notice.

The trails site's [ethics and safety advice](https://trails.visitazores.com/en/ethics-safety) applies doubly out here: tell someone your route, carry water (stream and lake water is not safe to drink), and note the number on the last marker post you passed, which helps rescuers find you. Many trails have no phone signal except at the start and end. For more on walking across the islands, see our guide to [hiking in the Azores](/stories/azores-hiking/).

## Corvo: one village, one crater

Corvo is the kind of place where a day trip feels like a journey. The island's single town, Vila do Corvo, is a knot of narrow lanes paved with dark cobbles, built tight, according to [Visit Azores](https://www.visitazores.com/en/the-azores/corvo), to give shelter from the wind. Look for the wooden door locks, still made by hand on the island and, per the tourism board, its most distinctive craft.

<Figure id="flco-corvo-windmill" caption="A windmill on the shore at Vila do Corvo, with the evening sky over the Atlantic." />

Above the town, a road climbs to the rim of the Caldeirão, the crater left when the top of Corvo's volcano collapsed. Visit Azores gives its maximum diameter as 2.3 km and its depth as 305 metres, with a shallow lake inside dotted with small cones that, many say, trace the outline of the Azores islands. The [Caldeirão lookout](https://www.visitazores.com/en/explore/caldeirao-lookout) is the place to stand and take it in.

<Figure id="flco-corvo-caldeirao" caption="Low cloud rolling over the rim of the Caldeirão, Corvo's great crater." />

If the cloud lifts, walk down. The [Corvo page on Azores Trails](https://trails.visitazores.com/en/islands/corvo) lists the Caldeirão circuit at 4.8 km with an average time of two and a half hours, and advises doing the crater walks only in good weather and visibility. Cattle, horses and goats graze up there; leave them be.

Visit Azores also notes that the Biosphere Reserve of Corvo was established in 2007, and that boat trips around the island reveal sea cliffs, seabirds and, sometimes, dolphins and whales. If marine life is your main reason for coming to the islands, read our guide to [whale watching in the Azores](/stories/whale-watching-azores/) first.

## Getting there and getting around

Both islands have airports. Azores Airlines lists both [Flores and Corvo among its destinations](https://www.azoresairlines.pt/en/destinations), and the routes it lists include flights to Flores from Ponta Delgada and from Lisbon. Visit Azores says Corvo has had scheduled flights from Flores, Faial and Terceira since 1991.

Between the two islands, the simplest link is the boat. [Atlânticoline's timetable](https://www.atlanticoline.pt/en/timetable/) includes sailings between Santa Cruz on Flores and Vila Nova on Corvo. Frequencies change through the year and crossings depend on sea conditions, so check the dates you need. Our overview of [getting around Portugal](/stories/getting-around-portugal/) covers the bigger picture, and if you are still deciding between Atlantic islands, start with [Azores or Madeira](/stories/azores-or-madeira/).

On Flores itself, a hire car is the practical way to reach trailheads and viewpoints scattered around the island. On Corvo, the village is walkable and the climb to the crater is by road.

## Weather and timing

Flores and Corvo sit far out in the Atlantic, and the weather here can change by the hour. Ferries, flights and trails can all be disrupted. Plan buffer days at either end, keep an eye on the [IPMA warnings page](https://www.ipma.pt/en/otempo/prev-sam/), which covers the Azores, and treat any fixed itinerary as a draft. Summer brings the hydrangeas and calmer seas, but even then the plateau can vanish into cloud.

Food reflects the islands' isolation. Visit Azores mentions salted pork with potatoes and cabbage, taro with sausage, watercress soup and fritters of seaweed on Flores, and on Corvo a hand-made cheese cured for at least 60 days. For the wider picture of Portuguese cooking, see our [regional food guide](/stories/what-to-eat-in-portugal/).

## Before you book

- Build at least one spare day into any trip that includes flights or ferries to or from the western islands.
- Check the [Azores Trails alerts page](https://trails.visitazores.com/en/alerts) the night before each walk, and again in the morning.
- Confirm Flores–Corvo sailings on [Atlânticoline's timetable](https://www.atlanticoline.pt/en/timetable/) for your exact dates.
- Pack waterproofs and layers whatever the season, and plan waterfall walks for clear days.
- Look at the [IPMA warnings](https://www.ipma.pt/en/otempo/prev-sam/) before committing to a crater walk on Corvo.
